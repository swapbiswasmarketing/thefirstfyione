# Fact, history, science and education accounts on Instagram (2024-2026): winning patterns and weak spots

State as of 1 November 2026. Every bullet carries one label:
- **[primary]**: Meta/Instagram's own statements, help pages or Instagram-served data. This includes follower counts and posts read from Instagram's own logged-out profile pages, as preserved in dated Wayback Machine captures.
- **[measured]**: third-party data, or my own computation from captures, with the sample and period given.
- **[lore]**: unverified, anecdotal or self-reported. This covers marketing case studies, search-result summaries and press figures with no stated source.

## 1. Which accounts lead the niche on Instagram, and what are their dated follower counts and growth?

### Takeaway
Two institutions dwarf the niche: National Geographic, at 268.4M on 14 Oct 2026, and NASA, at 104.4M on 22 Oct 2026. NatGeo is shrinking: it had about 284M in Jan 2024, so it has lost roughly 16M. NASA gained about 5M around the Artemis II mission in April 2026 and has been flat since. The faceless fact, science and history brands sit far below, in the low millions:
- Interesting Engineering: 4.35M, flat since early 2024
- NASA Webb: 3.57M
- Science Channel: 2.66M
- History Cool Kids: 2.22M
- Kurzgesagt: 2-3M
- Smithsonian: about 1M
- ScienceAlert: 919K and falling

Two faceless brands outside that band:
- **The Good Quote**, a faceless quote brand, is the large exception at 24M (Dec 2025).
- **The Cultural Tutor** has no verifiable Instagram presence.

UberFacts' Instagram was last measurable in 2020, at 1.95M. Third-party "influencer" rankings for Instagram history and science contain almost only face-led personal creators, such as Alice Loxton (1.9M) and Bill Nye (3.5M).

### Cited Findings

#### How the data were obtained (read before using the numbers)
- **Source of counts.** instagram.com cannot be read logged-out, and Social Blade blocks this machine. Every count therefore comes from a dated Wayback Machine capture of one of two page types:
  - Instagram's own logged-out profile page. Its embedded JSON carries an exact `follower_count`, and its `og:description` meta tag carries an abbreviated count.
  - A Social Blade page, which shows exact numbers only in its late-2026 captures; earlier captures load the numbers by script.
- **Truncated counts.** Instagram's abbreviated `og:description` count is truncated to whole millions above 1M. "2M Followers" sits beside an exact 2,224,252 in the same History Cool Kids capture. "284M" therefore means 284.0-284.99M. — [Wayback, History Cool Kids, 8 Oct 2026](https://web.archive.org/web/20261008173425/https://www.instagram.com/historycoolkids/)
- **What a capture shows.** Each logged-out capture also lists the account's 12 most recent profile-grid posts, pinned posts first. For each post it gives the format (`clips` = Reel, `carousel_container` = carousel, `feed` = single image), the caption, Instagram's auto-generated alt text (which includes the post date) and the carousel slide count. It gives no like counts. Those come only from captures of individual post pages, which are rare.
- **Rate limit.** Archive.org began refusing my queries with HTTP 429 (1 Nov 2026), which stopped post-level engagement sampling for NatGeo, Interesting Engineering, TED and BBC Earth. Future runs should space out Wayback lookups.

### Inferences
- The niche on Instagram has three tiers:
  1. Two mega-institutions that post their own material (NatGeo, NASA).
  2. Broadcaster and publisher brands, mostly posting Reels clipped from their own shows (Discovery, BBC Earth, TED, Science Channel).
  3. Faceless fact, history and science brands in the low millions (Interesting Engineering, History Cool Kids, ScienceAlert).
- Unlike X, Instagram has no faceless viral-clip aggregator showing fast 2024-2026 growth in this data. The faceless brands measured are flat (Interesting Engineering since 2024) or shrinking (ScienceAlert, and NatGeo among institutions). The growth points are original-content brands (Kurzgesagt into the 2M band; NASA) and face-led creators.
- That pattern fits Instagram's push, from 2024 on, to cut aggregator reach (see Q2 and Q4). It cannot be proved from follower counts, and the sample is small.
- NASA's April 2026 jump and subsequent slide suggest that event-driven follows are partly temporary: about 1.1M of roughly 5M had gone again by September, a cross-tracker estimate. For a small account, a dated peg (anniversary or launch) can bring a burst of follows, but holding them needs a steady stream of posts afterwards.
- The Good Quote's counter shows that a faceless brand can prune its catalogue hard (-46% of posts) and keep growing followers. UberFacts and Fascinating did the same on X.

### Gaps
- No growth series exists for History Cool Kids: there is a single Wayback capture and no tracker page was reachable.
- No 2024-2026 Instagram data exists for UberFacts.
- No Instagram account could be confirmed for The Cultural Tutor.
- Exact 2024-2025 counts for accounts above 1M are truncated to whole millions, so growth for Kurzgesagt, TED, Smithsonian and Interesting Engineering is only approximate.
- NASA's own internal follower figures for Artemis II appeared only in a search-result summary of press coverage: flagship Instagram "+4.6M during the mission", Artemis account "+2M", and "104 million as of July 7". I could not open them on a fetched page, so treat them as [lore].
- No dated counts were found for other faceless Instagram fact or quote brands. Wayback lookups beyond those above were stopped by archive.org rate limiting.

## 2. Formats, video sourcing, caption length, source credits and posting frequency

### Takeaway
Formats split by type of account:
- **Video brands** are nearly all Reels: Interesting Engineering 100%, Science Channel 100%, BBC Earth 11/12, TED 9/12, NatGeo 7/12.
- **Still-image educators** use single images and carousels:
  - History Cool Kids: 12 of 12 are single text-on-image cards, maps or archival photos.
  - NASA: 7 single images, 3 carousels, 2 Reels.
  - NASA Webb: 5 carousels (one of 20 slides), 5 images, 2 Reels.

Captions run long by X standards: medians of 44 words (NASA), 66-75 (Interesting Engineering), 74 (NatGeo), 148 (History Cool Kids) and 297 (NASA Webb). Science Channel's 7-word clip titles are the exception. Hashtags are sparse (0-4).

Crediting splits by account type too:
- **Institutions credit.** NASA tags astronauts and its own mission accounts and co-publishes as Collabs; NatGeo writes "Photo by @photographer"; NASA Webb prints full image credits.
- **Faceless brands mostly do not.** History Cool Kids credited 1 of 12 posts; Interesting Engineering credited 0 of 22, although it used "Via: @creator" in 2020.

Cadence ranges from about 0.4 posts a day (NASA Webb) to 12 or more in one day (History Cool Kids, 7 Oct 2026). NASA averages about 1.4 a day and NatGeo about 2-3.

Measured benchmarks rank carousels highest on engagement rate: 0.55% of followers in 2024 and 2025, 0.50% in Q3 2026. Since 30 Apr 2026, Instagram no longer recommends photo or carousel aggregators. Adding a credit does not make a repost original, but adding context text or graphics does.

#### What the posts look like (examples from the same captures; [primary] Instagram-served text)
- **History Cool Kids** — [Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/20261008173425/https://www.instagram.com/historycoolkids/)
  - **Text on every image.** Instagram's alt text detects text on all 12 images: 4 are text-only cards ("May be an image of text"), 3 are maps or graphics, and 5 are photos or illustrations with text.
  - **Captions are mini-essays** that expand the card. One begins "Hong Xiuquan was born in 1814 in Guangdong…" and runs to 325 words. Another begins "For most of history, maps were not automatically drawn with north at the top."
  - **Personal voice.** Some posts are first-person and nostalgic ("2016 is also when Pokémon Go dropped… I remember everyone in NYC being outside"). Others invite readers in ("If you have more, I would love to read!").
- **NASA** — [Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/20261022011308/https://www.instagram.com/nasa/)
  - **Structure:** a playful first line, then 1-3 explanatory sentences, then three hashtags.
  - **First lines:** "Space jellyfish! 🪼", "Objects in mirror are farther than they appear 🪞", "This butterfly didn't take shape in a cocoon. 🦋", "Swipe for countless stars", "POV: you're watching the Earth set".
  - **Pop-culture hooks:** a Lagoon Nebula post is tagged #Spongebob.
- **Interesting Engineering, 2026 vs 2020** — [2026](https://web.archive.org/web/20261020012949/https://www.instagram.com/interestingengineering/); [2020](https://web.archive.org/web/20200102203931/https://www.instagram.com/interestingengineering/)
  - **2026 captions** are 41-106-word explainers that name the organisation behind the footage, e.g. "built by Shanghai Jiao Tong University's Hainan Research Institute". One names a study's authors and journal.
  - **Jan 2020 captions** were about 7 words, often "Via: @habu_official" or "Via: @equipter" plus #engineering.
- **National Geographic** — [Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/20261014165020/https://www.instagram.com/natgeo/)

#### Instagram's originality rules, which decide whether a faceless page gets recommended
- **[primary, as reported by TechCrunch, 30 Apr 2024]** — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/04/30/instagram-is-updating-its-ranking-systems-to-surface-more-content-from-smaller-original-creators/)
  - When two or more identical pieces of content exist, recommendation surfaces (Explore, Reels, in-feed recommendations) show only the original.
  - Reposts get a label linking to the original creator.
  - Accounts that repeatedly post content they "didn't create or enhance" are excluded from recommendations, though followers still see their posts.
  - Content substantially changed (a meme or parody) is exempt.
- **[lore] Threshold:** an Engadget headline and a search summary say Reels accounts that posted unoriginal content 10 or more times in 30 days were cut from recommendations. TechCrunch gives no number. — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/instagrams-algorithm-overhaul-will-reward-original-content-and-penalize-aggregators-130018977.html)
- **[primary] Instagram, 30 Apr 2026: the rules now cover photos and carousels.** — [Instagram creators blog](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/rewarding-original-creators-on-instagram); [Social Media Today, 30 Apr 2026](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-updates-algorithm-to-benefit-original-creators/819016/)
  - **Scope:** the 2024 Reels safeguards now also cover photos and carousels. Aggregator accounts that mainly re-upload others' work no longer qualify for recommendations.
  - **Not original:** re-uploading with only "a border, watermark, subtitles, or credit added to the caption or content".
  - **Original:** your own photos or videos; a photo series, guide or visual story; third-party material "substantially edited", e.g. "adding unique text that gives context beyond describing the content" or "creative graphics that provide new information".
  - **Recovery:** most recent photos, carousels and Reels must count as original over a rolling 30-day window. Status shows in Account Status.
  - **Recommended tools:** Collabs, Remix, Reposts and Stories.
  - **Scale:** "In the US, 75% of recommendations now come from original posts."
  - **Flag:** this change is six months old, and Instagram may adjust it further.
- **[primary] Recommendation Guidelines** (help page; the copy I read showed only "© 2026 Meta", no effective date). It was the Hindi-language rendering, machine-translated, so check the English wording. — [Facebook-hosted copy](https://www.facebook.com/help/instagram/313829416281232); [Instagram help](https://help.instagram.com/313829416281232)

### Inferences
- **What successful sourced-facts posts on Instagram look like:**
  - a native visual that carries the fact by itself (a text card, map, archival photo or short Reel);
  - a 40-150-word caption that explains and adds context;
  - almost no hashtags;
  - a named origin: a photographer, an archive, an institution or a Collab tag.
  - The giants (NASA, NatGeo, NASA Webb) do all four. History Cool Kids does the first three and is weak on the fourth.
- **On Instagram, credit alone does not protect reach.** The X-style "📹 creator" credit no longer earns reach: since 30 Apr 2026 a caption credit on a reposted photo or carousel does not make it original. The way through is the one Instagram itself names: original text-on-image cards and carousels that add context. A strictly.fyi-style carousel (a claim, the evidence, the source slide) fits Instagram's definition of original almost word for word.
- **Credits have shrunk at Interesting Engineering.** Its 2020 "Via: @creator" credits gave way by 2026 to paraphrased explainers with no creator credit. One plausible reading, unconfirmed, is a move to look more "original". Either way, viewers lose the trail back to the source.
- **Cadence:** institutions post about 1-3 times a day. History Cool Kids batches heavily (12 or more in a day). The data do not show which approach grows faster; nothing here suggests that more than 1-2 quality posts a day is needed.
- **Carousels:** the measured rate edge of carousels over Reels is small: 0.50% vs 0.48% of followers in Q3 2026. A fact account can lead with carousels, its strongest-fit format, without giving up much reach rate. If the Buffer figures check out, Reels still win on raw reach.

## 3. What do their most-engaged posts have in common?

### Takeaway
Hard engagement data are scarce, and what exists points one way. NASA's biggest Instagram posts are rare, awe-heavy "event" images:
- an Artemis II liftoff post with more than 6M likes (Apr 2026);
- its all-time most-liked post, a James Webb Carina Nebula image.

In October 2026 a Webb carousel ("Swipe for countless stars") drew 836K likes in about 3 days, 2.5x NASA's 341K average. That post combines an image of something no one could see unaided, a short playful opener, 1-2 sentences of explanation and a named source (the telescope).

History Cool Kids' sampled posts drew 158-1,345 likes within hours. Its best was a credited LIFE archival portrait with a human story.

The very largest accounts have very low engagement rates: NatGeo 0.05% and NASA 0.33%, against a 0.48% platform average. So huge follower counts do not mean a fact account must chase reach the way X aggregators do.

## 4. How accuracy problems (misattributed photos, false facts, Community Notes, fact-check labels) show up on Instagram, and with what effect

### Takeaway
The rules now differ by country:
- **US:** Meta said on 7 Jan 2025 that it would stop demoting fact-checked content, and third-party fact-checking ended on 7 Apr 2025. The replacement, Community Notes (tested from 18 Mar 2025), by design has "no penalties" and "won't limit who can see that content". Notes rarely appear: only 3.0% of Instagram posts debunked by AFP's US bureau since Apr 2025 had a note, against 20.5% on X (Sep 2026).
- **Outside the US:** third-party fact-checking still runs, with "no changes to other countries" as of 9 Sep 2026. The pages fetched do not list countries, so whether India is covered is my assumption. A "False" or "Altered" rating gets an overlay label and lower ranking. Accounts that repeatedly post such content lose recommendation eligibility. Meta began testing Community Notes in 16 Latin American countries in September 2026, without publishing notes yet.

AI labels apply on top. AI-generated media shows an "AI info" label; content that was only AI-edited carries the label in the menu since Sep 2024. Accounts that repeatedly share realistic AI people without labels are not recommended.

In this niche, the known accuracy problems are AI "vintage" fakes passed off as real:
- unnamed Instagram history pages, Mar 2024;
- a Meta-AI-watermarked "1908 Hyderabad flood" image on Instagram, Facebook and X, Jun 2025;
- AI-faked Beatles and Auschwitz photos on a Facebook history page, Jan 2025.

For a faceless page, the larger and more certain penalty is not an accuracy label but the originality rule (Q2), which removes repost-based pages from recommendations everywhere.

### Inferences
- **The cost of being wrong depends on where viewers are.**
  - **US:** a false fact costs almost nothing in distribution. Notes carry no penalty and almost never appear (3%). The cost is reputational: notes and notification of earlier engagers, when they happen, plus loss of trust.
  - **Fact-checked markets outside the US** (India presumably among them; not confirmed in fetched pages): one "False" or "Altered" rating brings an overlay and a demotion. Repeated ratings cost the whole account its recommendation eligibility, which is how a small account gets found.
  - **For strictly.fyi:** its audience spans India and the US, so it should be planned under the stricter, non-US regime.
- **The AI label is a new hazard for history accounts.** AI colourisation, upscaling or "restoration" of archival photos can trigger "AI info". Since Sep 2024 that label sits in the menu for edits but stays visible for generated images. The safe practice is to post untouched archival scans with the archive named. Any edit should be disclosed in the caption ("colourised by…"), so the account is never the one passing off an AI image as a photograph, the failure behind the Musi flood and Amaral cases.
- **An accuracy niche is open.** Misattribution and AI fakes are common on Meta platforms, and notes rarely catch them on Instagram (3% of debunked posts). A sourced account that posts "Viral photo: real or fake?" carousels has a visible accuracy hook. That slot is filled on X by notes and by @fakehistoryhunt-style debunkers. Such carousels must add original context to count as original: a source slide, a side-by-side with the archive copy, the date.
- **Originality outranks accuracy in effect.** The originality rule (Q2) is enforced everywhere and continuously, while accuracy labels are rare and regional. For a faceless fact page, re-uploading others' viral images, even with credit, is a bigger reach risk than an occasional factual error.

### Gaps
- No 2024-2026 press report or fact-check names History Cool Kids, Interesting Engineering, UberFacts or The Good Quote for a false or misattributed Instagram post. Searches surfaced only unnamed pages.
- No study measures the reach effect of a fact-check label on Instagram specifically, as opposed to Facebook.
- Instagram publishes no counts of notes published on Instagram, and the Indicator sample size is paywalled.
- Whether third-party fact-checking will end in India or elsewhere is not settled. As of 9 Sep 2026 Meta says "no changes to other countries at this time", and that could change.

## 5. Documented case studies of faceless educational accounts that grew from zero on Instagram in 2024-2026

### Takeaway
I found no reputable press case study, with dated numbers, of a faceless educational Instagram account growing from zero in 2024-2026. What exists is anecdotal or comes from other platforms:
- **Stan's @creatorstrategies** (company blog; self-reported): an AI-voiced education account using Reels. It went from 0 to more than 15K followers in 30 days with 20 posts and passed 20K by its 28th post, driven by one 615K-view Reel.
- **The 2025 AI "history POV" wave** (POV Lab, Time Traveller POV, Histairy_Films) grew in months on TikTok, with tens of millions of views per video, and sold AI-video courses. Histairy_Films has 907K Instagram followers (CreatorDB, Oct 2026).

The best-documented growth spurt in the niche is NASA's: +5.2M in April 2026, tied to Artemis II. It is event-driven, not faceless, and not from zero. The established faceless brands (Interesting Engineering, ScienceAlert) were flat or shrinking over the same period.

### Inferences
- **What the anecdotes share:**
  - growth comes from Reels and from one or two outlier videos, not from steady averages;
  - the content is a "breakdown" or explainer layered over existing material, with an AI voice or AI video;
  - views convert to follows at 1-2%.
- **Rule risk:** Stan's method of downloading others' viral clips and adding commentary is borderline under Instagram's 30 Apr 2026 originality rules. Commentary that "adds context" can count as original, but a re-upload with only subtitles or credit cannot. Copying it for strictly.fyi would need properly transformed clips, or better, public-domain or archive media.
- **A cautionary tale:** the AI-POV wave grew fast on spectacle and sold courses off it, but historians and reporters found errors. Under Meta's rules, accounts repeatedly sharing realistic AI people without labels are not recommended, and AI-generated video carries a visible "AI info" label. The wave is an example of what a sourced, accurate account should not do.
- **Benchmark:** no credible zero-to-scale faceless case exists from 2024-2026. A realistic new-account benchmark is the Stan anecdote: thousands of followers in the first month only if one Reel breaks out. Measure follows per 1,000 views; Stan's lifetime figure was 20 and the typical rate is about 1. Do not expect a 2020-style ramp like Interesting Engineering's 1.6M to 4M.

### Gaps
- No independent, dated follower series was found for any faceless educational Instagram account started in 2024-2026, including Histairy_Films, POV Lab and @creatorstrategies. Wayback has no captures of their profiles, and the trackers either hold one snapshot (CreatorDB) or block this machine (Social Blade).
- BBC and ITV coverage of the AI-POV creators could not be fetched, so their creator details and Instagram-specific numbers remain unverified.
- No case study shows how a sourced or fact-checked faceless account performs against an unsourced one on Instagram.
