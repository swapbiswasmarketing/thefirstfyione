# Fact, trivia, history and science accounts on X (2024-2026): winning patterns and weak spots

## 1. Which accounts lead the niche on X, and what are their follower counts and growth?

### Takeaway
The niche is led by NASA (about 92M), then a few personality-free aggregator accounts. These are UberFacts (11.8M and falling), Historic Vids/@historyinmemes (6.1M), Massimo/@Rainmaker1973 (4.4M) and History Photographed/@HistoryInPics (3.6M). The fastest growth since 2022 went to high-volume viral-clip aggregators: @historyinmemes grew 61x and Massimo 7.8x. The one researched, original account in the set, The Cultural Tutor, stood at about 1.76M in Dec 2024 and had slipped to 1.67M by Aug 2026. The legacy accounts (UberFacts, HistoryInPics) are shrinking.

### Inferences
- The top tier on raw follower count is not the top tier on momentum. UberFacts and HistoryInPics are large but shrinking legacy accounts. The 2022-2026 winners (@historyinmemes, Massimo, Science girl, Fascinating) are all faceless aggregators of viral clips and images.
- The biggest jumps cluster in late 2022 to early 2024: Fascinating's 207K-follower week ended 4 Nov 2022, and Science girl added 34.8K a week in early 2024. That fits the period after X's ownership change, when "For You" distribution and Musk's replies favoured these accounts, but this research did not establish the cause.
- Massimo's 2026 plateau and The Cultural Tutor's 2025-26 decline suggest saturation, or a 2025-26 algorithm or audience shift away from these formats. That cannot be confirmed from follower counts alone.
- The Science girl vs Massimo feud (her allegations only) suggests the aggregators compete for the same viral clips and treat "visibility" as zero-sum. A new account that posts original, sourced material sits outside that copying contest.
- UberFacts' accelerating decline alongside net post deletion (see Q3) looks like a semi-dormant brand coasting on a 2010s audience.

### Gaps
- No 2025-2026 counts were found for Fascinating, Wonder of Science, Science girl (after Feb 2024), Physics In History (after Feb 2024) or Interesting As F*ck. Their archived 2025-26 x.com captures are login walls, Social Blade blocks this IP, and Nitter was shut down after X's legal letters.
- The Massimo discrepancy (3.15M on xcancel in Sep 2025 vs 4.40M from X in May 2026) is unresolved. The X-served figures are probably more authoritative.
- The main "Interesting As F*ck" handle could not be confirmed. Only @interestingsasf (2022) turned up in Social Blade archives.
- No 2024-2026 press profiles were found that identify the operators of Massimo, Science girl, @historyinmemes, Fascinating, Wonder of Science or Physics In History.

## 2. Which formats do they use, and what do their most-engaged posts have in common?

### Takeaway
The highest-reach format in this niche is a native short video or a single striking photo with a one-line caption of about 10 words. The caption is a curiosity or emotion hook, often with a year tag such as "(1957)", and has no source, credit or link. @historyinmemes' top 101 posts are 84% video and 16% photo, with a median of 36M views and 342K likes. Depth formats exist (The Cultural Tutor's illustrated 20-post threads, Physics In History's photo-plus-paragraph posts and quote cards), but they draw two to three orders of magnitude less engagement per post (compared against a top-post sample). On engagement rate (not reach), Buffer's 2025 data ranks text first, then video, then images, with links last.

### Inferences
- **Reach vs rate:** the reach winners (video and single images) are not the engagement-rate winners (text). Viral fact accounts optimise for reach to non-followers through native media. A text-led sourced account can earn high rates from followers but should not expect @historyinmemes-scale reach without striking visuals.
- **What the top posts share:**
  1. One self-explanatory visual moment.
  2. A caption under about 15 words that names the surprise or emotion.
  3. A credibility cue (a year, "real and not scripted", "Never forget") instead of a citation.
  4. No outbound link.
  5. Universal subjects (celebrities, animals, kindness, sport) under a loose "history" label.
- **What to borrow:** a sourced account can copy points 1, 2 and 4 and replace point 3 with a real citation inside the caption or image, e.g. "Library of Congress, 1937".
- **Thread fatigue:** in the Dante thread, likes fell by more than half after the opener. That fits a "hook post carries the thread" pattern; it is one sample only.
- **Per-post engagement:** the volume aggregators (Massimo) get low engagement per post; the curators (@historyinmemes' top posts, Science girl's best) get very high engagement per post. Followers seem to come from occasional mega-hits, not from average posts.

### Gaps
- No systematic comparison of single images vs videos vs threads vs quote posts was found for these specific accounts. The @historyinmemes sample looks like a top-posts view, not a random sample, so it overstates typical engagement.
- No current (2025-26) post samples were found for Wonder of Science, Fascinating, Science girl or Interesting As F*ck.
- Quote-post usage (quoting trending posts) could not be measured. The parser could not reliably detect quotes in the 2026 X captures.

## 3. How often do they post, and do they link out or credit sources?

### Takeaway
Cadence splits the niche in two. The firehose accounts include Massimo (about 160-345 items a day including reposts and replies, and a new post every ~6 minutes in a sampled window) and Science girl (about 95 a day by early 2024). The curators include Wonder of Science (~10 posts a week), HistoryInPics (~13 a week) and NASA's main account (~25-31 a week). Sourcing is weak across the niche. The top aggregator posts carry no credits or links. Credits, where they exist, are a tiny "[📹 creator]" tag or a separate reply. Since March 2025, X has given non-Premium link posts effectively zero engagement, which pushes everyone further from linking.

### Inferences
- **Credit placement:** credits in a reply or a tiny bracket tag let accounts claim attribution without hurting the hook. But a credit in a separate post gets a sliver of the attention: 32 likes vs 4,364 on the main post in the Physics In History case, under 1%.
- **Sourcing with links penalised:** a sourced-facts account should put the citation inside the post (e.g. "Source: National Archives, 1944") or on the image. The link goes in the first reply, or the account buys Premium so link posts are not zeroed out.
- **No need for the firehose:** firehose cadence (Massimo, Science girl) needs a content pipeline that a fact-checked account cannot sustain. The curated tier (Wonder of Science's ~10 a week grew 445K followers in 2021-22; HistoryInPics' ~13 a week) shows a few strong posts a day can also build an audience.
- **Self-reposts:** both Massimo and Physics In History reposted their own older posts the same day, a cheap way to fill the timeline.
- **Deletion:** net post deletion (UberFacts, Fascinating) is consistent with pruning weak or wrong posts. The reason cannot be confirmed.

### Gaps
- Original posts could not be separated from replies and reposts in the counter-based rates. Posts-per-day figures therefore overstate original content.
- No dated measure of what share of posts credit a source was found for Wonder of Science, Fascinating or Interesting As F*ck.

## 4. How often are they hit by Community Notes or accused of misattribution and false facts, with what effect? Is being sourced and correct a measurable advantage?

### Takeaway
No per-account count of Community Notes exists in public sources: X's notes data does not name the author of the noted post. The base rates show notes are rare and slow. Fewer than 10% of submitted notes are ever published, the English publication rate halved to 4.9% by early 2025, and the average time to publication was 14 days in 2025. When a note does land, it is costly. Reposts fall about 46% after attachment, the effect is largest on altered media, deletion odds double, and noted posts lose revenue-share eligibility. But because most engagement happens before the note, the lifetime hit is only about 12% of reposts. Accuracy is therefore a real defence and a trust or off-platform asset, not a measurable growth engine: the fastest-growing accounts are the least sourced.

### Cited Findings

#### Effect of a note
- **Slaughter, Peytavin, Ugander and Saveski (PNAS; Yale News, 25 Sep 2025):** more than 40,000 X posts with proposed notes. — [Yale News](https://news.yale.edu/2025/09/25/flagging-misinformation-social-media-reduces-engagement-study-finds); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.13322)
  - After a note was attached: reposts -46.1%, likes -44.1%, replies -21.9%, views -13.5%.
  - Over the post's lifetime: reposts -11.6%, likes -13.3%, replies -6.9%, views -5.5%, because a large share of engagement happened before notes arrived.
  - Labels had a large effect on altered media and a more modest one on outdated information.
  - Labels were more effective on posts from accounts the reader did not follow.
  - Ugander: "time is of the essence".
- **Chuai et al. (arXiv, 13 Sep 2024):** 237,677 noted cascades with more than 431M reposts. — [arXiv 2409.08781](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08781)
  - Notes reduced the spread of misleading posts by 62.0% on average.
  - Notes raised the odds that the author deletes the post by 103.4%.
  - The authors say notes "might be too slow to intervene in the early (and most viral) stage".
- **Money:** in Oct 2023 Musk announced that posts corrected by Community Notes would no longer earn ad revenue share. NewsGuard later found ads on 15 noted posts in the week of 13 Nov 2023, so enforcement was imperfect. — [Wikipedia: Community Notes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_Notes)
- **Media notes:** notes were extended to images in May 2023 and to videos in Sep 2023. They can flag media as digitally altered or AI-generated. — [Wikipedia: Community Notes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_Notes)

#### How often notes appear (base rates; no account-level data)
- **DDIA (via Fortune, 10 Jul 2025):** all 1.76M public notes, Jan 2021 to Mar 2025. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/07/10/more-than-90-x-community-notes-are-never-published-stuck-limbo-study/)
  - More than 90% are never published.
  - English publication fell from 9.5% (2023) to 4.9% (early 2025). Spanish rose from 3.6% to 7.1%.
  - Average time to publication fell from more than 100 days (2022) to 14 days (2025), which DDIA called "far too slow for the reality of viral misinformation".
- **Arjmandi-Lari, Mantzarlis and Stafford (arXiv, 1 Oct 2025):** the share of notes rated "helpful" (and so shown) is "low, 10%, and declining". Having a note published raises the author's future note-writing. — [arXiv 2510.00650](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.00650)
- **Earlier audits:** — [Wikipedia: Community Notes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_Notes)
  - Atlantic Council (Nov 2023): notes took 7 hours on average, up to 70 hours, across 400 misinformation posts.
  - CCDH (Oct 2024): 74% of 283 sampled election-misinformation posts got no note, and published notes drew 13x fewer views than the original posts.
  - Mashable (Nov 2023): posts drew 5-10x more views than the notes attached to them.

  - Example: AAP FactCheck rated a viral Einstein classroom anecdote false; the Einstein Papers Project called the quote a fake. — [AAP FactCheck](https://www.aap.com.au/factcheck/story-about-einsteins-maths-mistake-doesnt-add-up/)
- **AI fake history (AFP, 17 Oct 2024):**

#### Accuracy vs growth in the same period
- The least-sourced aggregator, @historyinmemes (0 of 101 top posts credited), grew 61x between Jul 2022 and Jun 2026. — [2022](https://web.archive.org/web/20220730173114/https://socialblade.com/twitter/user/historyinmemes); [2026](https://web.archive.org/web/20260627221841/https://x.com/historyinmemes)
- The researched, essayistic Cultural Tutor lost 89,840 followers between Dec 2024 and Aug 2026. Its expertise did convert off-platform, into a Penguin book and a 75,000-reader newsletter. — [Nitter Dec 2024](https://web.archive.org/web/20241201062245/https://nitter.privacydev.net/culturaltutor); [x.com Aug 2026](https://web.archive.org/web/20260823004641/https://x.com/culturaltutor); [Penguin](https://www.penguin.co.uk/books/458970/the-cultural-tutor-by-quirke-sheehan/9781405975520)

### Inferences
- **Expected cost of being wrong:** for an unsourced viral post, it is small. At most about 5-10% of notes get published, with roughly two weeks' delay on average, and the lifetime engagement loss is about 12%. Even so, a note cuts revenue, invites deletion and does most damage to altered or AI media, which is the main misattribution category in history accounts.
- **Who is most exposed:** labels work best on viewers who do not follow the account, and viral fact accounts live on exactly that For You reach. They are therefore the accounts most exposed when a note does land.
- **No growth reward for accuracy:** nothing found shows the algorithm rewards accuracy. The measurable advantages of sourcing are defensive (avoiding note penalties and revenue loss), reputational, and off-platform (books, newsletters, press).
- **An open niche in fast correction:** notes are slow and rare, so there is room for fast, sourced corrections of trending fakes, the role @fakehistoryhunt plays in note citations. A new account that quote-posts a viral misattributed photo with the correct archive and date gets news-pegged reach while showing its accuracy.
- **Possible connection (unconfirmed):** the AFP article names Jo Hedwig Teeuwisse as a historian who debunks false history online. She is widely reported elsewhere to run "Fake History Hunter", but the sources fetched here do not confirm that she runs @fakehistoryhunt.

### Gaps
- No public dataset links Community Notes to post authors. Birdwatch Archive shows placeholder handles, and X's notes export lacks the post author. How often Massimo, Science girl, @historyinmemes or others get noted could not be counted.
- No press coverage from 2024-2026 was found of a specific note on these accounts. Searches on fact-check sites (Snopes, Lead Stories, Full Fact, AAP, PolitiFact, Newsweek, Daily Dot and others) found nothing naming them. AFP, Reuters, USA Today and India Today block the search crawler.
- No study compares the growth of sourced vs unsourced fact accounts, so "accuracy as an advantage" rests on indirect evidence.

## 5. How do edutainment accounts use date pegs (holidays, anniversaries, "on this day") and trending topics?

### Takeaway
Anniversaries reliably produce each topic's attention peak of the year, and round-number anniversaries roughly triple it. Across 7 recurring pegs over 2024-2025, Wikipedia pageviews peaked on the peg date in 13 of 14 topic-years (the exception: Krampus 2024, which peaked on 25 Dec). Peg-day traffic ran from 3.5x the yearly median (Titanic, 2024) to more than 400x (Pi Day). The 80th D-Day anniversary drew 102x vs 31x a year later. One-off news pegs (the Notre-Dame reopening) spike once and do not recur. The big aggregators use trending pegs opportunistically: an archival clip or celebrity moment tied to that day's event (Beijing 2008 drumming posted 50 minutes into the Paris 2024 opening ceremony; Hugh Jackman meeting a Wolverine fan on the film's opening weekend). Most of their top posts, though, are evergreen and carry only a year tag.

### Inferences
- **Calendar:** a sourced-facts account should build its schedule around recurring pegs:
  - fixed anniversaries (moon landing, D-Day, Titanic, Pearl Harbor)
  - "days" with outsized relative spikes (Pi Day, Halloween)
  - folklore holidays (Krampus on 5 Dec, a topic strictly.fyi already covers)
  - round-number anniversaries, which roughly triple attention (80th D-Day)
- **Peg plus visual:** the aggregators' best trending pegs pair a verifiable archival or celebrity visual with a live event within hours. That is high-reward and also the highest-risk moment for misattribution, because the pressure to post fast is greatest. A sourced account can compete by preparing verified, cited assets in advance for scheduled events (Olympics, film releases, launches).
- **Mixing pegged and evergreen:** news pegs decay (Notre-Dame did not recur in 2025), and anniversaries return to the original event (the fire anniversary drove the 2025 peak). Mixing a dated peg ("80 years ago today") with an evergreen hook gives repeatable annual traffic.
- **Year tags as cheap pegs:** they borrow the authority of a date without a citation. Adding "Source: [archive]" next to the year is the obvious way for a sourced account to stand out.

### Gaps
- Wikipedia pageviews measure public attention, not X engagement. No X-native study of "on this day" performance was found.
- No systematic count of how often each account posts "on this day" content was possible from the archived timelines.
- No 2024-2026 coverage was found of how The Cultural Tutor, Massimo or Science girl choose date pegs or respond to trending topics (e.g., whether The Cultural Tutor posted on the Paris 2024 *Last Supper* / *Feast of the Gods* debate).
