# X (Twitter) format and posting-practice performance data, 2024-2026

Research date: 2026-11-01. Labels used below: **[2025-26]** = data collected in 2025 or 2026; **[2024]** = 2024 data; **[OLDER]** = pre-2024 data, or a long multi-year window that ends before 2024; **[VENDOR, NO METHOD]** = a vendor claim with no stated sample or method; **[SNIPPET]** = taken from a search-result summary because the page itself could not be fetched (x.com, business.x.com and help.x.com all returned HTTP 403 to the fetcher).

Engagement-rate (ER) definitions differ by roughly 100x between sources: **per follower** (Rival IQ, Socialinsider, SociaVault) versus **per impression or reach** (Buffer, Ordinal, Adilo). Never compare an ER across sources without checking the denominator; each finding below states it.

## 1. Engagement and reach by format (text, image, multi-image, video, GIF, polls, threads, long posts, Articles, quote posts, links), and small vs large accounts

### Takeaway
For a small account in 2025-2026, the biggest measured driver of reach is the account tier, not the format. In Buffer's 18.8M-post panel, Premium accounts got about 10x the median impressions of free accounts, and since March 2025 free accounts' median engagement on link posts has been 0%. Across formats, engagement per impression ranks text ≥ image > video > link in Buffer's 2025 data, which is mostly small accounts. Among established brand accounts measured per follower (Rival IQ, 2024 data), photo or video tweets led in 12 of 14 industries. Bare link posts finish last in every dataset that measured them. There is no rigorous 2024-2026 data on polls, GIFs, multi-image posts, video length, threads, long posts or X Articles. The claims that circulate for those formats are vendor figures with no stated method.

### Cited Findings

#### Account tier (Premium) dominates reach [2025-26]
- Buffer analysed 18.8 million X posts from 71,000 accounts, August 2024 to August 2025, using medians throughout; about 27% of the accounts subscribed to Basic, Premium or Premium+. Median impressions per post were "typically under 100" for regular (free) accounts, "closer to 600" for Premium and "over 1,550" for Premium+. That puts Premium at about 10x free-account reach. — [Buffer, X Premium study, Oct 2, 2025](https://buffer.com/resources/x-premium-review/)
- In the same panel, median reach across all accounts fell from about 1,000 impressions per post (Aug 2024) to under 750 (Aug 2025). — [Buffer, X Premium study](https://buffer.com/resources/x-premium-review/)
- Median engagement rate by tier: Premium Basic ~0.55%, Premium+ 0.53%, Premium ~0.49%, regular 0%. Engagement means likes + replies + reposts; Buffer does not state the denominator. Regular accounts fell from about 0.15% to 0% by March 2025, while Premium rose from about 0.3% (late 2024) to about 0.4% (mid-2025). A median of 0% means at least half of free-account posts got no likes, replies or reposts. Buffer says follower count alone does not explain the gap but presents no controlled analysis. — [Buffer, X Premium study](https://buffer.com/resources/x-premium-review/)
- Buffer's 2026 engagement report on the same platform data says Premium and regular accounts moved together until about January 2025, then split: Premium rose, and regular accounts' median engagement rate "hit 0%" in the most recent months. — [Buffer, State of Social Media Engagement 2026, Mar 5, 2026](https://buffer.com/resources/state-of-social-media-engagement-2026/)

#### Format medians, mixed accounts (engagement per reach or impression)
- **[2025-26]** X median engagement rate by format in 2025: **text 3.56%, images 3.40%, video 2.96%, links 2.25%**. Text was 5% ahead of images, 20% ahead of video and 58% ahead of links; video beat links by 32%. Method: medians from Buffer's 2026 State of Social Media Engagement dataset, with ER = engagements ÷ reach (or the closest equivalent X provides) and X engagement = likes + reposts + replies. The X-specific post count is not stated; the cross-platform total is "45M+". — [Buffer, Best Content Format 2026, Mar 19, 2026](https://buffer.com/resources/data-best-content-format-social-media/); [Buffer, State of Social Media Engagement 2026](https://buffer.com/resources/state-of-social-media-engagement-2026/)
- **[2025-26]** The same format ranking split by tier, as of August 2025 (18.8M posts, 71,000 accounts):

  | Format | Free accounts | Premium accounts |
  |---|---|---|
  | Text | ~0.40% | ~0.90% |
  | Video | ~0.25% | ~0.85% |
  | Image | ~0.20% | ~0.42% |
  | Link | 0% | ~0.28% |

  Links are "the only format" at 0% for free accounts. Note that these rates sit on a different scale from the 2-3.6% medians above; Buffer does not reconcile the two, and the Premium article puts free-account text at ~0.25% after March 2025 rather than ~0.40%. — [Buffer, links on X, Oct 14, 2025](https://buffer.com/resources/links-on-x/); [Buffer, X Premium study](https://buffer.com/resources/x-premium-review/)
- **[2024-26]** Ordinal measured posts published January 2024 to May 2026, sample skewed toward B2B and startup accounts; post and account counts are not stated. With ER = (likes + replies + reposts + quotes) ÷ impressions, it found **image 0.79% ER (18,334 average impressions)**, **text-only 0.68% (35,975 average impressions)** and **video ~0.54% (lowest)**. Threads had the "highest" ER, but the figure is missing from the page. So image posts had the higher rate, while text posts drew almost 2x the impressions. — [Ordinal, X engagement data study](https://www.tryordinal.com/blog/twitter-x-engagement-data-study)
- **[2025-26]** Adilo analysed "over 3,200" X posts from creators, brands and advertisers in 2025 (about 29,000 data points). ER = (likes + reposts + replies + shares + bookmarks) ÷ views. Results: **text average 3.2% / median 1.13%; image 2.09% / 1.28%; ad 1.36% / 0.47%; video 1.34% / 0.94%**. Mixed-media posts were excluded. On medians, images slightly beat text; on averages, text leads. Adilo is a video-hosting vendor, so this result runs against its own commercial interest. — [Adilo, X engagement rate chart](https://adilo.com/blog/x-engagement-rate-chart-2025/)
- **[2024-25]** Distribution skew: in 1.7M posts from 56,000 users across X, Threads and Bluesky, half of X posts received **4 or fewer engagements** (median 4 in 2024 and in Feb 2025). The mean was 328 and the standard deviation over 5,000. Method: mixed-effects regression. — [Buffer, 1.7M posts on X/Threads/Bluesky, Mar 10, 2025](https://buffer.com/resources/x-threads-bluesky-data/)
- **[2024-25]** Average per-post counts for brand accounts with 1K-1M followers (10,291,540 organic X posts): likes per post fell from 40 (2024) to 15 (2025); reposts stayed at 1 and replies at 1; views rose from 1,430 to 2,979. (Socialinsider calls this a 50% rise; the arithmetic gives about 108%.) — [Socialinsider, 2026 Social Media Benchmarks](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks)
- **[2024]** Metricool's 2024 study (23,561 accounts, 2,144,853 posts, 2024) gives average interactions per post for the largest account band: likes 47.95, link clicks 32.54, profile clicks 15.11, reposts 11.46, replies 4.69, quotes 1.63. Across all accounts, likes averaged 44 per post. — [Metricool, 2024 X/Twitter Study, Oct 16, 2024](https://metricool.com/twitter-study/)

#### Brand accounts, engagement per follower [2024 data, published Feb 2025]
- Rival IQ's 2025 benchmark covers 2,100 companies (150 drawn at random from each of 14 industries). Inclusion criteria: Facebook fans between 25,000 and 1,000,000 and at least 5,000 X followers as of January 2025. The X sample is 1.7M tweets and 338M engagements from 2024, aggregated as company medians, with ER = interactions ÷ followers. The top tweet type by ER was **photo** in 7 industries (Financial Services, Food & Beverage, Higher Ed, Home Decor, Retail, Sports Teams, Tech & Software) and **video** in 4 (Alcohol, Fashion, Media, Nonprofits). Travel had video and photo "about twice" link and status tweets. Status updates led for Influencers, and links and status updates "edged out" photos and video for Health & Beauty. — [Rival IQ, 2025 Social Media Industry Benchmark Report (PDF)](https://get.rivaliq.com/hubfs/eBooks/2025-Social-Media-Industry-Benchmark-Report.pdf)
- Media brands "favored link tweets", but **video tweets pulled in about 2.5x more engagement** than link tweets. For Higher Ed, photos earned the highest ER while being posted "3x more than any other tweet type". Nonprofits got their top ER from video, though photo and link tweets were more common. — [Rival IQ 2025 report (PDF)](https://get.rivaliq.com/hubfs/eBooks/2025-Social-Media-Industry-Benchmark-Report.pdf)

#### Links: data vs X's statements
- **[2025-26]** The Buffer link study above (18.8M posts) found free accounts' link-post median engagement "stuck at zero" since March 2025. Before that, links were already "the lowest-performing format" (no figure given). Buffer suggests putting links in replies, calling it "not foolproof". — [Buffer, links on X](https://buffer.com/resources/links-on-x/)
- **[2026]** A NiemanLab analysis of **200 tweets from 18 publishers** (period and method not described in the write-up) found that posts with link clicks see less reach. — [Social Media Today, Apr 13, 2026](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/niemanlab-offers-advice-for-posting-news-on-x/817383/)
- **[2025-26]** X's position: in Oct 2025, X head of product Nikita Bier denied that links are "deboosted". He said lower reach comes from lower engagement, "because the web browser covers the post and people forget to Like or Reply". On Oct 19, 2025, X began an iOS test that collapses the post to the bottom of the screen while the link is open, so readers can still react. Bier said the change does not alter the algorithm. — [NiemanLab, Oct 20, 2025](https://www.niemanlab.org/2025/10/x-makes-overtures-to-journalists-with-new-feature-designed-to-improve-reach-for-links/)
- **[2026]** In April 2026, Bier said link clicks are "higher than they've ever been in the app". He blamed outdated posting styles, citing the NYT's one-line link captions, and recommended more descriptive posts, added context, or "longer threads that summarize key points". — [Social Media Today, Apr 13, 2026](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/niemanlab-offers-advice-for-posting-news-on-x/817383/). [SNIPPET] Also reported: Bier called the link-penalty claim "Fake news" and recommended links in the post body with context (Apr 2026), and told the Vatican account in late May 2026 that embedding a link does not reduce reach. — [roboin, Apr 13, 2026](https://roboin.io/article/en/2026/04/13/x-denies-link-deboost-recommends-in-post-links/); [BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/nikita-bier-pope-x-links-deboost/)
- **[2026]** In the open-sourced 2026 ranking code, link opens and clicks carry *positive* default weights (OpenLinkWeight 0.2, ClickWeight 0.3). The README describes no link penalty, and no hashtag, media-type or Premium boost or penalty either. — [xai-org/x-algorithm param.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/main/home-mixer/params/param.rs); [xai-org/x-algorithm README](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm)

#### Video
- **[2025-26]** Video ranks third of four formats on Buffer's 2025 medians (2.96%). Video carries the largest Premium-vs-free gap: about 0.85% vs about 0.25% as of Aug 2025, or >0.7% for Premium per the Premium article. — [Buffer, Best Content Format 2026](https://buffer.com/resources/data-best-content-format-social-media/); [Buffer, links on X](https://buffer.com/resources/links-on-x/)
- **[2026]** In April 2026, Bier said reposts of content from third-party platforms, "such as videos with a TikTok watermark", see a **90% reduction in impressions**. — [Social Media Today, Apr 13, 2026](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/niemanlab-offers-advice-for-posting-news-on-x/817383/)
- **[2026]** In the 2026 code defaults, a video quality view has weight 0.0 (VqvWeight), video open 0.02, PostVideoWeight 0.0, and a separate video-carousel open weight 1.0. Video views therefore add little *direct* score in the main blend; video posts earn their score through replies, reposts, shares and dwell like any other post. — [xai-org/x-algorithm param.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/main/home-mixer/params/param.rs)
- **[2025-26]** Sprout Social says X users are "most likely to interact with" short-form video under 60 seconds (37%), with text-based posts close behind (36%). The page does not describe the basis; it appears to be a survey preference figure, not a performance measurement. — [Sprout Social, best times to post on X 2026](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-times-to-post-on-Twitter/)

#### Threads, polls, long posts, X Articles, quote posts, GIFs, multi-image
- **Threads [VENDOR, NO METHOD]:** SociaVault (Apr 15, 2026; sample not disclosed) says threads get 2.1x the engagement of single tweets. Ordinal says threads had the highest ER in its January 2024 to May 2026 sample, but the value is missing from the page. — [SociaVault](https://sociavault.com/blog/twitter-x-engagement-declining-2026-data); [Ordinal](https://www.tryordinal.com/blog/twitter-x-engagement-data-study)
- **Polls [VENDOR, NO METHOD]:** SociaVault says polls get "2-3x" the engagement of standard text tweets. No sample is disclosed. — [SociaVault](https://sociavault.com/blog/twitter-x-engagement-declining-2026-data)
- **Long posts (Premium, up to 25,000 characters) [2024]:** Metricool's 2024 sample included 31,066 threads. Among verified accounts, the only ones that can write long posts, the averages were 44.87 long posts vs 15.28 threads. Metricool gives usage only, no engagement comparison. — [Metricool, 2024 X/Twitter Study](https://metricool.com/twitter-study/)
- **X Articles [2026]:** X offered $1M to the author of the most popular Article in the payout period ending Jan 30, 2026. The winner was judged "primarily on Verified Home Timeline impressions"; entries had to be original, at least 1,000 words, from US users with X Premium. X had recently extended Articles from Premium+ to all Premium subscribers. The coverage includes no reach or engagement data for Articles. — [Social Media Today, Jan 17, 2026](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/x-formerly-twitter-offers-million-dollar-prize-best-article-long-form-post/809930/)
- **Quote posts [OLDER, peer-reviewed]:** Ng, Indran and Liu studied 204,905 tweets from 53 age-advocacy organisations over a 12-year period. Using Cox models of time to peak engagement (peak = above the 75th percentile of likes + retweets + quotes + replies), they found that quote tweets "increased engagement by 3 times" compared with regular tweets, controlling for account-level covariates (P<.001). Only 4.5% of the tweets were quote tweets. — [Ng et al., JMIR Aging 2024;7:e49608](https://aging.jmir.org/2024/1/e49608)
- **Quote posts [2026 code]:** QuoteWeight is 5.0, the same as ReplyWeight and about 7.6x FavoriteWeight (0.66). — [xai-org/x-algorithm param.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/main/home-mixer/params/param.rs)
- **GIFs [OLDER / low confidence, SNIPPET]:** An Indonesian study of 3,021 Twitter posts found photo posts drew more replies, retweets and likes than video posts, and that video and GIF posts performed about the same. In Ng et al., 99.7% of tweets had no GIF. — [garuda.kemdikbud.go.id (search summary)](https://garuda.kemdikbud.go.id/author/view/6566949); [Ng et al. 2024](https://aging.jmir.org/2024/1/e49608)
- **Photos [OLDER, peer-reviewed]:** In Ng et al., photos "increased peak engagement by 3 times" (P<.001). — [Ng et al. 2024](https://aging.jmir.org/2024/1/e49608). [SNIPPET] A 2024 study of world-renowned libraries on X (Wani & Ahmad, *Digital Library Perspectives*) found images produced higher engagement than video clips. — [Emerald](https://emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/DLP-06-2023-0043/full/html)

#### Account size
- **[VENDOR, NO METHOD]** SociaVault's 2026 median ER per follower, with interquartile ranges: nano (1K-10K followers) 2.18% (1.32-3.47%); micro (10K-50K) 1.42% (0.87-2.24%); mid (50K-100K) 0.97% (0.58-1.54%); macro (100K-500K) 0.63% (0.37-1.02%); mega (500K+) 0.35% (0.18-0.61%). That is a 6.2x drop from nano to mega. ER = (likes + replies + reposts + quotes) ÷ followers; sample size not disclosed. — [SociaVault, Apr 15, 2026](https://sociavault.com/blog/twitter-x-engagement-declining-2026-data)
- **[2024]** Metricool's ER by account-size band: tiny 2.21%, small 1.3%, medium 1.83%, big 1.81%, huge 1.83%. Band thresholds and the ER formula are not stated (23,561 accounts, 2,144,853 posts). — [Metricool, 2024 X/Twitter Study](https://metricool.com/twitter-study/)
- **[2026 code]** The ranker has a cold-start mechanism. The README says "posts from authors whose impressions are below a threshold are lifted toward a target position". The parameters are ColdStartImpressionThreshold 200, ColdStartFollowerCap 50000, ColdStartMaxPostAgeSecs 7200 (2 hours), ColdStartSlotMin 15 / ColdStartSlotMax 16, and a Beta prior of alpha 0.75 / beta 49.25. — [xai-org/x-algorithm README](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm); [param.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/main/home-mixer/params/param.rs)
- No source found compares format rankings for small vs large accounts directly. The closest evidence is the Premium-vs-free split, and the contrast between Buffer's mostly-small-account panel (text leads) and Rival IQ's brand panel (photo or video leads).

#### How the 2026 ranking code scores posts [2026]
- **[SNIPPET]** X open-sourced its rebuilt For You ranker around January 19-20, 2026 in the xai-org GitHub organisation, reportedly under an Apache 2.0 licence. Coverage describes it as a Grok-based transformer that dropped hand-engineered features. — [TechCrunch, Jan 20, 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/20/x-open-sources-its-algorithm-while-facing-a-transparency-fine-and-grok-controversies); [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/data/x-open-sources-its-algorithm-5-ways-businesses-can-benefit). The repository README (fetched) describes Phoenix retrieval, SimClusters and Thunder candidate sources, and lists updates through Oct 26, 2026. — [xai-org/x-algorithm README](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm)
- The score is "Final Score = Σ (weight_i × P(action_i))", so weights multiply *predicted probabilities*, not raw counts. Default weights in `home-mixer/params/param.rs` (read 2026-11-01; README updates run through Oct 26, 2026; the file says these are defaults and the live config may differ):
  - Positive actions: favorite (like) 0.66; reply 5.0, plus a 15.0 boost when author and replier follow each other; repost 1.0; quote 5.0; share 2.0; share via DM 5.0; share via copy link 20.0; follow author 4.0; open link 0.2; click 0.3; dwell 0.05; continuous click dwell time 0.4; photo expand 0.05; video open 0.02.
  - Zero weight: profile click 0.0; video quality view 0.0.
  - Negative actions: not interested -52.272; mute author -58.8; block author -31.2; report -234.0.

  — [param.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/main/home-mixer/params/param.rs)
- A comment in the code warns against reading "one report cancels 468 likes". It says the weights scale predicted probabilities, and that a report's baseline probability is ">1000x lower than a Like". (468 = 234/0.5, which suggests the like weight was 0.5 when the comment was written; the current default is 0.66.) — [param.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/main/home-mixer/params/param.rs)
- Feed-assembly rules in the README:
  - Author diversity: "each post after an author's first is multiplied by a decaying factor, down to a floor".
  - Out-of-network discount: posts from accounts the viewer does not follow are multiplied by a factor below 1, as are replies and reposts from followed accounts.
  - AgeFilter: removes posts "older than 48 hours".

  — [xai-org/x-algorithm README](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm)
- Bookmarks do not appear among the predicted actions the README lists. — [xai-org/x-algorithm README](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm)

#### Older data and what has changed since [OLDER]
- The April 5, 2023 heavy-ranker weights were: like 0.5; retweet 1.0; reply 13.5; good profile click 12.0; video playback 50% 0.005; reply engaged by author 75.0; good click 11.0; good click v2 10.0; negative feedback -74.0; report -369.0. In 2023 a reply was worth 27x a like (150x if the author replied back). The 2026 defaults cut reply to ~7.6x a like and profile clicks to zero, and add share via copy link (~30x a like) and follow author (~6x). Most 2023-2024 "algorithm hacks" are therefore out of date. — [twitter/the-algorithm-ml README](https://github.com/twitter/the-algorithm-ml/blob/main/projects/home/recap/README.md)

### Inferences
- For a new, small publisher account, the evidence says to spend effort first on (a) whether to pay for Premium and (b) native, self-contained posts. Format tweaks come after. Premium's ~10x reach effect is far larger than any measured format gap (text vs image ~5%, text vs video ~20%). The Premium effect is observational and not controlled for account quality, so this is a strong association, not proof.
- The default format ranking depends on the metric and the account type. Engagement per impression on small mixed accounts favours text, with images close. Engagement per follower on established brands favours photos, then video. Images beat text in two of the four datasets (Ordinal, and Adilo on medians), so "text or text + one strong image" is the safe default. Video is not a reliable lift on X unless it is native and original: the watermark penalty, the zero weight on video views and video's third-place medians all point that way.
- The link gap is consistent across independent datasets (Buffer, Rival IQ media, NiemanLab), even though X denies a penalty and the 2026 code gives link opens a positive weight. The likely mechanism is indirect: off-platform clicks reduce predicted replies and dwell. A post that delivers the fact itself (the answer, the number) with the link as an extra fits both the data and X's own advice.
- Because the 2026 score rewards replies, quotes and shares (especially copy-link and DM shares) many times more than likes, formats that trigger conversation or forwarding should outperform formats that collect passive likes. That would explain why quote posts and threads look strong in older and vendor data, but it is a mechanism argument, not a measurement.
- The cold-start parameters suggest each fresh post from an account under 50,000 followers, while under 2 hours old and below 200 impressions, gets a chance at a slot around feed position 15-16. If that reading of the parameter names is right, regular fresh posting gives a small account repeated discovery chances, and the 48-hour age filter caps each post's For You life at 2 days.

### Gaps
- No rigorous 2024-2026 data was found for **multiple images vs a single image**, **GIFs**, **polls**, **video length**, **threads vs single posts**, **long posts vs standard posts**, or **X Articles reach**. Every multiplier in circulation for these is a vendor claim without a stated sample (SociaVault, Ordinal), or a usage count (Metricool). Search summaries also mentioned an Article contest "total prize pool of $2.15 million" and an "18x" surge in Articles; neither source showed underlying data, and neither claim was verified.
- **Bookmarks** benchmarks by format were not found, and bookmarks are not a listed ranking action in the 2026 code.
- No study directly compares format rankings by follower band. The 2025-2026 small-vs-large evidence is about overall ER and the Premium tier.
- Tweet Hunter, Typefully, Hypefury, Postwise, Emplifi, Later and Hootsuite format data were not reached within the 20-search budget. Hootsuite's best-time page returned an image instead of text.
- The live (production) weights may differ from the repo defaults. The code says so explicitly.

## 2. Post length and structure (character count, line breaks, emojis, numbers and lists)

### Takeaway
No rigorous 2024-2026 study of X post length was found. The only recent figure is Ordinal's unquantified finding that 100-200-character posts had the highest engagement rate. X's own guidance is "keep your message concise", while X's head of product tells publishers to write more descriptive, self-contained posts. No measured data exists for line breaks, emojis, numerals or lists on X in this period.

### Cited Findings
- **[2024-26]** Ordinal (Jan 2024 to May 2026, B2B/startup-skewed sample, per-impression ER): posts of **100 to 200 characters** had the highest engagement rate. No rates or sample counts are given. — [Ordinal, X engagement data study](https://www.tryordinal.com/blog/twitter-x-engagement-data-study)
- **[SNIPPET]** X Business organic best practices: "Organic Post copy: keep your message concise and avoid using hashtags in your Post copy". The page also says to use analytics to "learn what tone, content — even publishing times — work best". The page returned 403 to the fetcher; wording is from the search-result snippet. — [business.x.com organic best practices](https://business.x.com/en/basics/organic-best-practices)
- **[2026]** For news posts, Bier recommends "more descriptive posts", adding context within the post, or "longer threads that summarize key points". He pinned the NYT's weak reach on one-line link captions unchanged "in 20 years". In NiemanLab's 200-tweet, 18-publisher sample, NYT tweets beginning "Breaking News:" saw 4x the publication's average engagement. — [Social Media Today, Apr 13, 2026](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/niemanlab-offers-advice-for-posting-news-on-x/817383/)
- **[2025]** Bier advised that a post with a link "should stand alone as great content". — [NiemanLab, Oct 20, 2025](https://www.niemanlab.org/2025/10/x-makes-overtures-to-journalists-with-new-feature-designed-to-improve-reach-for-links/)
- **[2026 code]** The ranker rewards attention as well as clicks: dwell 0.05, continuous dwell time 0.006, continuous click dwell time 0.4, click 0.3, with a small negative weight for "not dwelled" (-0.02). — [param.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/main/home-mixer/params/param.rs)
- **[2024]** Long posts (up to 25,000 characters, verified/Premium only) were used more than threads among verified accounts in Metricool's 2024 sample (44.87 vs 15.28 average). — [Metricool, 2024 X/Twitter Study](https://metricool.com/twitter-study/)
- **[OLDER, SNIPPET]** SocialFlow's 2017 one-week sample, taken just after the limit doubled, found tweets over 140 characters were retweeted 26.52 times on average vs 13.71 for shorter ones. — [Inverse](https://inverse.com/article/39205-twitter-280-characters-study). Twitter's 2018 review found the most common tweet length was 33 characters, and only 1% of English tweets hit 280. — [MarTech](https://martech.org/despite-280-character-expansion-short-tweets-are-still-the-norm/)
### Inferences
- The evidence that exists points to a complete thought in roughly one to two sentences (about 100-200 characters) for standard posts, with a long post or thread when the content has real depth. Bier's advice and the dwell and click-dwell weights both reward posts that stand alone, not teaser captions.
- For a fact-checking or explainer publisher, the "answer in the post" format (the verified fact plus a key number) suits both the length evidence and the link evidence in section 1.

### Gaps
- No 2024-2026 study with a stated sample measures engagement by character count, line breaks, emoji use, numerals or list formatting on X. Search summaries surfaced vendor claims ("under 100 characters gets 17% higher engagement", attributed to Sociality.io; "80-200 characters is optimal"), but no methodology or originating page could be confirmed. Buffer published "The Most Popular Emojis Used in Social Posts in 2025" (Dec 23, 2025, [listing](https://buffer.com/resources/data/)), but its title suggests usage frequency, not engagement; it was not fetched.
- No data compares long posts (Premium) with equivalent threads on engagement or impressions.

## 3. Hashtags: do they raise or lower reach and engagement on X now, and how many?

### Takeaway
No controlled 2024-2026 study measures hashtag vs no-hashtag performance on X. X's guidance has moved against hashtags. X Business now says to avoid hashtags in organic post copy, the Help Center caps the recommendation at two, Musk told users in December 2024 to stop using them, and hashtags were banned from ads in June 2025. The 2026 ranking code has no hashtag parameter. The only positive evidence is older, from peer-reviewed data that predates the current ranker, plus brand data showing event hashtags among the top performers.

### Cited Findings
- **[SNIPPET]** X Business: "avoid using hashtags in your Post copy" (organic). — [business.x.com organic best practices](https://business.x.com/en/basics/organic-best-practices)
- **[SNIPPET]** X Help Center: recommends "no more than 2 hashtags per post as best practice, but allows as many as you like". — [help.x.com, How to use hashtags](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/how-to-use-hashtags)
- **[2024, SNIPPET]** In December 2024, replying to an X employee's post about whether to use hashtags, Musk asked users to "please stop" using them. He said the system no longer needs them and that they look "ugly" (fragments quoted as reported in headlines). — [Fox 5 DC](https://www.fox5dc.com/news/hashtags-x-elon-musk-says-please-stop-using-them); [La Voce di New York, Dec 18, 2024](https://lavocedinewyork.com/en/business/2024/12/18/elon-musk-wants-to-remove-hashtags-from-x-theyre-ugly-and-useless)
- **[2025, SNIPPET]** In June 2025, Musk announced that "the esthetic nightmare that is hashtags" would be banned from ads on X. Organic posts can still use them. — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/brand-makers/elon-musk-bans-hashtags-from-ads-on-x-calls-them-esthetic-nightmare-71969.htm)
- **[2026 code]** The README describes no score boost or penalty for hashtags. Ranking uses a Grok-based transformer with no hand-engineered features. — [xai-org/x-algorithm README](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm)
- **[2024 data]** Rival IQ lists top hashtags by ER for each industry, not a with-vs-without comparison. "Almost every industry earned top engagement rates from holiday-hashtagged posts". Media's top X hashtags were timely news and event tags (#paris2024, #internationalwomensday, #donaldtrump, #olympics). Sample as in section 1 (2,100 companies, 1.7M tweets, per-follower ER). — [Rival IQ 2025 report (PDF)](https://get.rivaliq.com/hubfs/eBooks/2025-Social-Media-Industry-Benchmark-Report.pdf)
- **[OLDER, peer-reviewed]** Ng et al.: using 3 or more hashtags was associated with reaching peak engagement (P<.001). Most tweets had 0 or 1 hashtag. Sample: 204,905 tweets, 53 advocacy organisations, 12 years; outcome = time to peak engagement. — [Ng et al., JMIR Aging 2024](https://aging.jmir.org/2024/1/e49608)
- **[OLDER, SNIPPET]** Buffer's earlier "1 million tweets" analysis (pre-2023) found engagement rose with hashtag count up to 4 per tweet. — [Buffer, 1 million tweets](https://buffer.com/resources/twitter-data-1-million-tweets/)

### Inferences
- Under the 2026 ranker, a hashtag is unlikely to *add* reach by itself, because no feature consumes it and the transformer reads the post's content. X's own business guidance now says to omit hashtags. Using zero hashtags by default, or at most one where a live event tag has an active search audience, fits the guidance and costs nothing measurable.
- The positive older findings (3+ hashtags, up to 4 hashtags) come from the hashtag-search era before 2023 and should not be carried forward.

### Gaps
- No 2024-2026 within-account or controlled comparison of hashtag count vs reach or engagement on X was found. The often-repeated "tweets with 1-2 hashtags get 21% more engagement" figure is attributed in vendor posts to an old Buddy Media study (circa 2012) that could not be located.
- The weight, if any, of hashtags in X search and Explore surfaces is not documented in the code read here.

## 4. Questions and calls to action in the post text: measured effect on replies and reach

### Takeaway
No 2024-2026 study measures the effect of questions or calls to action (CTAs) in X post text. The strongest relevant evidence is mechanical. The 2026 ranker weights a predicted reply at ~7.6x a like (plus a 15.0 boost between mutual follows), and quotes, DM shares and copy-link shares carry similar or higher weights. Replying to commenters showed a small, only marginally significant +8% engagement lift on X in Buffer's within-account data. X says it will act against habitual engagement-bait patterns such as "BREAKING" on every post.

### Cited Findings
- **[2026 code]** Default weights: reply 5.0 vs like 0.66; BidirectionalFollowReplyWeightBoost 15.0; quote 5.0; share via DM 5.0; share via copy link 20.0; follow author 4.0. Negatives: not interested -52.272, mute -58.8, block -31.2, report -234.0. — [param.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/main/home-mixer/params/param.rs)
- **[OLDER, 2023 code]** Reply 13.5 and "reply engaged by author" 75.0 vs like 0.5. — [twitter/the-algorithm-ml README](https://github.com/twitter/the-algorithm-ml/blob/main/projects/home/recap/README.md)
- **[2025]** Buffer ran a fixed-effects regression on about 30,000 X posts from 16,000 accounts, about 2,100 of which had comments the author replied to. Replying gave about **+8% engagement**, "only marginally significant" (p ≈ 0.097); about 51% of accounts showed a positive effect. Engagement is not defined for X. Causality is not established: high performers may simply attract more replies. — [Buffer, replying to comments, Nov 12, 2025](https://buffer.com/resources/replying-to-comments-boosts-engagement/); [Buffer, State of Social Media Engagement 2026](https://buffer.com/resources/state-of-social-media-engagement-2026/)
- **[2026]** Bier said X will target accounts that habitually use "BREAKING" on every post. — [Social Media Today, Apr 13, 2026](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/niemanlab-offers-advice-for-posting-news-on-x/817383/)
- **[VENDOR, NO METHOD]** Polls, a built-in CTA format, are claimed to get 2-3x the engagement of text tweets. — [SociaVault](https://sociavault.com/blog/twitter-x-engagement-declining-2026-data)

### Inferences
- A genuine question that readers want to answer should raise the predicted reply probability, which is the cheapest heavy-weight signal a small account can earn. The large negative weights on "not interested", mute and report mean that clickbait or bait-style CTAs can cost more than they gain. Make the question part of the content, not a formula.
- Replying to the first commenters is low-cost and directionally positive (+8%). The mutual-follow reply boost (15.0) suggests conversations within an engaged follower network count most.

### Gaps
- No measured effect of question marks, explicit CTAs ("reply with…", "repost if…") or "comment below" phrasing on X replies or reach in 2024-2026. The search turned up only feature lists with no reported effects.

## 5. Best times and days to post for a US audience, and posting frequency vs engagement (especially for small accounts)

### Takeaway
The two large 2025-2026 timing datasets agree on **Tuesday to Thursday** and on avoiding **Saturday** and **evenings**. They disagree on the hour. Buffer (8.7M posts, engagement rate) puts the peak at weekday mornings around **9-11 a.m.**, peaking at **Tuesday 9 a.m.**; Sprout (nearly 2B engagements, Nov 2025 to Feb 2026) puts it at **12-6 p.m.** All times are the audience's local time. No X-specific study tests posting frequency within accounts. Cross-sectional data shows a median brand posts ~2 tweets a week, and very high-volume media accounts have the lowest per-tweet engagement; those figures say nothing about total engagement.

### Cited Findings

#### Timing
- **[2025-26]** Buffer's top slots are **Tuesday 9 a.m.**, then Wednesday 10 a.m. and Wednesday 9 a.m. The top three hours each day fall between 8 and 11 a.m. Best days are Wednesday, Tuesday, Thursday; worst is Saturday, then Friday. Engagement "drops significantly" from 6 to 11 p.m. Method: tweets sent through Buffer, metric = engagement rate, normalised to local time. The sample is "8.7 million tweets" in the body but "1 Million Posts" in the header; the period and account count are not stated. — [Buffer, best time to post on X 2026, Mar 13, 2026](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-twitter-x/)
- **[2025-26]** Buffer's 2026 engagement report gives a best window of 6-11 a.m. on weekdays, peaking Tuesday 9 a.m. (data through Dec 3, 2025). Buffer's growth playbook cheat sheet says 8-11 a.m. on weekdays. — [Buffer, State of Social Media Engagement 2026](https://buffer.com/resources/state-of-social-media-engagement-2026/); [Buffer, creator growth playbook, Jun 10, 2026](https://buffer.com/resources/creator-growth-playbook/)
- **[2025-26]** Sprout Social analysed "nearly 2 billion" engagements from Nov 27, 2025 to Feb 27, 2026, across about 307,000 profiles on six networks and 30,000+ customers, in local time. Results by day:
  - Tuesday to Thursday: 12-6 p.m. (peak).
  - Monday: 2-3 p.m. and 5 p.m.
  - Friday: 12-6 p.m. (moderate).
  - Sunday: 2-6 p.m. (moderate).
  - Saturday: lowest; the summary also lists Sunday among the worst days.

  The page is internally inconsistent: some passages say Monday to Thursday and some Tuesday to Thursday. — [Sprout Social, best times to post on X 2026, Mar 31, 2026](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-times-to-post-on-Twitter/)
- **[2025-26]** Sprout by sector:
  - **Education & Schools:** best on weekends (Saturday 1-11 p.m., Sunday 12-6 p.m.); on weekdays, evenings (Monday 6 p.m., Tuesday 7-9 p.m., Wednesday 6 and 9 p.m.). Worst during school hours (9 a.m. to 5 p.m.).
  - **Nonprofits:** Tuesday to Thursday, 9 a.m. to 6 p.m.; weekends worst.
  - **Media:** no media-specific guidance.

  — [Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-times-to-post-on-Twitter/)
- **[2024]** Metricool (23,561 accounts, 2024, time zones grouped): best day Wednesday, then Tuesday and Thursday. The highest average count of *connected users* was at 9 p.m. That measures audience presence, not engagement. — [Metricool, 2024 X/Twitter Study](https://metricool.com/twitter-study/)
- **[OLDER, SNIPPET, unverified]** A 2023 summary of Hootsuite's data gave 9 a.m. PST on Fridays as the best time on Twitter. Hootsuite's current page could not be read. — [Hypebot, 2023](https://www.hypebot.com/hootsuite-shares-the-best-times-to-post-on-social-media-in-2023)

#### Frequency
- **[2024 data]** Rival IQ (2,100 brands, ≥5,000 X followers, 2024): the median brand posted **2.16 tweets per week**, and X posting frequency fell 33%, the sharpest drop of any platform. Media brands posted **49.90 tweets/week**, "23x the median", but had the lowest per-tweet ER (0.006%) of any industry that tweets at least weekly. Sports Teams posted 44.25/week with the *highest* ER (0.073%). Rival IQ's conclusion: for Media, "more tweets don't mean more engagement". — [Rival IQ 2025 report (PDF)](https://get.rivaliq.com/hubfs/eBooks/2025-Social-Media-Industry-Benchmark-Report.pdf); [Rival IQ blog summary](https://www.rivaliq.com/blog/social-media-industry-benchmark-report/)
- **[2024-25]** Socialinsider (brands with 1K-1M followers, 10.29M posts): average posting rose from 50 posts/month in 2024 to 70 in 2025 (about 16/week), while per-follower ER fell from 0.15% to 0.12%. — [Socialinsider, 2026 Social Media Benchmarks](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks)
- **[2024]** Metricool: the average account posted **12 posts per week**, and the largest accounts almost 95/week. Higher frequency tracks larger size; no causal effect on growth is claimed. — [Metricool, 2024 X/Twitter Study](https://metricool.com/twitter-study/)
- **[2024-25 data]** In the Rival IQ/Quid higher-ed report (667 D1/D2 institutions, June 1, 2024 to May 31, 2025), X total posts fell 27%, total engagements fell only 4%, and per-follower ER rose from 0.09% to 0.12%. — [Quid/Rival IQ, 2025 Higher Education report](https://www.quid.com/knowledge-hub/resource-library/blog/2025-higher-education-social-media-engagement-report)
- **[OLDER, peer-reviewed]** In Ng et al., tweets from organisations with higher tweet volume were **40% less likely** to reach peak engagement (P<.001). — [Ng et al., JMIR Aging 2024](https://aging.jmir.org/2024/1/e49608)
- **[No data]** Buffer's playbook recommends "three to five times per day, with three to four posts being a sustainable target" as a "rule of thumb", not a study. Its best-time article says "post daily (or multiple times if you have the bandwidth)". — [Buffer, creator growth playbook](https://buffer.com/resources/creator-growth-playbook/); [Buffer, best time to post on X](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-twitter-x/)
- **[2026 code]** Within one feed request, each additional post from the same author is multiplied by a decaying factor down to a floor. Posts older than 48 hours are filtered out, and the cold-start lift applies only to posts under 2 hours old. — [xai-org/x-algorithm README](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm); [param.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/main/home-mixer/params/param.rs)
- **[Context, SNIPPET]** Buffer's within-account fixed-effects analysis on LinkedIn (2M+ posts, 94,000+ accounts) found small accounts got the same relative lift from posting more often as large accounts. This is LinkedIn, not X; Buffer has published no equivalent X frequency study. — [Buffer, how often to post on LinkedIn](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-linkedin/); [Buffer data index (no X frequency study listed)](https://buffer.com/resources/data/)

### Inferences
- For a national US audience: Buffer's morning peak in local time means 9-11 a.m. ET lands at 6-8 a.m. PT, while Sprout's midday window fits 12-3 p.m. ET. A schedule of one post at about 9-10 a.m. ET and one at about 12-1 p.m. ET on Tuesday to Thursday covers both datasets' peaks across time zones. Avoid Saturday and 6-11 p.m. If the audience is students or teachers, Sprout's education data points the other way, to evenings and weekends. The two timing datasets use different metrics (Buffer: rate; Sprout: engagement volume), which probably explains the hour disagreement.
- The cross-sectional "more posts, lower per-post ER" pattern (Media, Ng et al., Socialinsider) is consistent with per-post dilution and does not show that extra posts lower *total* engagement. With author-diversity decay damping bursts and cold-start lift rewarding fresh posts, spacing a few quality posts through the day (for example 1-3 a day for a small publisher) looks more efficient than batch-posting. No X study tests this directly.

### Gaps
- No X-specific within-account study of posting frequency vs engagement or follower growth exists in the 2024-2026 sources found, for small accounts or any other band.
- Hootsuite's current X best-times data could not be read. A search summary paraphrased a Hootsuite blog as saying "mornings and evenings" are best, with Monday and Tuesday mornings highest, but the page was not identified, so that is unverified. Media and publisher-specific best times were not found (Sprout gives none for media).
- No timing study is US-only; all are global samples normalised to local time.

## 6. Engagement-rate benchmarks for media, publisher and education accounts on X

### Takeaway
Per-follower benchmarks for organisations are tiny and falling. Rival IQ's 2024 data puts the all-industry median at **0.015% per tweet**, **Media at 0.006%** (the lowest of the seven industries that tweet at least weekly, despite ~50 tweets a week), **Higher Ed at 0.036%** and **Nonprofits at 0.023%**. Higher-ed accounts measured over 2024-25 rose to 0.12%. Per-impression benchmarks for mixed and creator accounts are far higher, at about 2-3.6% medians (Buffer) or about 0.5-1% (Ordinal, B2B). A publisher should choose one definition and benchmark only against sources that use the same one.

### Cited Findings
- **[2024 data, published Feb 25, 2025]** Rival IQ X benchmarks. Per-tweet ER is engagement ÷ followers, as company medians, for brands with ≥5,000 X followers and 25K-1M Facebook fans; 150 brands per industry.

  | Segment | Tweets/week | Median ER per tweet |
  |---|---|---|
  | All-industry median | 2.16 | 0.015% |
  | Media | 49.90 | 0.006% |
  | Higher Ed | 4.90 | 0.036% |
  | Nonprofits | 5.68 | 0.023% |
  | Sports Teams (top) | 44.25 | 0.073% |
  | Tech & Software | 3.89 | 0.019% |
  | Financial Services | 4.27 | 0.025% |
  | Influencers | 1.54 | 0.021% |

  X ER fell 48% year over year, the largest drop of any platform. Note: a widely repeated secondary figure of "0.009% for Media" does not match the primary PDF (0.006%); 0.009% is Travel's figure. — [Rival IQ 2025 report (PDF)](https://get.rivaliq.com/hubfs/eBooks/2025-Social-Media-Industry-Benchmark-Report.pdf); [Rival IQ blog summary](https://www.rivaliq.com/blog/social-media-industry-benchmark-report/)
- **[2024-25 data]** Higher education, 667 D1/D2 institutions, June 1, 2024 to May 31, 2025: X per-follower ER rose from **0.09% (2023-24) to 0.12% (2024-25)**, while posts fell 27% and engagements 4%. "Athletics-specific handles" outperform flagship institutional accounts. — [Quid/Rival IQ, 2025 Higher Education Social Media Engagement Report](https://www.quid.com/knowledge-hub/resource-library/blog/2025-higher-education-social-media-engagement-report)
- **[2024-25]** Socialinsider all-industry average for brands with 1K-1M followers (10,291,540 organic posts): **0.15% (2024) → 0.12% (2025)**, with 0.10% for 2026 to date. ER = (likes + reposts) ÷ followers × 100; replies are excluded. The methodology says accounts over 100K were excluded, but the sample table includes a 100K+ band. — [Socialinsider, 2026 Social Media Benchmarks](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks)
- **[2025-26]** Buffer's per-reach medians for its users' X posts: **1.96% (2024) → 2.83% (2025)**, +44%. The same page's tier list says "~2.5%". Buffer's analyst says the rise "likely reflect[s] changes in the user base or metric definitions rather than genuine performance improvements". — [Buffer, State of Social Media Engagement 2026](https://buffer.com/resources/state-of-social-media-engagement-2026/)
- **[2024]** An earlier Buffer study of 10.2M X and Threads posts across all of 2024 put the X median at **3.6%** (interactions ÷ reach or impressions). That conflicts with the 1.96% 2024 figure above; the samples and definitions differ. — [Buffer, Threads vs X, Feb 3, 2025](https://buffer.com/resources/threads-vs-twitter/)
- **[2024-26]** Ordinal's B2B self-assessment bands (per-impression ER, January 2024 to May 2026): above 1% strong, 0.5-1% normal, below 0.5% a sign of format or timing issues. Including clicks inflates ER by more than 50%, and the choice of formula alone produced a 4.7x spread on the same data. — [Ordinal](https://www.tryordinal.com/blog/twitter-x-engagement-data-study)
- **[VENDOR, NO METHOD]** SociaVault's all-account per-follower medians: 1.34% (2024), 1.22% (2025), 1.11% (2026); nano accounts (1K-10K) 2.18%. — [SociaVault, Apr 15, 2026](https://sociavault.com/blog/twitter-x-engagement-declining-2026-data)
- **[2026]** Publisher-specific evidence: in NiemanLab's sample of 200 tweets from 18 publishers, link-click posts saw less reach, and NYT "Breaking News:" posts got 4x the outlet's average engagement. — [Social Media Today, Apr 13, 2026](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/niemanlab-offers-advice-for-posting-news-on-x/817383/)
### Inferences
- For a small fact-checking or explainer publisher measuring engagement ÷ impressions in X analytics, realistic reference points are Ordinal's 0.5-1% "normal" band and Buffer's free-account text median of ~0.25-0.40%. Buffer's 2-3.6% medians come from a broader creator sample on a different denominator. Measured per follower, anything above Rival IQ's Media 0.006% and Higher Ed 0.036% medians beats established brands in those sectors.
- Media accounts' combination of the highest volume and the lowest per-follower ER, together with link tweets being their most common type, suggests that link-led, high-volume news posting is the pattern the data most consistently penalises. Video or photo posts and self-contained descriptive posts are the measured alternatives (Media's video tweets drew ~2.5x link tweets).

### Gaps
- No 2025-data (2026 edition) Rival IQ industry figures were verified. A search-result summary mentioned a "2026 Rival IQ median of 0.03%, doubled from 0.015%", but the page making the claim was not identified, so it is unverified. Socialinsider's free pages give no X industry breakdown, and no Emplifi, NewsWhip or Sprout publisher-specific X engagement-rate benchmark was found within the search budget.
- No benchmark specifically for small (<5K follower) publisher or education accounts. Rival IQ requires ≥5,000 followers, and Socialinsider's lowest band (<5K) has sample counts but no published ER.
