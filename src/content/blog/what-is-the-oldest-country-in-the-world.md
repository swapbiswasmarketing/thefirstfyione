Guinness World Records lists San Marino as the [oldest existing republic](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/oldest-existing-republic), "founded on 3 September 301." UNESCO, which added the town and Mount Titano to its World Heritage List in **2008**, does not give that date at all: [its listing](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1245/) says the historic centre "dates back to the foundation of the republic as a city-state in the 13th century."

The two dates sit about nine centuries apart. The 301 comes from the legend of a stonemason named Marinus, while the 13th century is when San Marino's government starts to appear in the records. Several of the best-known answers to this question have a gap like that, between the date people repeat and the date the records support.

## What is the oldest country in the world?

The usual answers are Egypt, [united under one king](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3780825/) around **3100 BC**; Iran, where numerical and then proto-Elamite tablets [appear at Susa from about **3200 BC**](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/elam-i/); Japan, whose monarchy is [officially dated](https://www.kunaicho.go.jp/learn/about/kosei/keizu.html) to **660 BC**; and San Marino, the oldest existing republic, [founded in **AD 301**](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/oldest-existing-republic) by tradition. Only Japan's monarchy and San Marino's republic still continue, and both of their founding dates come from legend.

Each answer wins under its own rule, and each has a catch:

| If oldest means | Usual answer | Date given | The catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Earliest unified state | Egypt | c. 3100 BC | Conquered by Persia in 525 BC, under Roman rule from 30 BC |
| Earliest civilization on its land | Iran | c. 3200 BC | Counts from the first tablets at Susa, in Elam, which the Persians conquered around 539 BC |
| Oldest continuous monarchy | Japan | 660 BC | Comes from a chronicle finished in AD 720; the first verifiable emperors reigned in the 5th or 6th century AD |
| Oldest surviving republic | San Marino | AD 301 | Comes from a saint's legend; UNESCO dates the republic to the 13th century |

## San Marino's 301 comes from a saint's legend

The US State Department's [background note on San Marino](https://2009-2017.state.gov/outofdate/bgn/sanmarino/55324.htm) gives the founding story "according to tradition." Marinus the Dalmatian, a Christian stonemason, fled the anti-Christian emperor Diocletian, hid on the peak of Mount Titano and founded a small Christian community there.

Even the legend's date varies. The 1913 [*Catholic Encyclopedia*](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Catholic_Encyclopedia_%281913%29/San_Marino) tells it "according to the legend" but has Marinus, "a stonecutter," reaching the mountain "about A.D. 350."

The documented republic is younger. The office of its two Captains Regent, still elected every six months as joint heads of state, dates from **1243**, when, in the [State Department's account](https://2009-2017.state.gov/outofdate/bgn/sanmarino/55324.htm), "the first two Captains Regent were nominated by the Council." The heritage body ICOMOS, in the [evaluation](https://whc.unesco.org/archive/advisory_body_evaluation/1245.pdf) it wrote for that listing, dates "a first document testifying its independence" to **1296**.

San Marino's own government has been pushed aside twice. Cesare Borgia held it in **1503**, "but only for a few months," in the [*Catholic Encyclopedia*'s](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Catholic_Encyclopedia_%281913%29/San_Marino) words. On **17 October 1739**, Cardinal Giulio Alberoni, the papal legate at Ravenna, [invaded the republic and imposed a new constitution](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Catholic_Encyclopedia_%281913%29/San_Marino), and on **5 February 1740** the pope recognized San Marino's rights and [restored its independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_San_Marino). Neither occupation lasted a year.

In September 1944, German troops retreating through San Marino [violated its neutrality](https://archive.org/details/allied-mil-admin-italy), and the British Eighth Army followed them in. The official British history of the [Allied military administration of Italy](https://archive.org/details/allied-mil-admin-italy) calls this "the occupation of this neutral republic by Allied forces" but records that "out of respect for its neutrality Military Government was not proclaimed." UNESCO still credits San Marino with "a unique, uninterrupted continuity as the capital of an independent republic since the 13th century," in the words of [its listing](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1245/).

## Japan's 660 BC was probably fitted to a calendar cycle

The [genealogy of emperors](https://www.kunaicho.go.jp/learn/about/kosei/keizu.html) published by Japan's Imperial Household Agency begins with Jimmu, dated from **660 BC**, and counts the current emperor as the **126th**. That date comes from the *Nihon Shoki*, a chronicle finished in [**AD 720**](https://archive.org/details/historyofjapan01murd), nearly 1,400 years after the accession it records, and [no evidence that he existed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Jimmu) has been found.

The chronicles give their first rulers very long lives. In the *Kojiki*, Jimmu lives to 137: his "august years" were ["one hundred and thirty-seven."](https://www.sacred-texts.com/shi/kj/kj061.htm) James Murdoch's 1910 [*A History of Japan*](https://archive.org/details/historyofjapan01murd), which dates the *Kojiki* to 712, says a reader stops being "bewildered by the mirage of centenarian reigns" only with the fifth century AD.

The usual explanation for that date is the one [Naka Michiyo](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%82%A3%E7%8F%82%E9%80%9A%E4%B8%96), a Meiji-era historian, published in 1897. Murdoch, who thought it "the most plausible account" of the date, [summed it up](https://archive.org/details/historyofjapan01murd): Chinese chronology grouped years into cycles of 60, and twenty-one cycles, or **1,260 years**, made a great cycle.

> The fifty-eighth year of each cycle, [Murdoch wrote](https://archive.org/details/historyofjapan01murd), "was supposed by the Chinese to be the year in which some revolution was liable to take place."

[The year 601](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%BE%9B%E9%85%89), the ninth of Empress Suiko's reign, was such a year, and 660 BC falls exactly one great cycle before it.

Where the verifiable line begins is [debated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Kinmei), but the candidates fall in the fifth and sixth centuries AD: Yuryaku, 21st on the [official list](https://www.kunaicho.go.jp/learn/about/kosei/keizu.html), who died in 479, and Kinmei, the 29th, who reigned from 539 to 571.

A 2016 *New York Times* explainer by Russell Goldman, [reprinted by *The Seattle Times*](https://www.seattletimes.com/nation-world/5-things-to-know-about-japans-emperor-and-imperial-family/), called the imperial house "the oldest continuous monarchy in the world," though "evidence of the first 25 emperors is shrouded in myth." The [same explainer](https://www.seattletimes.com/nation-world/5-things-to-know-about-japans-emperor-and-imperial-family/) found "ample historical proof of an unbroken hereditary line from A.D. 500 to today."

Japan still marks Jimmu's legendary accession every **February 11**, as [National Foundation Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Jimmu), a date set by [cabinet order](https://www8.cao.go.jp/chosei/shukujitsu/gaiyou.html). Japan's [declaration of war of December 8, 1941](https://worldjpn.net/documents/texts/docs/19411208.O1E.html), was issued in the name of an emperor "seated on the Throne of a line unbroken for ages eternal."

## Egypt's and Iran's dates are real, but those ancient kingdoms fell

Egypt's date has archaeology behind it. A 2013 radiocarbon study led by Michael Dee of the University of Oxford [dated the foundation of the Egyptian state](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3780825/), taken as the accession of King Aha, to **3111-3045 BC** at 68 percent probability, and noted that Aha's predecessor, Narmer, "most probably held political control over the whole state."

Manetho, an Egyptian historian of the third century BC, called the king who by tradition joined Upper and Lower Egypt Menes, and modern scholars have, in [*Britannica*'s](https://web.archive.org/web/20260419145310/https://www.britannica.com/biography/Menes) phrase, "inconclusively identified" him with one or more of three early kings: Narmer, Aha and a king called Scorpion. On a carved slate palette, Narmer appears alternately wearing the red and white crowns of Lower and Upper Egypt, "a combination symbolic of unification," as the [same article](https://web.archive.org/web/20260419145310/https://www.britannica.com/biography/Menes) puts it.

Continuity is where Egypt's claim fails. The Persian king Cambyses II [defeated Psamtik III](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/egypt-i), the last pharaoh of the Twenty-Sixth Dynasty, in **525 BC**; Egypt was independent again from around 402 BC under three native dynasties, fell to Persia a second time in 342 BC, and was handed to Alexander the Great in 332 BC without a fight. [Rome's rule](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/roman-egypt) began in 30 BC, when Octavian arrived after defeating Antony and Cleopatra.

Iran's usual date, about **3200 BC**, comes from Susa, in Elam in the south-west of the country, where the [*Encyclopaedia Iranica*](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/elam-i/) dates numerical and then proto-Elamite tablets from about 3200 BC and calls the proto-Elamite script one "which has defied all efforts to decipher it." Elam itself, in the [same account](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/elam-i/), began "to emerge from anonymity" only after that script fell out of use around 2700 BC, and its independent history ended with the Achaemenid Persian conquest, "perhaps by Cyrus in 539."

## Several listed dates come from founding legends

Ranked lists of the oldest countries apply a different test to each country, so the answer changes with the test. Several of their dates come from founding stories rather than records: Harshita Singh's list of [the eleven oldest countries](https://keghart.org/harshita-singh-oldest-countries/) gives Vietnam **2879 BC**, Armenia **2492 BC** and China **2070 BC**.

Vietnam's date is the start of the legendary [Hong Bang period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%E1%BB%93ng_B%C3%A0ng_dynasty), as the *Dai Viet su ky toan thu*, a 15th-century chronicle, reckons it. Armenia's goes back to 11 August 2492 BC, one of the dates scholars of the 18th and 19th centuries calculated for the mythical battle in which [Hayk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayk), the legendary founder of the Armenian nation, killed Bel.

China's is the start date for the [Xia dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xia_dynasty) proposed by a chronology project the Chinese government commissioned in 1996. There are [no contemporary records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xia_dynasty) of the Xia, and some scholars, especially in the West, consider it legendary or unproven, while most Chinese archaeologists consider it historical.

Every founding date in this argument that is exact to the day comes from legend, San Marino's [3 September 301](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/oldest-existing-republic) and Jimmu's accession day among them. The founding dates with evidence behind them come as ranges: the radiocarbon date for the accession of Egypt's King Aha spans [3111 to 3045 BC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3780825/), and even that is only at 68 percent probability.
