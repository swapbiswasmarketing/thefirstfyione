Rain that falls on the northern Yucatan Peninsula has almost nowhere to run. The land is flat limestone, and a [2011 review of its aquifer](https://www.amigosdesiankaan.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/HJ_Yucatan_Review.pdf) in *Hydrogeology Journal* calls surface runoff there "practically non-existent", so the rain sinks and the region lives on **groundwater** alone.

The holes where that water reaches daylight are cenotes. One [250-kilometre-long arc of them](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/sediment-swirls-off-the-yucatan-149114/) traces the rim of a crater [buried under a kilometre of younger rock](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/a-smoking-gun-for-dinosaur-extinction/). The impact that made it ended the age of the dinosaurs.

## What is a cenote?

A cenote is a **natural sinkhole in limestone**, formed when the ground over an underground cavity [collapses and exposes the groundwater beneath](https://www.wordnik.com/words/cenote). The word comes from Yucatec Maya **ts'onot** by way of Spanish, and it first named the sinkholes scattered across Mexico's Yucatan Peninsula, which [supplied the ancient Maya with fresh water](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/sediment-swirls-off-the-yucatan-149114/).

## Slightly acidic rain does the digging

Rain is slightly acidic, so it dissolves the limestone as it seeps down and hollows out caves; [NASA's Earth Observatory](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/sediment-swirls-off-the-yucatan-149114/) credits the same process with the world's longest underground river. Writing about Florida's limestone, the [US Geological Survey](https://pubs.usgs.gov/circ/circ1182/pdf/15WCFlorida.pdf) puts the pace of dissolution on the order of millimetres per thousand years.

A cavity grows until the ground over it [collapses into a sinkhole](https://floridadep.gov/fgs/sinkholes). On most of the Yucatan's coastal plains the water table sits only [**10 to 20 metres** below ground](https://www.amigosdesiankaan.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/HJ_Yucatan_Review.pdf), so a collapse there can open straight onto water. Cenotes [range from caves filled with groundwater to open pools](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1014923217206).

## The fresh water floats on seawater

Seawater seeps far inland through the porous rock, and the rain that soaks in floats on top of it as a [thin lens, tens of metres thick, over denser saline water](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1014923217206). The density gap is small: fresh water weighs [about 1.000 grams per cubic centimetre and seawater about 1.025](https://pubs.usgs.gov/circ/2003/circ1262/).

Under Merida, capital of Yucatan state, the **freshwater lens** is [only 60 metres thick, and the upper 20 metres were unfit to drink](https://www.amigosdesiankaan.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/HJ_Yucatan_Review.pdf) when the review was written, polluted by septic tanks according to Mexico's National Water Commission.

The lens stays thin because the water table on the coastal plains sits barely above sea level. By the Ghyben-Herzberg relation, named for two European scientists who worked it out independently, there is [**40 feet** of fresh water below sea level for every 1 foot above it](https://pubs.usgs.gov/circ/2003/circ1262/), so a water table 1 metre up means fresh water to about 40 metres down.

The boundary between the layers is the **halocline**. Mix fresh water and seawater that are each saturated with calcite, and the blend is undersaturated, so the mixing zone keeps dissolving rock. In Quintana Roo's flooded caves, the review reports, cave depth tracks the halocline, so the passages run shallow near the coast and deeper inland.

## A ring of sinkholes traces the buried crater

In 1990 Kevin Pope, Adriana Ocampo and Charles Duller were working on a project on surface water and Maya archaeology when they saw "this perfect semi-circular structure" in Landsat images, as Pope later told [NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/a-smoking-gun-for-dinosaur-extinction/). It was the arc across the peninsula's northern tip now known as the **Ring of Cenotes**.

They turned to gravity and magnetic data that Pemex, Mexico's state oil company, had collected while searching for oil. The data showed a large circular structure that [Glen Penfield](https://web.archive.org/web/20251130195517/https://www.aapg.org/news-and-media/details/explorer/articleid/55293/unlikely-impact), a geophysicist on a 1978 Pemex aeromagnetic survey, had already read as an impact crater, though by his account Pemex geoscientists had it down as the "Merida Volcanics".

Pope's team reasoned that the cenotes marked fractures in the structure's buried rim, and [JPL](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/a-smoking-gun-for-dinosaur-extinction/) counts them "among the first to propose Chicxulub as the impact site" behind the mass extinctions at the end of the Cretaceous. Their [1991 letter in *Nature*](https://www.nature.com/articles/351105a0), titled "Mexican site for K/T impact crater?", lists Pope at a private firm, Geo Eco Arc Research, Ocampo at JPL and Duller at NASA's Ames Research Center.

In 1995 [Hildebrand and four colleagues](https://www.nature.com/articles/376415a0) used gravity data to put the crater's diameter at **about 180 kilometres**, a size the cenotes confirmed. The match, they wrote, "suggests that cenote formation is closely related to the presence of slump faults near the crater rim."

## The asteroid story is half right

The popular shorthand says the asteroid that killed the dinosaurs created the cenotes. The impact decided where the ring's holes would line up, but it did not dig them.

When it struck, [**66 million years ago**](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/sediment-swirls-off-the-yucatan-149114/), the Yucatan lay under a shallow sea. The crater became a basin that filled with sediment. [NASA's Photojournal](https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/shaded-relief-with-height-as-color-yucatan-peninsula-mexico) says scientists believe the impact altered the rock beneath, so that the limestone laid down later, which erodes very easily, wore away more near the crater rim than elsewhere.

On that account, the erosion made the ring of sinkholes and a trough [only 3 to 5 metres deep and about 5 kilometres wide](https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/shaded-relief-with-height-as-color-yucatan-peninsula-mexico), so subtle that on foot you "probably would not notice it".

> The crater left by the impact that ended the age of the dinosaurs lies under a kilometre of younger rock, and the trough that marks its rim is so shallow you would probably walk across it without noticing.

Cenotes also turn up far from the ring. The review notes that they cluster on the coastal plains and are practically absent from the central hills, perhaps because intruding seawater speeds the dissolving. It cites a 1996 thesis on the peninsula's northwest that mapped [more than 7,000 cenotes and other water holes](https://www.amigosdesiankaan.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/HJ_Yucatan_Review.pdf) from government topographic maps.

## The Sacred Cenote's dead were mostly children

The popular story says the Maya threw young maidens into the Sacred Cenote at Chichen Itza, whose name [means the city at the edge of the well of the Itza](https://www.inah.gob.mx/zonas/146-zona-arqueologica-de-chichen-itza). A [1911 article in the Penn Museum's journal](https://www.penn.museum/sites/journal/125/) calls the notes of Diego de Landa, Bishop of Yucatan, the first description of the site, supposed to have been written in 1566. In its translation, the notes record "the custom to throw into it live men as a sacrifice to the Gods in time of drought".

Yet a [2024 study in *Nature*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11208145/) says Spanish colonial accounts and the investigations that followed the dredging of the cenote "popularized the notion that young women and girls were primarily sacrificed at the site". That dredging [began in 1904](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/600/60053572003/html/) under Edward H. Thompson, the US consul at Progreso.

The bones do not support the maiden story. The study says that ["more recent osteological analyses indicate that the bodies of both males and females were deposited in the Sacred Cenote"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11208145/). Citing earlier work, it puts the cenote's dead at [more than 200 ritually sacrificed individuals, mostly children](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11208145/).

The study's own subject was a chultun, an underground cistern near the Sacred Cenote that held the bones of [at least 106 infants and young children](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11208145/), and DNA from 64 of them showed that, unlike the cenote's dead, every child analysed was a boy. Among them were two pairs of identical twins.
