Squid carry their own pen and ink. A dissection guide written for teachers by Stanford's Hopkins Marine Station and NOAA's fisheries lab in Santa Cruz suggests lifting out the ink sac without popping it, because it ["will give you plenty of ink so you can write something with the pen"](https://gillylab.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj20896/files/media/file/squids4kidsdissectionguide_0.pdf). The pen, or gladius, is ["the internal shell of the squid"](https://gillylab.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj20896/files/media/file/squids4kidsdissectionguide_0.pdf), a stiff strip you can feel by pressing along the length of the body.

Cut into rings and fried, squid is sold as calamari. The name is Italian, and in Italian it is a regional form of calamaio, the word for an inkwell.

## What is calamari made of?

Calamari is **squid cooked as food**, often the body, called the mantle, sliced into [rings and fried](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/insight/squid-calamari-recipes). In Italian, calamari is the plural of calamaro, squid. In the United States, a 2020 update to the FDA's Seafood List gives ["Squid or Calamari"](https://www.fda.gov/food/seafood-guidance-documents-regulatory-information/fda-seafood-list-updates-2020) as the market name for the California market squid and for the beka squid, Loliolus beka.

The tentacles get eaten as well as the body. NOAA's squid chowder recipe says to cut [the mantle into strips and the tentacles into 1-inch pieces](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/insight/squid-calamari-recipes), and its fried calamari is dusted with flour and cooked in oil until golden brown.

## Market squid, longfin squid and the Humboldt fishery

The California market squid, Doryteuthis opalescens, [reaches up to 1 foot in total length](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/california-market-squid), arms included, and NOAA Fisheries describes its taste as "Mild, and subtly sweet." It is usually fished at night over the spawning grounds, where squid seiners typically work with light boats because [the lights attract groups of spawning squid to surface waters](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/california-market-squid). In 2024, [commercial landings of market squid totaled 127 million pounds](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/california-market-squid), valued at **$68 million**.

On the Atlantic coast, longfin squid were taken for bait from the late 1800s and have been [harvested for their mild, sweet meat](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/longfin-squid) since the mid-1960s. Their [2024 landings totaled 29 million pounds](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/longfin-squid), worth **$32.5 million**. In the eastern Pacific, Humboldt squid support ["the largest invertebrate fishery in the world"](https://gillylab.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj20896/files/media/file/squids4kidsdissectionguide_0.pdf), the Hopkins guide says.

## Squid, octopus or cuttlefish: the limb count finds the octopus

Squid are mollusks, members of the group known as cephalopods, a name the Monterey Bay Aquarium says is derived from Greek and means ["head foot"](https://www.montereybayaquarium.org/animals/animals-a-to-z/cephalopods). Octopuses and cuttlefish belong to it too. Counting limbs picks out an octopus, but it cannot tell a squid from a cuttlefish.

The aquarium separates the limbs this way: arms are lined with suction cups along their whole length, and tentacles [sometimes terminate in rounded clubs with suction cups and sometimes with hooks](https://www.montereybayaquarium.org/animals/animals-a-to-z/cephalopods). Here are the [aquarium's counts](https://www.montereybayaquarium.org/animals/animals-a-to-z/cephalopods), with what it says each body has for internal support:

| Animal | Arms | Tentacles | Internal support, per the aquarium |
|---|---|---|---|
| Squid | 8 | 2 | A pen, more flexible than a cuttlebone |
| Cuttlefish | 8 | 2 | A hard cuttlebone |
| Octopus | 8 | None | No shell-like structure |

## The name began with a reed pen

[Calamaro](https://www.treccani.it/vocabolario/calamaro/), the Italian word for squid, is **a regional variant of calamaio, the word for an inkwell**, in Treccani's Italian dictionary. The [calamaio](https://www.treccani.it/vocabolario/calamaio/) was the small container that held ink for dipping a pen, and the dictionary derives the word from the Latin calamarius, from calamus, a pen. In the Lewis and Short Latin dictionary, calamarius means ["pertaining to a writing-reed"](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.04.0059:entry=calamarius), as in a theca calamaria, a pen case.

Greek made the same jump from writing kit to seafood. The Liddell and Scott lexicon derives [kalamarion](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.04.0057:entry=kalama/rion) from kalamos, reed, and takes its second sense from a scholium, a margin note, on Oppian's poem about fishing, where the word equals teuthis, which the lexicon translates as ["calamary or squid"](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.04.0057:entry=teuqi/s).

> In the Liddell and Scott Greek lexicon, kalamarion has two meanings: a ["reed-case, pen-case"](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.04.0057:entry=kalama/rion), and, in one scholium on Oppian's fishing poem, the squid.

None of these dictionaries says outright why the squid took the name. Treccani's description of the animal does mention both pieces of the writing kit: an internal shell ["simile a una penna"](https://www.treccani.it/vocabolario/calamaro/), like a pen or quill, and a ["ghiandola del nero ben sviluppata"](https://www.treccani.it/vocabolario/calamaro/), a well-developed ink gland.

## The pig-rectum rumor, tested on the radio in 2013

One rumor about calamari holds that some of it is pig. On January 11, 2013, This American Life aired a segment chasing a tip about ["a meat plant selling pig intestines as fake calamari"](https://www.thisamericanlife.org/484/doppelgangers).

The producer Ben Calhoun traced it to a farmer in the pork industry who said he had seen boxes labeled ["artificial calamari"](https://www.thisamericanlife.org/484/transcript) at a pork processing plant in Oklahoma. The contents, he was told, were bung, the hog's rectum, to be sliced into rings and deep fried.

The USDA's food inspection service told Calhoun that ["Products we inspect, including those derived from pork, must be accurately labeled and cannot purport to be a product of another species."](https://www.thisamericanlife.org/484/transcript) The US Meat Export Federation said it was ["not aware of them being used as a substitute for calamari, but it's not impossible"](https://www.thisamericanlife.org/484/transcript).

Then the show fried both and served them blind at a Brooklyn restaurant. Of the two This American Life staffers who tasted blind, the one who called himself a regular calamari eater picked the untreated bung as the squid.

Calhoun still closed with ["no proof that anyone, anywhere, has ever tried to pass off pork bung as calamari in a restaurant"](https://www.thisamericanlife.org/484/transcript). The test showed that bung can pass for squid, not that anyone sells it that way, so the rumor stays unproven.

The calamari at the fish counter is a mollusk with a pen for internal support, sold under a regional form of the Italian word for the pot a pen was dipped in. Strictly FYI.
