Behind E.D.I.T.H., the AI in the glasses the late Tony Stark left Peter Parker in Spider-Man: Far From Home, there turns out to be a pilot and a fatal accident. For seven years the name looked like nothing more than a joke: Business Insider's 2019 review spelled it out as ["Even Dead I'm The Hero"](https://www.businessinsider.com/spider-man-far-from-home-is-fun-and-surprising-review-2019-6) and added "too soon, Tony."

VisionQuest, the Disney+ series in which [Paul Bettany reprises his role as Vision](https://tvline.com/2171425/visionquest-release-date-paul-bettany-marvel-series-disney-plus/), gives those letters an owner, a woman named Edith Marks, and her life was no joke.

## Who is Edith Marks?

Edith Marks is the pilot Tony Stark's AI E.D.I.T.H. was modeled on, and naming her spoils **VisionQuest episode 4**. [Inverse describes her](https://www.inverse.com/entertainment/edith-marks-visionquest-emily-hampshire-interview) as "a test pilot for Stark Industries who passed away with her husband in a freak plane crash." In the Disney+ series, E.D.I.T.H. is played by **Emily Hampshire**, a [Schitt's Creek star](https://www.gamesradar.com/entertainment/marvel-tv-shows/visionquests-e-d-i-t-h-star-brought-elements-of-tony-stark-into-her-role-tonys-legacy-is-in-there/).

Inverse says E.D.I.T.H. learned in episode 3 that she and her fellow AIs are based on real people, and in episode 4 realized who her person was; GamesRadar+ puts both moments in episode 4. Edith Marks belongs to the Marvel Cinematic Universe's fiction: nothing in the coverage presents her as a real pilot.

TheWrap reported on premiere day that the **eight-episode** series would open with two episodes on [October 13, 2026, a day earlier than planned](https://www.thewrap.com/media-platforms/tv/what-time-is-visionquest-on-disney-plus/), then drop two each Tuesday except its final two episodes, which each get a solo release, ending on **November 10**. Its showrunner is [Terry Matalas](https://tvline.com/2171425/visionquest-release-date-paul-bettany-marvel-series-disney-plus/), of Star Trek: Picard.

## Most Stark AIs copy a person from Tony's life

In The Direct's recap of **episode 3, "Reboot,"** FRIDAY tells her roommates that [nearly all of Tony Stark's AI assistants are based on real people from his life](https://thedirect.com/article/visionquest-episodes-friday-edith-ai-real-mcu-characters). The recap lists who stands behind each:

| Stark AI | Based on, per The Direct's recap |
|---|---|
| JARVIS | **Edwin Jarvis**, the Stark family butler |
| FRIDAY | **Allison Hogan**, Howard Stark's office assistant, who helped raise Tony |
| E.D.I.T.H. | **Edith Marks**, a test pilot who worked for Howard Stark |
| Dum-E and U | Nobody |

GamesRadar+'s Easter-egg guide spells FRIDAY's namesake [Alison Hogan](https://www.gamesradar.com/entertainment/marvel-tv-shows/visionquest-episode-3-4-easter-eggs-mcu/) and says only that she "kept his office." Ultron is left off FRIDAY's list too; The Direct writes that he calls himself [a "one-off"](https://thedirect.com/article/visionquest-episodes-friday-edith-ai-real-mcu-characters) that Stark cooked up on a whim.

## The coverage disagrees on whether she died in the crash

Three write-ups of the reveal, by Inverse, The Direct and GamesRadar+, tell her story in three ways. None of them is the episode itself:

| Outlet | Her job | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| Inverse | Test pilot for Stark Industries | She died with her husband in a plane crash |
| The Direct | Test pilot who worked for Howard Stark | She lost her husband in an accident and used thrills to escape the pain |
| GamesRadar+ | Fighter pilot | Her husband died in a plane crash |

A **test pilot** can also be a **fighter pilot**, so the job labels need not clash. Her death is the real split, and it is Inverse against the rest: Inverse alone has her dying with her husband, The Direct has her outliving him, and GamesRadar+ mentions only his death. So does the Film Shrine explainer on The Tab's site, which says [the real Edith Marks "was a fighter pilot whose husband died in a plane crash"](https://thetab.com/filmshrine/?p=10539). Den of Geek's episode 4 review names him: E.D.I.T.H.'s forerunner [was married to Edward Marks](https://www.denofgeek.com/tv/visionquest-episode-4-review/).

The Direct's version, in which she outlives her husband and turns to thrills, fits Inverse's description of E.D.I.T.H. as ["the daredevil AI"](https://www.inverse.com/entertainment/edith-marks-visionquest-emily-hampshire-interview) of the series. Inverse and The Direct both put her on a Stark payroll.

## The acronym came first, from the 2019 film

E.D.I.T.H. arrived in Spider-Man: Far From Home. In Business Insider's account, Nick Fury hands Peter Parker [a pair of glasses Stark wanted him to have](https://www.businessinsider.com/spider-man-far-from-home-is-fun-and-surprising-review-2019-6), giving him access to all of Stark Industries' military capabilities, and the AI inside is E.D.I.T.H.

Hampshire started from that film's version: ["I started with the E.D.I.T.H. who is in Spider-Man: Far From Home,"](https://www.gamesradar.com/entertainment/marvel-tv-shows/visionquests-e-d-i-t-h-star-brought-elements-of-tony-stark-into-her-role-tonys-legacy-is-in-there/) she told GamesRadar+. Stark, she said, ["named her E.D.I.T.H. for Even Dead, I'm The Hero,"](https://www.gamesradar.com/entertainment/marvel-tv-shows/visionquests-e-d-i-t-h-star-brought-elements-of-tony-stark-into-her-role-tonys-legacy-is-in-there/) and "her boss was a teenager, and she was controlling weapons and blowing things up." Her version of the AI is that one let out of the glasses: ["she wants to experience life and have fun, and her idea of fun is blowing things up."](https://www.gamesradar.com/entertainment/marvel-tv-shows/visionquests-e-d-i-t-h-star-brought-elements-of-tony-stark-into-her-role-tonys-legacy-is-in-there/)

Inverse says that before the reveal, both E.D.I.T.H. and Hampshire thought her name meant ["Even Dead, I'm The Hero."](https://www.inverse.com/entertainment/edith-marks-visionquest-emily-hampshire-interview) The Tab quotes Hampshire saying she ["didn't know about it until we got the script for it"](https://thetab.com/filmshrine/?p=10539), in what she called "the fourth month or something."

## The fun one, built from a loss

In the episode, the discovery unsettles E.D.I.T.H. GamesRadar+ quotes her question to Ultron: ["Why would Stark pass that on to me? I was supposed to be the fun one?"](https://www.gamesradar.com/entertainment/marvel-tv-shows/visionquest-episode-4-finally-explains-the-stark-ais-origins-and-e-d-i-t-h-star-says-it-made-so-much-sense/) Ultron, the villain of Avengers: Age of Ultron, answers that she is "exceptionally brave" and that she gets to be the next version of who she once was.

Hampshire told Inverse the reveal ["really explained Edith a lot to me"](https://www.inverse.com/entertainment/edith-marks-visionquest-emily-hampshire-interview), and that it raised a question: "Is this generational thing passed down through AI?" She told GamesRadar+ that she learned it alongside the character: ["I didn't know that before, and when I learned that, it was so touching."](https://www.gamesradar.com/entertainment/marvel-tv-shows/visionquest-episode-4-finally-explains-the-stark-ais-origins-and-e-d-i-t-h-star-says-it-made-so-much-sense/)

> "She's like I'm the fun one, and it comes from such tragedy." - Emily Hampshire, [to GamesRadar+](https://www.gamesradar.com/entertainment/marvel-tv-shows/visionquest-episode-4-finally-explains-the-stark-ais-origins-and-e-d-i-t-h-star-says-it-made-so-much-sense/)

She called it ["so smart that Terry [Matalas, showrunner] did that"](https://www.gamesradar.com/entertainment/marvel-tv-shows/visionquest-episode-4-finally-explains-the-stark-ais-origins-and-e-d-i-t-h-star-says-it-made-so-much-sense/), because "I think of comedy as the cry of the powerless." She could relate, she said, and laughed as she put it: "I discovered comedy because I lost all my friends and I was like, 'I gotta be funny.'"

## Her death and her comics source are still open

Only the episode can settle whether she died in the crash; a recap is a reading of it, not a transcript.

Her comics link runs through her husband. Den of Geek counts Edward Marks among the episode's Marvel Comics references, calling him ["a character from the '80s war series The 'Nam"](https://www.denofgeek.com/tv/visionquest-episode-4-review/). None of the pages cited here names a comic-book original for Edith herself, and none says the show invented her.

E.D.I.T.H., the Stark AI meant to be the fun one, turned out to be modeled on Edith Marks, a pilot whose life was shaped by a crash, and the actor playing her learned it from a script. The letters first spelled out Even Dead, I'm The Hero, and now they also spell a woman's name. Strictly FYI.
