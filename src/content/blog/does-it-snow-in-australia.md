Uluru stands in the dry middle of the continent, a long way from any ski slope, and in the winter of 1997 it had snow on it. The National Film and Sound Archive's catalogue entry for the event opens with ["No, it's not a hoax, snow did fall on Uluru on 11 July 1997."](https://www.nfsa.gov.au/collection/curated/asset/98580-snow-uluru)

The Bureau of Meteorology's summary of that month lists, among its notable weather, that ["snow was reported at Uluru."](https://web.archive.org/web/20211204040109/http://www.bom.gov.au/inside/services_policy/public/sigwxsum/sigw0797.shtml) On the 12th, the same summary records, the weather station at Yulara, near the rock, reached a top temperature of only [**5.9** degrees C](https://web.archive.org/web/20211204040109/http://www.bom.gov.au/inside/services_policy/public/sigwxsum/sigw0797.shtml), the lowest maximum then on record for any Northern Territory station.

## Does it snow in Australia?

**Yes, every winter.** Snow falls on the Australian Alps of New South Wales and Victoria each year, with the peak season [usually between June and August](https://media.bom.gov.au/social/blog/2749/how-climate-drivers-affect-australias-snow-season/), the southern winter. Tasmania's highlands can see it in any month, Bluff Knoll in Western Australia gets light falls most winters, and rare snow has reached Queensland, the tropics and even Uluru.

## The Alps carry the season, and the snow is thinning

Australia's dependable snow sits on the Australian Alps, the high country that runs through eastern Victoria, the ACT and southern New South Wales, topped by [Mount Kosciuszko at **2228** metres](https://www.ga.gov.au/scientific-topics/national-location-information/landforms/highest-mountains).

Snowy Hydro measures snow depth at Spencers Creek in the Snowy Mountains, the site the Bureau calls [the longest record of snow depth at high elevations](https://media.bom.gov.au/social/blog/2749/how-climate-drivers-affect-australias-snow-season/). One storm can shape a season: around **a metre of snow** fell in less than a week in 2014, the Bureau notes, [approximately the average snow depth](https://media.bom.gov.au/social/blog/2749/how-climate-drivers-affect-australias-snow-season/) during the seasons between 1954 and 2022.

Over the long run, peak snow depths at Spencers Creek [have been decreasing by 0.35 cm/year between 1954 and 2022](https://media.bom.gov.au/social/blog/2749/how-climate-drivers-affect-australias-snow-season/), and State of the Climate 2024 reports that [maximum snow depth, snow cover and number of snow days have decreased](https://www.csiro.au/en/research/environmental-impacts/climate-change/state-of-the-climate/australias-changing-climate) in Australian alpine regions since the late 1950s.

## Snowfields "larger than those of Switzerland": a boast from at least 1957

Weatherzone's Anthony Sharwood calls it [one of the oldest myths in Australian weather-related folklore](https://www.weatherzone.com/au/news/does-australia-really-have-more-snow-than-switzerland/1381140). He checked it in 2023, after the New South Wales premier Chris Minns posted that the Snowy Mountains receive more snow than the Swiss Alps in winter. He points to the very first minute of a 1957 documentary, Roof of Australia, whose narrator says that Australia has ["snowfields larger than those of Switzerland"](https://www.weatherzone.com/au/news/does-australia-really-have-more-snow-than-switzerland/1381140).

Sharwood's verdict on the amount is blunt: Australia has nothing ["even remotely close to the amount of snow that would cover the Swiss Alps"](https://www.weatherzone.com/au/news/does-australia-really-have-more-snow-than-switzerland/1381140). The size version can be measured too, and it also fails. The Swiss figures here are the Swiss government's, and the Australian one is Sharwood's:

| Region | Area |
|---|---|
| Switzerland | [41,285 square kilometres](https://www.aboutswitzerland.eda.admin.ch/en/geography) |
| Swiss Alps, [60% of Switzerland's surface area](https://www.aboutswitzerland.eda.admin.ch/en/geography) | about 24,800 square kilometres |
| Australian Alps bioregion, Mt Baw Baw to the Brindabella Ranges | [about 12,300 square kilometres](https://www.weatherzone.com/au/news/does-australia-really-have-more-snow-than-switzerland/1381140) |

That puts the whole Australian Alps bioregion at about **half the Swiss Alps**, and since the bioregion also takes in snow-free slopes and valleys below the winter snowline, the ground that holds real winter snow is smaller again.

## Tasmania can have snow at Christmas

Tasmania lies south of the mainland Alps, and its alpine areas have no month in which snow is ruled out. For the Cradle Mountain area, the state's parks service warns that ["Snow, rain, wind and sun are all possible at any time of the year"](https://parks.tas.gov.au/explore-our-parks/cradle-mountain).

Christmas 2006, which falls in the southern summer, showed what that means. One of the most severe December cold outbreaks of the previous 50 years, the Bureau recorded, brought substantial snow [(**up to 20 centimetres** in places) above 800 metres in Tasmania](https://web.archive.org/web/20160128173657/http://www.bom.gov.au/inside/services_policy/public/sigwxsum/sigw1206.shtml) and above 1,000 to 1,200 metres in Victoria and southern New South Wales. On the morning of the 25th, [snow fell (but did not settle)](https://web.archive.org/web/20160128173657/http://www.bom.gov.au/inside/services_policy/public/sigwxsum/sigw1206.shtml) in the Dandenong Ranges, on the outer fringe of Melbourne.

In winter it comes far lower: in the same July that snow reached Uluru, a cold southerly airstream left Tasmania with [snow settling to the 150 metre level](https://web.archive.org/web/20211204040109/http://www.bom.gov.au/inside/services_policy/public/sigwxsum/sigw0797.shtml) on the 10th, closing many roads.

## Western Australia's snow lands on the Stirling Range

The highest point of the Stirling Range is the [**1099-metre** Bluff Knoll](https://web.archive.org/web/20190420095559/http://www.weatherzone.com.au/news/snow-falls-in-wa-in-april-for-first-time-in-49-years-as-good-friday-cold-blast-turns-bluff-knoll-white/529610). An ABC News report that Weatherzone republished in 2019 put the peak about 100 kilometres north of Albany and said it [generally records light snow a couple of times each winter](https://web.archive.org/web/20190420095559/http://www.weatherzone.com.au/news/snow-falls-in-wa-in-april-for-first-time-in-49-years-as-good-friday-cold-blast-turns-bluff-knoll-white/529610). That year flurries arrived on Good Friday, in April, and the ABC called it [the earliest recorded snow event in a calendar year](https://web.archive.org/web/20190420095559/http://www.weatherzone.com.au/news/snow-falls-in-wa-in-april-for-first-time-in-49-years-as-good-friday-cold-blast-turns-bluff-knoll-white/529610) in the state's history.

## Queensland's heavy falls are rare, and the tropics had snow in 1965

Snow began falling around Stanthorpe on the Granite Belt about midnight on 17 July 2015, and by morning the Bureau said [**up to eight centimetres**](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2015-07-17/thick-snow-blankets-southern-queensland/6626630) had been recorded by locals around Eukey and Stanthorpe. Senior forecaster Sam Campbell told the ABC the Bureau had ["a record of five centimetres falling in 2007 and nothing significant in our records between then and 1984"](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2015-07-17/thick-snow-blankets-southern-queensland/6626630), so these were possibly the state's best falls in over 30 years.

Farther north, a farm's rainfall books revived the story. Going through rainfall records kept on his property since 1895, the cane grower and grazier Joseph Borg found a note that [on **July 19, 1965**, snow fell on the peak of Blue Mountain near Mackay](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2020-07-17/when-snow-fell-in-tropical-queensland/12407184), well north of the Tropic of Capricorn. When he posted the find on Facebook, some of his friends argued it was perhaps a heavy frost, but Blair Trewin, senior climatologist at the Bureau, told the ABC: ["There was indeed snow in July 1965."](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2020-07-17/when-snow-fell-in-tropical-queensland/12407184)

Falls that year were reported down to about 800 metres, Trewin said, and a repeat of that event a degree warmer would bring snow [down to maybe 1,000 metres](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2020-07-17/when-snow-fell-in-tropical-queensland/12407184), which a few peaks in the region still clear.

## Uluru gets freezing nights, but rarely cold air and moisture together

Snow needs cold air and moisture at the same time. Trewin, explaining the 1965 fall, told the ABC: ["You need the air mass to be cold enough, which doesn't happen very often in the tropics, and secondly you need moisture."](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2020-07-17/when-snow-fell-in-tropical-queensland/12407184) In July 1997 the centre had the cold, enough to give Yulara the [lowest maximum on record for any Northern Territory station](https://web.archive.org/web/20211204040109/http://www.bom.gov.au/inside/services_policy/public/sigwxsum/sigw0797.shtml). Usually it is the moisture that winter fails to bring, as the archive's note on the Uluru snowfall puts it:

> "Located in central Australia, temperatures around Uluru have been known to fall below zero but usually there is no precipitation in winter to create snow." - [National Film and Sound Archive of Australia](https://www.nfsa.gov.au/collection/curated/asset/98580-snow-uluru)

The same note says the event was so rare that [no record of such an event appears in any stories of the local Pitjantjatjara Anangu people](https://www.nfsa.gov.au/collection/curated/asset/98580-snow-uluru).

The snowfields once billed as larger than Switzerland's take up less than half the area of its Alps, and the rock in the middle of the desert has had snow on it within living memory. Strictly FYI.
