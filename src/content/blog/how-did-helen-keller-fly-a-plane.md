Over Corsica on **November 26, 1946**, the pilot of a plane bound from Rome to Paris invited one of his passengers to sit beside him.

The pilot was a Captain Lamb, flying the plane of Myron C. Taylor, the US president's [personal representative to the Pope](https://history.state.gov/departmenthistory/people/taylor-myron-charles). The passenger was Helen Keller, 66, deaf and blind since she was [19 months old](https://afb.org/about-afb/history/helen-keller/biography-and-chronology/biography). Once she was seated, Lamb let her touch the instruments "registering height, bumpiness, closeness to the ground," the engine throttles, the de-icing gear and what she called "the seeing eye of the plane - the radar." Then he offered her the wheel.

## How did Helen Keller fly a plane?

Helen Keller flew a plane by steering it in mid-flight with the pilot beside her. On November 26, 1946, between Rome and Paris, Captain Lamb handed her the control wheel. She felt the plane's motion through the controls while her companion Polly Thomson relayed his instructions, and by her own account steered "for several minutes." She never flew solo.

She was in Europe for the American Foundation for Overseas Blind, now Helen Keller International, which had made her its counselor on international relations that year. She and Thomson [left La Guardia Field](https://archive.org/details/sim_journal-of-visual-impairment-blindness_1948-01_42_1) on **October 17, 1946**, on the [first of their world tours](https://afb.org/about-afb/history/helen-keller/biography-and-chronology/chronology), visiting blind people in London, Paris, Italy, Greece and Scotland. In Rome she went down into [the catacombs](/blog/why-were-the-catacombs-built/), where she "touched the Roman oil-lamps and the earthen water-jars."

## She steered by feel, and said so in print

Keller described the trip in ["My Latest Visit to Europe"](https://archive.org/details/sim_journal-of-visual-impairment-blindness_1948-01_42_1), published in January 1948 in *Outlook for the Blind*, the American Foundation for the Blind's journal.

> "He offered me the wheel, and I could easily perceive the motions up and down, back and forth, from side to side as I steered for several minutes." - Helen Keller, *Outlook for the Blind*, January 1948

The navigator then showed her his instruments, "working in harmony with those of the pilot." It was her second cockpit visit in ten days. On **November 16** she had flown from London to Rome on an army plane that rose to 12,000 feet as it passed Corsica, and its pilot had invited her to sit with him and shown her his instruments. Sitting there, she wrote, she realized "what it must cost the pilot in alertness and fatigue."

Her article does not say how the pilot's words reached her. Thomson filled that in for a Glasgow paper, *The Bulletin*, whose clipping is in the foundation's Helen Keller Archive. "She sat in the co-pilot's seat with the pilot beside her, and I relayed to her his instructions," Thomson said, as [Snopes](https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/helen-keller-fly-plane/) quotes it. "There was no shaking or vibration." Keller called it wonderful to feel "the delicate movement of the aircraft through the controls."

## The 20 minutes come from the newspaper

*The Bulletin* said Keller "took over the pilot's controls for 20 minutes" in "a Douglas Skymaster," according to the text quoted by the fact-checking site [Lead Stories](https://leadstories.com/hoax-alert/2026/01/fact-check-helen-keller-life-achievements-were-not-faked.html). That is the figure that spread. Keller's own account, in the foundation's journal, says **several minutes**, and neither source explains the gap. [Einstein's IQ](/blog/what-was-einsteins-iq/) spread the same way, as numbers printed by writers with no test on record behind any of them.

The date has drifted as well. The archive catalogs the clipping as [June 1946](https://helenkellerarchive.afb.org/?a=d&d=A-HK01-02-B034-F01-009), and [Britannica's explainer](https://www.britannica.com/story/how-did-helen-keller-fly-a-plane) opens with a June 1946 flight. Neither can be right, since Keller did not leave New York until October 17.

## She had flown before, over Los Angeles

Nearly three decades earlier, while making *Deliverance*, a silent film about her life produced in 1918, Keller insisted on being taken up in a plane, though her teacher, her mother and her brother at first would not hear of it. "There was only room for the pilot and me," she wrote in her 1929 memoir, [*Midstream*](https://archive.org/details/midstreammylater00kell).

They shot over the tallest buildings of Los Angeles and returned "after half an hour's race with a high wind," and then the plane went through "a series of amazing dips." She wrote: "I had never had such a satisfying sense of physical liberty."

## Her conversations ran on touch

In 1887 Anne Sullivan spelled w-a-t-e-r into one of Keller's hands while water ran over the other, and for the rest of her life people spoke to her in the manual alphabet. She read it, she explained in [*The Story of My Life*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2397), by placing her hand "on the hand of the speaker so lightly as not to impede its movements."

She began learning to speak in the spring of 1890, and read lips with her fingers, "catching the vibrations of the throat, the movements of the mouth and the expression of the face." Speech researchers call this way of listening [Tadoma](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3973218/): a hand on the talker's face, tracking lip and jaw movement, airflow and the vibration of the larynx.

Vibration could carry whole messages. A cousin taught her the telegraph alphabet by making [dots and dashes](/blog/what-does-sos-stand-for/) on the back of her hand, and Sullivan wrote that she could talk to Keller at a distance by tapping on the floor with her foot. "She feels the vibrations and understands what is said to her."

## The joke question has a paper trail

The plane story went viral in December 2020, when a TikTok video sarcastically called Keller a liar, [Snopes](https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/helen-keller-fly-plane/) reported, and in January 2026 [Lead Stories](https://leadstories.com/hoax-alert/2026/01/fact-check-helen-keller-life-achievements-were-not-faked.html) was still answering posts claiming her achievements were faked. The American Foundation for the Blind records that she wrote [**14 books** and more than 475 speeches and essays](https://www.afb.org/about-afb/history/helen-keller/books-essays-speeches), and its [digitized archive](https://afb.org/about-afb/history/helen-keller/helen-keller-archive) of her papers is set to hold more than 160,000 images. Her account of the flight has been in print since 1948.

The whole episode began with a scheduling clash. Taylor had arranged for Keller to meet the Pope at around eleven that morning, and the audience made her miss her flight to Paris. So he sent her on later in the day in his own plane, and Keller owed her turn at the wheel to the Pope. Strictly FYI.
