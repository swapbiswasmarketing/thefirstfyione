Fraser McInnes was trying to make a cappuccino at Cafe Bodega on Willis Street, Wellington, "in the summer of late 1989," when the milk would not froth properly. There was not enough fat in it, [Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/life-style/food-wine/food-news/64886421/Kiwi-claims-flat-white-invention) reported in January 2015, so he went over to the customer and, by his account, said: "sorry, it's a flat white."

Across the Tasman Sea, a Sydney cafe owner says he had put the same name on a menu years earlier. Alan Preston, then 29, took a lease on a shop at **394 Sussex Street** in Haymarket in the summer of 1984 to 1985, and on the first menu of his Moors Espresso Bar the flat white sat second, under the cappuccino, according to [a site that makes his case](http://www.flatwhitehistory.com.au/) with his photographs.

Preston's [own recipe](http://www.flatwhitehistory.com.au/who-invented-the-flat-white.php) for the drink asks for "at least 1 inch of froth." His first menu survives in photographs, but much of both founding stories rests on the founders' own memories, and the name turns up in print before either of them.

## What is a flat white?

A flat white is a coffee made of espresso, often a double shot, and steamed milk finished with a thin layer of silky microfoam. It is smaller than a latte, New Zealand Story's [explainer](https://eyesonnewzealand.com/stories/who-invented-the-flat-white) says, and Google's [2024 doodle](https://doodles.google/doodle/celebrating-the-flat-white/) calls it "flatter" than a cappuccino or latte, for drinkers who are "looking for less foam." The headline claims in both Australia and New Zealand date from the 1980s.

The doodle added that the drink is traditionally served in a ceramic cup, and it ran on the anniversary of the day in **2011**, it [said](https://doodles.google/doodle/celebrating-the-flat-white/), when the term was added to the Oxford English Dictionary.

## Less milk than a latte, less foam than a cappuccino

In Australia, "it's typically a 3 to 1 drink: 3 parts milk to 1 part espresso," Mark Prince writes in [CoffeeGeek's guide](https://coffeegeek.com/guides/howtos/flat-white-how-to/). His [numbers](https://coffeegeek.com/guides/howtos/flat-white-how-to/) are **40-60 ml of espresso**, a double shot, and **140-180 ml of steamed milk**, in a 180-240 ml serving that should "only have a few millimeters of visible foam on top."

Prince calls it a drink that slots in between the cappuccino and the latte. In the historian Garritt Van Dyk's [description](https://theconversation.com/who-invented-the-flat-white-italian-sugar-farmers-from-regional-queensland-likely-played-a-big-role-224747), it blends the crema of the espresso with a thin layer of microfoam and, "without the thicker foam of a latte or cappuccino," delivers "a stronger coffee flavour." The proportions shift by country: in the USA, Prince [notes](https://coffeegeek.com/guides/howtos/flat-white-how-to/), the ratio "sometimes gets pushed up to 4 parts milk, and sometimes as low as a 2:1 ratio."

## Sydney's claim rests on a menu Preston dates to 1985

Van Dyk, a history lecturer at Australia's University of Newcastle, [writes](https://theconversation.com/who-invented-the-flat-white-italian-sugar-farmers-from-regional-queensland-likely-played-a-big-role-224747) of claims "from England to New Zealand" and calls Preston's "the best case for coining the term." Preston "claims he was the first to use the term on a menu, and has documented this use through photographs," he writes.

Preston, originally from North Queensland, says the drink came south with him. The cafes there, by his account, offered five coffees: a short black, a long black, a cappuccino, a Vienna and a "flat." On his own menu, Van Dyk [writes](https://theconversation.com/who-invented-the-flat-white-italian-sugar-farmers-from-regional-queensland-likely-played-a-big-role-224747), Preston changed the last one to "flat white."

Van Dyk's own argument is that the drink "very likely" took root in regional Queensland. Preston says these Queensland cafes had the best espresso machines of the time, imported from Italy, and Van Dyk writes that the drink was supposedly popular in sugar-producing towns, where it "catered to the tastes of wealthy Italian growers and their families." By **1925**, Van Dyk [notes](https://theconversation.com/who-invented-the-flat-white-italian-sugar-farmers-from-regional-queensland-likely-played-a-big-role-224747), Italians owned **44%** of the sugar farms in the Herbert River area.

The history on Preston's site, written by its researcher S.J. Will, [goes one step further back](http://www.flatwhitehistory.com.au/who-invented-the-flat-white.php): "We think the original Flat White was the North Queensland 'White Coffee - Flat'," as the drink was occasionally described in Golden Fleece roadhouses.

That Queensland chapter rests on Preston's memory. On his site he is careful: "I don't surely know if I really was the first to do so but I can prove I had Flat White on my permanent menus starting in 1985," he [says there](http://www.flatwhitehistory.com.au/), and for the Queensland drink the site admits it is "unable to find any evidence of it existing after the 70s," leaning on "anecdotal stories" for the years before. He was blunter with the [Sydney Morning Herald](https://www.smh.com.au/goodfood/australia-and-new-zealand-culinary-war-in-new-front-over-flat-white-inventor-20150926-gjvjkz.html) in September 2015: "I'm telling you I was the first one to name it."

## The name is older than either menu

The Oxford English Dictionary's [evidence](https://www.oed.com/dictionary/flat-white_n) for the term flat white goes back to 1971, in the writing of P. Shaffer, more than a decade before Sydney or Wellington put it on a menu. In Sydney itself, a Sydney Morning Herald review of the cafe Miller's Treat, by Liz Doyle and Brett Wright and dated 6 May 1983, is [cited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat_white) as mentioning a "flat white coffee," though the citation gives no page number or link.

Nor can any menu show that nobody poured the drink sooner. Ian Bersten, the founder of Belaroma coffee and the author of a history of coffee brewing technology, thinks the drink probably took off in England in the 1950s, but he told the [Herald](https://www.smh.com.au/goodfood/australia-and-new-zealand-culinary-war-in-new-front-over-flat-white-inventor-20150926-gjvjkz.html): "Nobody knows who invented the flat white; it just came out of nowhere."

## Wellington's story begins with a failed cappuccino

McInnes, a Wellington hospitality stalwart, told [Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/life-style/food-wine/food-news/64886421/Kiwi-claims-flat-white-invention) the story of the milk that would not froth properly a quarter-century after the episode it describes. His business partner, he said, then put the drink on the menu blackboard, and McInnes gave it a definition of his own: "A Wellington flat white is a failed cappuccino." Starbucks had just added the drink to its United States and Canadian menus, and McInnes said he was "chuffed" to see it drunk around the world.

That episode survives only in his telling, and its season moves. Stuff's [report](https://www.stuff.co.nz/life-style/food-wine/food-news/64886421/Kiwi-claims-flat-white-invention) places it "in the summer of late 1989" and, a line later, at "the end of summer." In the [Herald](https://www.smh.com.au/goodfood/australia-and-new-zealand-culinary-war-in-new-front-over-flat-white-inventor-20150926-gjvjkz.html) eight months later, the milk failed "on a Wellington winter's day in 1989."

New Zealand Story files the Wellington version as "one popular New Zealand story." Its [verdict](https://eyesonnewzealand.com/stories/who-invented-the-flat-white): "The truth is that there is no single agreed origin story."

McInnes himself allowed, [Stuff](https://www.stuff.co.nz/life-style/food-wine/food-news/64886421/Kiwi-claims-flat-white-invention) reported, that other flat whites "may have preceded his," among them an Auckland version made with a single shot and warm milk. Craig Miller, who was about to publish a history of Wellington's coffee houses, called the flat white a "generic drink" with no clear original. The one he remembered making in Auckland in the mid-1980s, from an Australian recipe, was a double shot of black coffee with a jug of milk on the side.

## The two founding stories disagree about the foam

Modern guides ask for a thin layer of foam. Preston's recipe, signed with his name on the [same site](http://www.flatwhitehistory.com.au/who-invented-the-flat-white.php), starts with "30 ml of dark roasted Arabica essence in a ceramic cup" and milk "frothed as you would for a Cappuccino," and the inch of froth is there "to create the meniscus." Latte art is out: "NO ART!"

> "The idea that a Flat White has no froth is a complete nonsense." - Alan Preston

McInnes's version, by his account, began as a cappuccino gone wrong, and Stuff described the result as a double shot under a silky layer of foam. The two countries are claiming one name for drinks that differed from city to city.

## Starbucks credited Australia when it brought the drink to America

Starbucks put the flat white on its year-round core menu from **January 6, 2015**, [CNBC reported](https://www.cnbc.com/2015/01/06/starbucks-debuts-down-under-inspired-drink.html), as steamed whole milk poured over two shots of concentrated espresso and topped with "microfoam" and a latte art dot, the very decoration Preston's recipe bans. Starbucks, the [Herald](https://www.smh.com.au/goodfood/australia-and-new-zealand-culinary-war-in-new-front-over-flat-white-inventor-20150926-gjvjkz.html) noted, "credits the drink to Australia."

Google's doodle split the difference: many speculate, it [said](https://doodles.google/doodle/celebrating-the-flat-white/), that the drink "popped up on menus in Sydney and Auckland around the same time."

The man with the Sydney menu photographs insists on at least an inch of froth. The popular Wellington story begins with milk that would not froth properly, and a modern guide asks for a few millimeters. All three answer to the same name. Strictly FYI.
