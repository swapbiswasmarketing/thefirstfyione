The story goes like this: a shadowy society called the Illuminati has secretly run world affairs for centuries, and it signs its work. The proof is in your wallet - a glowing eye floating over an unfinished pyramid on the back of the one-dollar bill, a boast hidden in plain sight.

It is a tidy story with one problem. Every checkable part of it fails, and it fails on dates.

## Why is the Illuminati on the dollar bill?

It isn't. The eye and pyramid are the reverse of the **Great Seal of the United States**, [officially adopted by Congress on June 20, 1782](https://www.govinfo.gov/features/great-seal), and [both its sides reached the one-dollar note in 1935](https://www.uscurrency.gov/history). The real Illuminati, a Bavarian society outlawed within a decade of its founding, [took the owl of Minerva as its symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illuminati).

## There was a real Illuminati, briefly

[Adam Weishaupt founded the Illuminati of Bavaria on May 1, 1776](http://freemasonry.bcy.ca/texts/illuminati.html). He [held the chair of natural and canon law at the University of Ingolstadt](http://freemasonry.bcy.ca/texts/illuminati.html), which had last belonged to a member of the recently dissolved Jesuit order. The name is Latin for "enlightened", and the society [set itself against superstition and obscurantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illuminati), against the church's grip on public life and against kings who abused their power.

One of its emblems was the [owl of Minerva perched on a book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illuminati), used in the "Minerval" degree, one of the junior grades new recruits passed through. Beyond that, the record is thin: BBC Culture notes that ["we also don't know how important visual symbols were to the original order."](https://www.bbc.com/culture/article/20201112-the-eye-of-providence-the-symbol-with-a-secret-meaning)

The order did not last. The Elector of Bavaria issued [edicts for its suppression on 22 June 1784 and 2 March 1785](http://freemasonry.bcy.ca/texts/illuminati.html), and Weishaupt lost his chair in 1785 and left the country. Raids on two members' homes in October 1786 turned up papers that the [Bavarian government published in Munich in 1787](http://freemasonry.bcy.ca/texts/illuminati.html) as *Einige Originalschriften des Illuminaten Ordens*. Whatever the Illuminati were planning, they did not survive long enough to plan it for anyone's currency.

## The eye is older than the country

The floating eye has a name, the **Eye of Providence**, and it was old long before 1776. The State Department's official history of the seal, *The Eagle and the Shield*, says that using an eye to stand for an all-knowing God was ["a well established artistic convention"](https://archive.org/details/TheEagleAndTheShield) in art and medals, independent of Freemasonry.

An often-cited early example is Pontormo's [*Supper at Emmaus*](https://www.uffizi.it/en/artworks/pontormo-supper-emmaus), painted in **1525** for the guest quarters of the charterhouse at Galluzzo, near Florence, and it comes with a twist. The Uffizi, which owns the painting, says the divine eye in a triangle at the top is ["the result of a posthumous repainting job"](https://www.uffizi.it/en/artworks/pontormo-supper-emmaus) that covered Pontormo's original three-faced Trinity after the Counter-Reformation banned that image. BBC Culture suggests the eye was painted on ["perhaps in the 1600s."](https://www.bbc.com/culture/article/20201112-the-eye-of-providence-the-symbol-with-a-secret-meaning)

A surer date comes from England, where in 1614 the frontispiece of Walter Raleigh's *History of the World* [showed an eye in a cloud labeled "Providentia"](https://skirret.com/archive/stb/stb1995-09.html) above a globe.

For the American seal, the eye came from the very first attempt. On July 4, 1776, [Congress named Franklin, Adams and Jefferson as a committee for a seal](http://www.greatseal.com/committees/firstcomm/index.html), and they asked the artist **Pierre Eugene du Simitiere** to help. His crest was ["The Eye of Providence in a radiant Triangle whose Glory extends over the Shield and beyond the Figures."](http://www.greatseal.com/committees/firstcomm/index.html) Congress received the design on **August 20, 1776**, and the [journal records that it was "Ordered, To lie on the table."](http://www.greatseal.com/committees/firstcomm/index.html)

Two more committees followed. In May 1782 William Barton, consultant to the third, proposed ["A Pyramid of thirteen Strata, (or Steps)."](http://www.greatseal.com/symbols/pyramid.html) with an eye above it. **Charles Thomson**, the secretary of Congress, was [asked to come up with a design on June 13, 1782](https://www.govinfo.gov/features/great-seal); he kept Barton's pyramid and [added a triangle around the eye](http://www.greatseal.com/symbols/eye.html), [as suggested by the first committee](http://www.greatseal.com/symbols/pyramid.html).

The wording Congress adopted for the reverse begins ["A Pyramid unfinished. In the Zenith an Eye in a triangle surrounded with a glory proper."](http://www.greatseal.com/symbols/blazon.html) [It adds "On the base of the pyramid the numerical letters MDCCLXXVI"](http://www.greatseal.com/symbols/blazon.html) for 1776. The thirteen courses on the dollar are a tradition, since [no number is specified in the official 1782 description](http://www.greatseal.com/mythamerica/mythreverse.html).

## The designers explained it in plain words

Thomson's remarks of June 20, 1782, the only official account of what the symbols mean, are blunt: ["The pyramid signifies strength and duration."](http://www.greatseal.com/symbols/explanation.html) The eye and the motto *Annuit Coeptis*, [he added](http://www.greatseal.com/symbols/explanation.html), "allude to the many signal interpositions of providence in favor of the American cause." The date beneath the pyramid, he wrote, ["is that of the Declaration of Independence"](http://www.greatseal.com/symbols/explanation.html), and *Novus Ordo Seclorum* marks "the beginning of the new American Era" that started then.

Both mottos are Thomson's adaptations of **Virgil**. *Annuit Coeptis* reworks a line of the *Georgics* that has been translated ["Give me an easy course, and favor my daring undertakings."](http://www.greatseal.com/mottoes/coeptisvirgil.html) Thomson changed the verb to the third person, giving a motto that can be translated ["Providence has favored our undertakings."](http://www.greatseal.com/mottoes/coeptis.html)

*Novus Ordo Seclorum* comes from the fourth *Eclogue*, line 5: ["Magnus ab integro seclorum nascitur ordo."](http://www.greatseal.com/mottoes/seclorumvirgil.html) [Thomson had once been a Latin teacher, and Virgil ranked among his favorite poets](http://www.greatseal.com/mottoes/seclorumvirgil.html). The phrase means a new order of the ages, a new era, and it has nothing to do with a "new world order", which [greatseal.com, John D. MacArthur's history of the seal, calls a 20th-century English phrase](http://www.greatseal.com/mythamerica/mythreverse.html).

## Even the Freemason version has a timeline problem

The fallback theory swaps the Illuminati for the Freemasons, who really do use an all-seeing eye. The Masonic historian S. Brent Morris, writing for the Masonic Service Association, says [Benjamin Franklin was the only Mason on the first design committee](https://skirret.com/archive/stb/stb1995-09.html) and that his suggestions had no Masonic content. Lodge records from the period are patchy, but the official history finds ["no evidence that either Charles Thomson or the Philadelphia William Barton was a Mason"](https://archive.org/details/TheEagleAndTheShield), and those two produced the final design.

The all-seeing eye had been popularized in Masonic designs of the late eighteenth century, but its first official Masonic definition, Morris writes, ["seems to have come in 1797 with The Freemasons Monitor of Thomas Smith Webb"](https://skirret.com/archive/stb/stb1995-09.html). Webb's book came off a press [at Albany in 1797](https://archive.org/details/the-freemasons-monitor-or-illuistrations-of-masonry-1797), fifteen years after Congress adopted the seal. Morris adds that Webb did not describe the eye as being in a triangle, and that the meanings differ: the seal's eye represents ["an active intervention of God in the affairs of men"](https://skirret.com/archive/stb/stb1995-09.html), while the Masonic eye stands for "a passive awareness by God of the activities of men."

> "The Latin phrase Novus Ordo Seclorum impressed me as meaning the New Deal of the Ages." - Henry A. Wallace, recalling 1934, [as quoted at greatseal.com](http://www.greatseal.com/dollar/hawfdr.html)

## Roosevelt moved it onto the dollar in 1935

The seal has a back at all because [two-sided pendant seals were in use](http://www.greatseal.com/symbols/pyramid.html) in 1782, yet no die of the pyramid side has ever been cut. A law of 1884 paid for one, but the official history concludes ["that the reverse was not cut."](https://archive.org/details/TheEagleAndTheShield)

[By his own later account](http://www.greatseal.com/dollar/hawfdr.html), Secretary of Agriculture **Henry A. Wallace** was waiting to see Secretary of State Cordell Hull one day in 1934 when he leafed through a State Department history of the seal and noticed that the reverse "had never been used."

[In Wallace's telling](http://www.greatseal.com/dollar/hawfdr.html), he suggested a coin, and Roosevelt, who looked on the "All Seeing Eye" as a Masonic symbol of Deity, preferred the dollar bill. [Wallace also wrote](http://www.greatseal.com/dollar/hawfdr.html) that both men were 32nd degree Masons, and that Roosevelt asked Postmaster General James Farley, a Catholic, whether Catholics would object. The official history notes that Wallace's claim to the idea ["cannot be verified in official records"](https://archive.org/details/TheEagleAndTheShield): Treasury files show suggestions from a number of sources, and the records show that Roosevelt approved the new $1 silver certificate in July 1935. So a Masonic reading of the dollar's design is on record after all: Roosevelt's, as Wallace remembered it.

## Some credit a 1970s satire with keeping the story alive

Robert Shea and Robert Anton Wilson's satire *Illuminatus!* began appearing in September 1975, and its first volume was titled [*The Eye in the Pyramid*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Illuminatus!_Trilogy), with the eye standing for the Bavarian Illuminati. Cecil Adams of The Straight Dope later called the book ["a cult classic"](https://www.straightdope.com/21342214/is-the-dollar-bill-s-eye-on-a-pyramid-the-symbol-of-a-secret-society) and reported that some credit its authors "with single-handedly keeping the thing alive."

The dollar bill does carry a message from a group of men with big plans for the world. They signed it, too, just not in code. They were the Continental Congress, and the watching eye was their way of saying someone up there had been rooting for them. Strictly FYI.
