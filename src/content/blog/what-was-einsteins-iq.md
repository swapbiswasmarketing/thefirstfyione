LIFE magazine gave Albert Einstein an IQ score on November 12, 1945, without printing the number. Its story ["Yale Prodigy"](https://archive.org/details/Life-1945-11-12-Vol-19-No-20/page/n52/mode/1up) runs under a photograph of Merrill Kenneth Wolf, 14, the youngest graduate in the college's history, standing outside Yale's Sterling Memorial Library with his diploma. The text credits him with an I.Q. of 182, ["which is only 23 points lower than Einstein's,"](https://russellwarne.com/2023/09/14/the-search-for-albert-einsteins-iq/) so by the magazine's arithmetic Einstein's IQ was **205**.

The story did not say where Einstein's number came from. When the psychologist [Russell Warne](https://russellwarne.com/2023/09/14/the-search-for-albert-einsteins-iq/) went looking for a score in 2023, he found no figure from Einstein's lifetime older than LIFE's, and he judged that "it is extremely unlikely that Albert Einstein took an intelligence test."

## What was Einstein's IQ?

Nobody knows Einstein's IQ, because **there is no record that he ever took an intelligence test**. The number usually quoted now, **160**, has no test behind it. Having found published figures [ranging from 150 to 207](https://russellwarne.com/2023/09/14/the-search-for-albert-einsteins-iq/), Warne concluded in 2023 that "any currently existing claims of an IQ score for Albert Einstein are not credible."

The 160 turns up in news stories that measure gifted children against Einstein. When a British 11-year-old scored **162** on a Mensa test in 2022, [The Independent](https://www.independent.co.uk/news/uk/home-news/uk-schoolboy-mensa-hawking-einstein-b2224501.html) set him beside Stephen Hawking and reported that Einstein "is said to have had an IQ of around 160, although it is unclear whether either man did a test." Warne, who [collected such claims](https://russellwarne.com/2023/09/14/the-search-for-albert-einsteins-iq/), noted that 160 is a typical value in the 21st-century press but wrote that "there does not seem to be anything special about that number."

Measuring prodigies against Einstein was already routine while he was alive. In September 1935 [*Time*](https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0,33009,749014,00.html) reported that skeptics of the Stanford-Binet intelligence test "guffaw loudly when, every few months, some bright moppet turns up with an IQ claimed to be greater than Einstein's."

## The published numbers run from 150 to 207

LIFE did not stick with 205. In 1954 it ran ["A Little, Lonely Genius,"](https://archive.org/details/Life-1954-03-22-Vol-36-No-12/page/n102/mode/1up) about Brian Van Dale, an 8-year-old in Rhode Island with an I.Q. of 185, and added in parentheses, "Genius is 140," and that Einstein's I.Q. "is estimated at 192." No estimator was named.

That September the boy's father told the family's story in [*Coronet*](https://archive.org/details/sim_coronet_1954-09_36_5_0). He recalls asking the head of Brian's school for gifted children, just after being told Brian's IQ was 185, "Genius is about 160, isn't it?" and getting the reply, "Albert Einstein's is probably a little higher."

In October 1962 an article on IQ scores in [*Popular Mechanics*](https://archive.org/details/PopularMechanics1962/Popular%20Mechanics-10-1962/page/n117/mode/1up), by Theodore Berland, called Einstein a man "who never took an intelligence test" and, in the same parenthesis, [gave his estimated IQ as 207](https://russellwarne.com/2023/09/14/the-search-for-albert-einsteins-iq/). Malcolm Gladwell's [*Outliers*](https://archive.org/details/outliersstoryofs0000glad) went down to **150** in 2008, crediting an episode of the TV quiz show *1 vs. 100* that, in his words, "pointed out that Einstein had an IQ of 150."

## The Army's test for adults arrived when Einstein was 38

The first practical intelligence test, Alfred Binet and Theodore Simon's scale for French schoolchildren, [appeared in a preliminary version in 1905](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Binet), the year Einstein published the [four papers of his miracle year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annus_mirabilis_papers) in the journal *Annalen der Physik*. Even the letters IQ are younger than special relativity: the psychologist William Stern introduced his Intelligenzquotient in [a 1912 book that grew out of his talk to a psychology congress in Berlin that April](https://archive.org/details/diepsychologisch00ster), and its list of abbreviations reads "IQ. = Intelligenzquotient." Stern's quotient was the ratio of a child's mental age to its actual age, so a child working exactly at the normal level for its age [scored 1](https://archive.org/details/diepsychologisch00ster).

Testing adults took longer. The Army Alpha, which a [committee of American psychologists put together in the summer of **1917**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Alpha) to sort US Army recruits, is what [Warne](https://russellwarne.com/2023/09/14/the-search-for-albert-einsteins-iq/) calls "the first successful intelligence test for adults," and he doubted Einstein ever sat one: "It is not clear what he would have to gain from taking an intelligence test in midlife or old age." Einstein was living in Berlin by then, and that year he was made [director of the Kaiser Wilhelm Institute for Physics](https://www.mpp.mpg.de/en/about-us/history) when it was founded.

## Estimating a genius's IQ from records dates to 1917

Scoring someone who never sat a test has a method behind it. Lewis Terman [tried it on Francis Galton's childhood in 1917](https://archive.org/details/geneticstudiesof011555mbp), and in 1926 his Stanford student [Catharine Cox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catharine_Cox_Miles) studied **301** "of the most eminent men and women of history," estimating IQs from what [her study](https://archive.org/details/geneticstudiesof011555mbp) calls "the historical records of their heredity, their childhood, and their youth." Up to the age of 17, Goethe, Leibniz and the Dutch jurist Hugo Grotius, she found, "average **185** IQ on the basis of the records"; for the years from 17 to 26 [she rated Grotius and Leibniz at 190 and Goethe at **200**](https://archive.org/details/geneticstudiesof011555mbp), a figure she reached in "a single case."

Her subjects lived "between the years 1450 and 1850," [the study says](https://archive.org/details/geneticstudiesof011555mbp), so Einstein, born in [1879](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/1921/einstein/biographical/), was not among them. Nobody has applied her method to him since, [Warne found](https://russellwarne.com/2023/09/14/the-search-for-albert-einsteins-iq/): "no one has used this reputable methodology to estimate Einstein's IQ." He doubted the number would add much anyway: "An IQ is a score that compares a person to their peers, and we already know that Einstein was at or near the top of his peers in intellectual accomplishment."

## Einstein did not fail math, and said so

A second legend travels with the IQ number: that Einstein failed mathematics at school. The story reached the Ripley's Believe It or Not! newspaper column, and in 1935 a rabbi in Princeton showed Einstein a clipping headlined "Greatest living mathematician failed in mathematics," [Walter Isaacson wrote in *Time*](https://content.time.com/time/specials/packages/article/0,28804,1936731_1936743_1936758,00.html) in 2007.

Einstein laughed, and his reply, [as Isaacson tells it](https://content.time.com/time/specials/packages/article/0,28804,1936731_1936743_1936758,00.html), was "I never failed in mathematics," followed by "Before I was fifteen I had mastered differential and integral calculus."

In primary school, [Isaacson wrote](https://content.time.com/time/specials/packages/article/0,28804,1936731_1936743_1936758,00.html), he was at the top of his class and "far above the school requirements" in math. He did fail one exam. At sixteen, in October 1895, he sat the entrance exam for the federal polytechnic school in Zurich, and [obtained "the best results in the mathematical- and natural science subjects,"](https://library.ethz.ch/en/collections-and-archives/platforms/einstein-online/jugend-1879-1896.html) but his work in languages and history was judged "unsatisfactory" and his overall result "was rated as insufficient."

He finished secondary school in Aarau, Switzerland, instead. His Matura certificate, dated October 3, 1896, after exams that September, [gave him the **top grade of 6**](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Albert_Einstein%27s_exam_of_maturity_grades_%28color2%29.jpg) in algebra, geometry, descriptive geometry, physics and history.

Einstein's own verdict on his gifts survives in [a letter of March 11, 1952](https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein) to Carl Seelig: "I have no special talents. I am only passionately curious."

LIFE's prodigy of 1945 fell 23 points short of Einstein, and The Independent's schoolboy of 2022 beat him by two. Those stories put Einstein about 45 points apart, with no test behind either number. Strictly FYI.
