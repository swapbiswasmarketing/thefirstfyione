"Why aren't you down at the beach?" Joel Pritchard asked the children on the porch when he and his friend Bill Bell came back from a morning of golf to the Pritchards' summer place on Bainbridge Island, Washington. They said there was nothing to do, and his son met the idea of making up a game with "Oh yeah, Dad, big deal," [Pritchard recalled for the Washington State Oral History Program](https://app.leg.wa.gov/oralhistory/pritchard/pritchardfull.pdf). So the two men went out to the blacktopped badminton court with a pair of paddles from Pritchard's car trunk and, before long, a plastic ball borrowed from a neighbor's boy, by his account.

Pritchard's telling opens with a date most histories of the game do not use: ["It was in 1963, just before Evans ran for governor,"](https://app.leg.wa.gov/oralhistory/pritchard/pritchardfull.pdf) he said. The game the two men started that day became pickleball, and its [official birth year is two summers later](https://magazine.wsu.edu/web-extra/a-concise-history-of-pickleball/).

## When was pickleball invented?

Pickleball is officially dated to the summer of **1965**, when Joel Pritchard and Bill Bell improvised it on the backyard badminton court of [the Pritchards' cabin at Pleasant Beach](https://www.historylink.org/File/22417) on Bainbridge Island, Washington, and Barney McCallum soon joined them. HistoryLink wrote in 2016 that July-August 1965 is ["the date that the USA Pickleball Association and Pickle-Ball Inc. now specify as the correct date,"](https://www.historylink.org/file/20195) though the inventors' own memories varied.

## Two of the three inventors remembered 1963

"The exact date is unknown," HistoryLink, the online encyclopedia of Washington state history, says in its essay on the founding of Pickle Ball Inc., because ["the memories of the inventors are vague on the subject."](https://www.historylink.org/file/20195) ["Pritchard himself once said it was in summer 1963,"](https://www.historylink.org/file/20195) it notes, and Joan Pritchard, his wife at the time, [wrote in a 2008 newspaper column that it was the summer of 1966](https://web.archive.org/web/20120322085100/http://www.newsandsentinel.com/page/content.detail/id/507610.html?nav=5055).

Bill Bell agreed with Pritchard. In a tribute printed in the 2000 oral history, Bell wrote that the game was ["invented and first played on the Pritchard badminton court on Bainbridge Island during the summer of 1963"](https://app.leg.wa.gov/oralhistory/pritchard/pritchardfull.pdf). The official date comes from other tellings: in most accounts, HistoryLink says, ["both Barney McCallum and Joel Pritchard narrowed it down to July-August 1965."](https://www.historylink.org/file/20195)

## The net came down to 36 inches once the ball bounced

The original equipment came from whatever was lying around, and the tellings disagree about the paddles. Pritchard said he had ["two of those paddles in my trunk"](https://app.leg.wa.gov/oralhistory/pritchard/pritchardfull.pdf), meaning paddleball paddles, while USA Pickleball's timeline says ["ping-pong paddles"](https://web.archive.org/web/20260915225317/https://usapickleball.org/about/) and Joan Pritchard wrote that the men [drew two paddles and cut them out of plywood from a shed](https://web.archive.org/web/20120322085100/http://www.newsandsentinel.com/page/content.detail/id/507610.html?nav=5055). The ball had a story of its own: ["Dickie Green's kid came down the beach with one of those plastic balls,"](https://app.leg.wa.gov/oralhistory/pritchard/pritchardfull.pdf) Pritchard recalled, and he called the boy's mother to say he was not stealing it.

They began with the net at the badminton height of **60 inches**, and when they found the ball bounced well on asphalt, ["the net was lowered to 36 inches."](https://web.archive.org/web/20260915225317/https://usapickleball.org/about/) Pritchard's first requirement for the game was a golfer's. ["You've got to be able to hit it hard. Nobody plays golf to putt,"](https://app.leg.wa.gov/oralhistory/pritchard/pritchardfull.pdf) he said.

The rules came next, built for a family: Bell credited Pritchard with the two-bounce rule, the ball bouncing ["once on each side of net before a player could volley the ball in mid-air."](https://app.leg.wa.gov/oralhistory/pritchard/pritchardfull.pdf) McCallum, a neighbor who, by HistoryLink's account, crafted and refined the original plywood paddles in his basement, was responsible for [the "kitchen," a 7-foot-deep non-volley zone](https://www.historylink.org/File/22417) on each side of the net, which stops taller players from having an advantage over shorter ones.

## The name most likely came from a boat full of leftovers

Joan Pritchard wrote in her 2008 column that the name of the game became Pickle Ball ["after I said it reminded me of the Pickle Boat in crew where oarsmen were chosen from the leftovers of other boats."](https://web.archive.org/web/20120322085100/http://www.newsandsentinel.com/page/content.detail/id/507610.html?nav=5055) In crew races, [the non-starters among the rowers make up the crew](https://magazine.wsu.edu/web-extra/a-concise-history-of-pickleball/) of the pickle boat, Washington State University's magazine explains.

Other theories went around too. McCallum [claimed it stemmed from him telling his opponents, "I've got you in a pickle"](https://www.historylink.org/file/20195), and Pritchard said ["we needed a nutty name"](https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/?date=19900824&slug=1089412). Pritchard also told a reporter, HistoryLink says, that ["tenny pong" was under consideration at one point](https://www.historylink.org/file/20195).

Jennifer Lucore and Beverly Youngren, authors of History of Pickleball: More Than 50 Years of Fun! (2018), [could not conclusively determine whether the game was named for the dog or the dog for the game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pickleball), according to Wikipedia's summary of their book. By the same account, they turned up another claimant: [Bill Bell said he named it](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pickleball) for hitting the ball in a way that put his opponent in a pickle.

## The dog named Pickles came after the game

The story most people hear is the one The Seattle Times laid out in 1990: ["It was widely accepted that Pritchard, then a state representative who later served 12 years in Congress, named the game after his dog, Pickles."](https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/?date=19900824&slug=1089412) The next line corrected the record: ["Actually, Pickles was named after the game."](https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/?date=19900824&slug=1089412)

HistoryLink calls the dog story a fabrication and dates the animal: [Pickles the dog came along in **1968**](https://www.historylink.org/File/22417), three years after the official date of the game. USA Pickleball's own research, from dog records, photos and interviews, found ["the dog was born in 1968"](https://web.archive.org/web/20250926035639/https://usapickleball.org/what-is-pickleball/history-of-the-game/) and concluded the name came from the pickle boat races.

Pritchard said in his oral history that ["the dog was named after the game,"](https://app.leg.wa.gov/oralhistory/pritchard/pritchardfull.pdf) that a reporter doing a national story on pickleball had been told the dog version anyway, and that everyone around him wanted it left alone.

> "Everybody said, 'Shut your mouth. It's a good story. It works better, leave it alone.'" - Joel Pritchard, in [his oral history](https://app.leg.wa.gov/oralhistory/pritchard/pritchardfull.pdf)

USA Pickleball's later account, built from interviews with people who were there, tells it differently: Pritchard himself proposed the dog story to the reporter, ["as a fun story"](https://web.archive.org/web/20250926035639/https://usapickleball.org/what-is-pickleball/history-of-the-game/), and his son Frank said his father and McCallum ["agreed that this was the tale they would tell"](https://web.archive.org/web/20250926035639/https://usapickleball.org/what-is-pickleball/history-of-the-game/). Pritchard later admitted the game was not named for the dog, Frank said, but McCallum [held to the dog story "to his dying day."](https://web.archive.org/web/20250926035639/https://usapickleball.org/what-is-pickleball/history-of-the-game/)

## The inventors had not taken a dime out of it by 1990

On February 13, 1968, Pritchard and friends filed articles of incorporation for Pickle Ball Inc. ["to develop the game of Pickle Ball and to sell and promote said game in a lawful manner"](https://www.historylink.org/file/20195). Each founder [pitched in $500](https://www.historylink.org/File/22417), and the company sold boxed sets of paddles, balls and a net for **$29.95**.

It stayed small: financial disclosure statements in 1974 showed Pritchard was president of Pickle-Ball Inc., which The Seattle Times described as ["a firm with net assets under $1,500"](https://www.historylink.org/file/20195).

By 1990 the company was selling about 150,000 balls, 30,000 paddles and 7,500 complete sets a year, and although it was profitable, Pritchard told The Seattle Times, ["we've never taken a dime out of it."](https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/?date=19900824&slug=1089412) But the company had no lock on the game: by 1990 it faced ["competition from several other firms,"](https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/?date=19900824&slug=1089412) The Seattle Times reported. In 2016 the McCallum family's Pickle-ball, Inc. was [acquired by PickleballCentral](https://sgbonline.com/pickleballcentral-acquires-pickle-ball/).

## Washington made the backyard game its state sport in 2022

The game ["gained popularity slowly,"](https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/?date=19900824&slug=1089412) The Seattle Times wrote in 1990. In **1967** a Bainbridge neighbor ["paved the first permanent pickleball court,"](https://www.historylink.org/file/20195) by HistoryLink's account, though USA Pickleball's current timeline puts that court at ["Joel and Joan Pritchard's home in Magnolia Bluff, Seattle."](https://web.archive.org/web/20260915225317/https://usapickleball.org/about/) USA Pickleball dates ["the first known pickleball tournament in the world"](https://web.archive.org/web/20250926035639/https://usapickleball.org/what-is-pickleball/history-of-the-game/) to the spring of 1976, at a club in Tukwila, Washington.

The United States Amateur Pickleball Association was organized in 1984, and ["the first rulebook was published in March 1984,"](https://web.archive.org/web/20250926035639/https://usapickleball.org/what-is-pickleball/history-of-the-game/) by USA Pickleball's account. By 1990, HistoryLink says, ["courts existed in all 50 states."](https://www.historylink.org/file/20195) The real boom came decades later: the Sports and Fitness Industry Association's 2026 participation report [estimates 24.3 million Americans played pickleball in 2025](https://www.thedinkpickleball.com/report-24-million-americans-are-now-playing-pickleball-up-171-in-just-3-years/). In **2022** Washington's legislature passed [Senate Bill 5615](https://app.leg.wa.gov/billsummary?BillNumber=5615&Year=2021), designating pickleball as the official state sport, and Governor Jay Inslee signed it, the Bainbridge Island Review reported, ["on the original court on Bainbridge Island."](https://www.bainbridgereview.com/2022/03/29/pickleball-becomes-states-official-sport/)

Pritchard went on to become ["a six-term U.S. Representative from Seattle and a two-term lieutenant governor."](https://www.historylink.org/file/20195) At a party in his honor shortly before he died in 1997, his daughter recalled, he said that out of everything he had done, ["he was most proud of that game"](https://www.historylink.org/file/20195). He gave more than one year for the summer he made it up. Strictly FYI.
