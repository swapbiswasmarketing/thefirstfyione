An American company pinned down by a German machine gun north of Verdun learned on the morning of November 11, 1918, [that the war would end at 11](https://www.baltimoresun.com/2008/11/11/the-sad-senseless-end-of-henry-gunther/). As the hour approached, Private Henry Gunther, a 23-year-old from Baltimore, rose with his rifle and ran through thick fog. The men of his company shouted at him to stop, and so did the Germans, but he kept running and firing.

His death was recorded at **10:59 a.m.**, one minute before the armistice took effect. The regiment's Roman Catholic chaplain gave that account to Gunther's family in early March 1919, putting the news at 10:30 a.m., as the [Baltimore Sun](https://www.baltimoresun.com/2008/11/11/the-sad-senseless-end-of-henry-gunther/) retold it in 2008. The [BBC](http://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/uk_news/magazine/7696021.stm) says Gunther "is now recognised as the last soldier to be killed in action in WWI."

A [history of his division](https://archive.org/details/historyofseventy0079th), compiled by its veterans' association and published in 1922, is more careful. It calls him "perhaps the last man killed in action on the Western Front," and says the armistice message reached his regiment's First Battalion at 10:44.

Another account came from James M. Cain, the Sun reporter who would later become a novelist. He interviewed members of Gunther's company in France and learned that Gunther had been [reduced from sergeant to private](https://www.baltimoresun.com/2008/11/11/the-sad-senseless-end-of-henry-gunther/) over a letter critical of Army life, and that he "brooded a great deal over his reduction in rank." The Army later restored his rank and awarded him the Distinguished Service Cross.

That deadline was the eleventh hour of the eleventh day of the eleventh month.

## Why do we celebrate Veterans Day?

We celebrate Veterans Day to thank everyone who served honorably in the US military, in wartime or peacetime, and the [Department of Veterans Affairs](https://department.va.gov/veterans-day/facts-and-information/) says it is largely intended to thank **living** veterans. It falls on **November 11** because it began as Armistice Day, which marked the day in 1918 when [the guns on the Western Front fell silent](https://www.theworldwar.org/learn/about-wwi/armistice).

Since 2016, [36 U.S.C. 145](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/36/145) has directed the president to issue a proclamation each year calling for **two minutes of silence** on the day, beginning at 2:11 p.m. eastern standard time, which is 11:11 a.m. in the Pacific time zone.

## The ceasefire came six hours after the signing

The armistice was agreed at **5 a.m.** on November 11, after talks in a railcar in the Forest of Compiegne, the [National WWI Museum](https://www.theworldwar.org/learn/about-wwi/armistice) records. Marshal Ferdinand Foch then sent word to Allied commanders that "Hostilities will be stopped on the entire front beginning at 11 o'clock, November 11th (French hour)."

The [BBC's account](http://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/uk_news/magazine/7696021.stm) says the **six-hour** wait was so that troops on the front line would be sure of getting the news. Robert Casey, an American artilleryman [the museum quotes](https://www.theworldwar.org/learn/about-wwi/armistice), wrote that day: "In three hours the war will be over. It seems incredible even as I write it."

Men kept dying until the deadline. By "the most conservative estimates," the historian Joseph Persico wrote in [his history of the armistice](https://archive.org/details/eleventhmonthele00pers), all sides on the Western Front suffered **10,944 casualties**, 2,738 of them deaths, "during the last day of the war, principally in the six hours after the armistice was signed."

## The peace treaty came in 1919, or 1921 for the United States

Calling November 11 the end of World War I is a shorthand. An armistice is a temporary cessation of hostilities, and the [Treaty of Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles), signed on **June 28, 1919**, ended the state of war between Germany and most of the Allied Powers.

The United States never ratified that treaty. It fell seven votes short in the Senate on March 19, 1920, and the US signed its own peace with Germany, the Treaty of Berlin, on **August 25, 1921**, according to the State Department's [Office of the Historian](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1914-1920/paris-peace).

## States made November 11 a holiday before Congress did

President Woodrow Wilson proclaimed the first Armistice Day in November 1919, and [the VA's history of the holiday](https://department.va.gov/veterans-day/history-of-veterans-day/) says the original idea was a day of parades, public meetings and a brief suspension of business beginning at 11:00 a.m.

When Congress passed a concurrent resolution on June 4, 1926, asking the president for a proclamation, [its text](https://department.va.gov/veterans-day/history-of-veterans-day/) observed that "the legislatures of twenty-seven of our States have already declared November 11 to be a legal holiday."

Federal law caught up in 1938, when Congress made Armistice Day a federal holiday. By then, a [Congressional Research Service report](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R41990.html) notes, it was already a holiday in **48 states**: 44 by statute and the other four by a governor's action.

## In 1954, Congress crossed out one word

After World War II and Korea, the 83rd Congress acted "at the urging of the Veterans service organizations," [the VA records](https://department.va.gov/veterans-day/history-of-veterans-day/), and amended the 1938 act "by striking out the word 'Armistice' and inserting in its place the word 'Veterans.'" The bill was approved on **June 1, 1954**, and from then on November 11 honored American veterans of all wars.

Who started the push is disputed. On Veterans Day 1982, Ronald Reagan [presented the Presidential Citizens Medal](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/remarks-presenting-the-presidential-citizens-medal-raymond-weeks-veterans-day-ceremony) to Raymond Weeks of Birmingham, Alabama, a World War II veteran who had directed the National Veterans Day Celebration there for 36 years, and called him "the driving force behind the congressional action which in 1954 established this special holiday as a day to honor all American veterans."

In 2003, Congress agreed to [a concurrent resolution](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-108hconres159rfs/html/BILLS-108hconres159rfs.htm) that declares Emporia, Kansas, "the founding city of Veterans Day" and recognizes "Alvin J. King, of Emporia, Kansas, as the founder of Veterans Day." Its preamble says King, who "never served in the Armed Forces," proposed the change in 1953, the year "Veterans Day was first organized and celebrated in Emporia, Kansas."

In the Senate, Senator Roberts described King as a shoe cobbler and read out the case Representative Ed Rees of Emporia made for his bill, as recorded in the [Congressional Record](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2003-10-31/html/CREC-2003-10-31-pt1-PgS13742-2.htm): "This legislation does not establish a new holiday. Rather it expands an existing holiday so that we may honor all veterans at the same time."

## For seven years, the federal Veterans Day fell in October

The Uniform Holiday Bill, signed on June 28, 1968, set Veterans Day on [the fourth Monday in October](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/5/6103), with the change taking effect on January 1, 1971. The same law moved Washington's Birthday and Memorial Day to Mondays and made Columbus Day, already a holiday in [45 states](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R41990.html), a federal holiday.

Congress expected the Monday holidays to bring "substantial benefits to both the spiritual and economic life of the Nation," the [CRS report](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R41990.html) notes. Many states disagreed: by 1975, the same report says, "46 states either never changed the original observation date or returned the official observance to November 11."

> "The first Veterans Day under the new law was observed with much confusion on October 25, 1971," [the VA's history](https://department.va.gov/veterans-day/history-of-veterans-day/) records.

In September 1975, President Gerald Ford signed [a bill returning Veterans Day to November 11](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/statement-signing-bill-redesignating-november-11-veterans-day) beginning in **1978**, so the October date lasted through 1977. In his statement he said the move "supports the expressed will of the overwhelming majority of our State legislatures, all major veterans service organizations, and many individuals."

## Veterans Day thanks the living, Memorial Day the dead

The [VA](https://department.va.gov/veterans-day/facts-and-information/) separates the two holidays by who is honored. Memorial Day is for military personnel who died in the service of their country, especially those killed in battle or by wounds sustained in battle.

| | Veterans Day | Memorial Day |
|---|---|---|
| Date | November 11 | Last Monday in May |
| Honors | Everyone who served honorably, mainly the living | Those who died in service |
| [Flag code](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/4/6) | A display day, with no half-staff rule | Half-staff until noon |
| Poppies in the US | Not traditional | Traditional |

The poppies trace to John McCrae's 1915 poem *In Flanders Fields*, [the VA says](https://department.va.gov/veterans-day/facts-and-information/), and Americans traditionally wear them on Memorial Day, while many Canadians wear them on November 11.

The VA also [settles the spelling](https://department.va.gov/veterans-day/facts-and-information/): Veterans Day takes no apostrophe, "because it is not a day that 'belongs' to veterans, it is a day for honoring all veterans."

The holiday that mainly thanks the living keeps the date of a ceasefire that came one minute too late for Henry Gunther. For seven years Congress even moved it to October, then gave November 11 back. Strictly FYI.
