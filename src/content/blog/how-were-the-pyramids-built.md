Loaded with limestone from the quarry at Tura South, an inspector named Merer and his crew cast off for Akhet-Khufu and stopped for the night at a place called She-Khufu. That is day 26 of a month in [Merer's logbook, in the English translation of Pierre Tallet's edition](https://amers.hypotheses.org/wp-content/blogs.dir/2495/files/2017/03/1705_Tallet.pdf), and the next two days run on in the same flat style: sail on toward Akhet-Khufu with the stone, sleep there, cast off in the morning and head back upriver to Tura South.

Akhet-Khufu, the ["horizon of Khufu,"](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/ancient-egypt-shipping-mining-farming-economy-pyramids-180956619/) was the name of the Great Pyramid. A team from Paris-Sorbonne University found Merer's papyri in **2013** at Wadi al-Jarf, a harbor on the Red Sea coast, and Tallet calls them [the oldest papyrus archive found in Egypt](https://aeraweb.org/mark-lehner-pierre-tallet-at-mfa-boston/). The harbor goes back to [the days of the Pharaoh Khufu, 4,500 years ago](http://www.nbcnews.com/science/cosmic-log/4-500-year-old-harbor-structures-papyrus-texts-unearthed-egypt-flna1C9356840), and Tallet and Gregory Marouard described it in [Near Eastern Archaeology](https://doi.org/10.5615/neareastarch.77.1.0004).

Merer wrote late in the reign: his log dates to the year after Khufu's 13th cattle count, [regnal year 26-27](https://aeraweb.org/khufus-30-year-jubilee/) if the counts came every two years, and in 2016 a Japanese team published a later date, the 14th count, from one of the boat pits beside the Great Pyramid.

## How were the pyramids built?

The Giza pyramids were built by organized crews of Egyptian workers, not slaves. They cut most of the Great Pyramid's [estimated 2.3 million blocks](https://web.archive.org/web/20210627235809/http://www.egyptian-architecture.com/JAEA4/article27/JAEA4_Burgos_Laroze.pdf) from quarries beside it with copper tools, shipped casing limestone and granite in by boat, dragged stone on sledges and most likely raised it with [ramps helped by levers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construction_of_the_Egyptian_pyramids). The exact ramp system is still debated.

## Most of the stone was quarried next door

Most of the blocks came, by the Egyptologist Mark Lehner's calculation, from [a horseshoe-shaped quarry just south of the pyramid](https://aeraweb.org/gpmp-project/great-pyramid-quarry/), its sides aligned with the pyramid's own. Only the casing limestone and the chamber granite traveled far.

| Stone | Where it came from | What it was for |
|---|---|---|
| Local limestone | The quarry just south of the pyramid | The core, most of the bulk |
| White limestone | The Tura quarries, by boat across the Nile | The smooth outer casing |
| Granite | Aswan, by boat from [more than 900 km (560 mi) upriver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Pyramid_of_Giza) | The King's Chamber, in blocks of up to **80 tonnes** |

At Wadi al-Jarf itself, in 2017, the stonemason Franck Burgos and the architect Emmanuel Laroze found the quarry that supplied the blocks sealing the boat galleries, with a block abandoned mid-extraction and the quarrymen's kit around it: [copper chisel points, wooden mallets, stone hammers and rope](https://web.archive.org/web/20210627235809/http://www.egyptian-architecture.com/JAEA4/article27/JAEA4_Burgos_Laroze.pdf). With copies of those tools they cut a block of about one cubic meter, the average size Lehner gives for the pyramid's blocks, and wetting the stone made the work [almost six times faster](https://web.archive.org/web/20210627235809/http://www.egyptian-architecture.com/JAEA4/article27/JAEA4_Burgos_Laroze.pdf). By their reckoning four people could cut the trenches around such a block in four six-hour days, and [3,521 quarrymen could have produced the 250 blocks a day needed to finish the pyramid in 27 years](https://web.archive.org/web/20210627235809/http://www.egyptian-architecture.com/JAEA4/article27/JAEA4_Burgos_Laroze.pdf).

The copper the builders needed to cut the pyramid stones came mainly from [the mines in the Sinai just opposite Wadi al-Jarf](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/ancient-egypt-shipping-mining-farming-economy-pyramids-180956619/), the harbor where Merer's papyri were buried.

## The long hauls went by water

Merer's logs follow his crew [transporting limestone blocks from the quarries of Tura to the Great Pyramid of Khufu at Giza](https://aeraweb.org/mark-lehner-pierre-tallet-at-mfa-boston/). Tallet, who thinks the building site ran for 25 or so years, reads the cargo as fine limestone ["most probably but not exclusively used in the exterior cladding of the monument"](https://amers.hypotheses.org/wp-content/blogs.dir/2495/files/2017/03/1705_Tallet.pdf), a cladding that "has today almost completely disappeared."

The logs name the official at the Giza end, ["the noble Ankhhaf, director of Ro-She Khufu"](https://amers.hypotheses.org/wp-content/blogs.dir/2495/files/2017/03/1705_Tallet.pdf), Khufu's half-brother and vizier, whose Ro-She Khufu, "the entrance to the pool of Cheops," was a staging post for boats coming from Tura North.

Tallet reckons that a single team like Merer's could move [a minimum of 200 blocks each month](https://amers.hypotheses.org/wp-content/blogs.dir/2495/files/2017/03/1705_Tallet.pdf), some 30 blocks of about 2.5 tons on each trip.

The Nile now runs several miles from Giza, and [Smithsonian reported](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/ancient-egypt-shipping-mining-farming-economy-pyramids-180956619/) that the papyri, which Lehner said were "like Excel spreadsheets of the ancient world," "offer important support for a hypothesis" he had been developing for years: that the Egyptians built a major harbor or port near the pyramid complex. A 2024 study in *Communications Earth & Environment* traced an extinct branch of the river, which its authors named the Ahramat Branch, [bordering 31 pyramids](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43247-024-01379-7), and suggested it carried workmen and building materials.

Herodotus, who visited Egypt [around 450 B.C.](https://www.csmonitor.com/World/2010/0111/Egypt-says-Jewish-slaves-didn-t-build-pyramids), was told that some workers dragged stone from quarries in the "Arabian mountains," east of the river, to the Nile while others received "the stones after they had been carried over the river in boats," in [Macaulay's translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2707/pg2707.txt).

## On land the stone rode on sledges

A wall painting in the tomb of Djehutihotep, from [about 1900 B.C.](https://www.livescience.com/45285-how-egyptians-moved-pyramid-stones.html), centuries after Khufu, shows 172 men hauling a huge statue on a sledge while a man at the front pours water on the sand. "Egyptologists thought it was a purely ceremonial act," the physicist Daniel Bonn [told Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/45285-how-egyptians-moved-pyramid-stones.html). His team at the University of Amsterdam found that sand at the right dampness stops piling up in front of a sledge, and [the university said](https://iop.uva.nl/content/news/2014/00/prl-egyptian-pyramids.html) the trick let the Egyptians "halve the number of workers needed."

At Hatnub, an alabaster quarry in the Eastern Desert, a French and British mission found a ramp flanked by two staircases with post holes, and a sled tied by ropes to wooden posts could be hauled up "very steep slopes of 20 percent or more," its co-director Yannis Gourdon [told Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/63978-great-pyramid-ramp-discovered.html) in 2018. Tool marks and two inscriptions of Khufu date the system [at least to Khufu's reign](https://www.livescience.com/63978-great-pyramid-ramp-discovered.html).

## The ramp that raised the top is still missing

How the blocks reached the upper courses is the open question. Archaeologists generally agree that the builders used a ramp system, but [how exactly this system worked has been a long-standing mystery](https://www.livescience.com/63978-great-pyramid-ramp-discovered.html). Lehner speculates that the limestone, sand, desert clay and gypsum now filling the Khufu quarry [might be remnants of the pyramid construction ramps](https://aeraweb.org/gpmp-project/great-pyramid-quarry/), which the workers removed and dumped back in at the end of the project.

Herodotus described stones raised from step to step "with machines made of short pieces of timber," in [the same translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2707/pg2707.txt). The architect Jean-Pierre Houdin argues that the upper part went up on a ramp that spirals around the inside of the pyramid, a theory he still hoped to verify with scans in 2007, when the Egyptologist David Jeffreys called it ["far-fetched and horribly complicated"](https://web.archive.org/web/20081119161734/http://www.theengineer.co.uk/Articles/299582/Secrets+of+a+lost+world.htm) in *The Engineer*.

## The slave story is a myth, and Herodotus did not say slaves

Even the Associated Press, reporting on the builders' tombs in 2010, wrote that Herodotus ["once described the pyramid builders as slaves"](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2010/jan/11/great-pyramid-tombs-slaves-egypt). His account describes Egyptians drafted by a tyrant: Cheops "bade all the Egyptians work for him," and they worked "by a hundred thousand men at a time, for each three months continually," in [Macaulay's translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2707/pg2707.txt). Zahi Hawass, then Egypt's chief archaeologist, said the tombs showed [the workers were paid laborers, rather than the slaves of popular imagination](https://www.csmonitor.com/World/2010/0111/Egypt-says-Jewish-slaves-didn-t-build-pyramids).

The builders' graves were first found near Giza in **1990**, and the shafts uncovered in **2010** held a dozen skeletons with jars of beer and bread for the afterlife, [the AP reported](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2010/jan/11/great-pyramid-tombs-slaves-egypt). Dieter Wildung, a former director of Berlin's Egyptian Museum, called the slave myth ["only the stuff of tabloids and Hollywood."](https://www.csmonitor.com/World/2010/0111/Egypt-says-Jewish-slaves-didn-t-build-pyramids) Their lives were still hard; the skeletons show arthritis, and "their bones tell us the story of how hard they worked," the excavation supervisor Adel Okasha [said](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2010/jan/11/great-pyramid-tombs-slaves-egypt).

> "No way would they have been buried so honourably if they were slaves." - Zahi Hawass, Egypt's chief archaeologist, [in the AP report](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2010/jan/11/great-pyramid-tombs-slaves-egypt), 2010

The version with Israelite slaves fares no better. Amihai Mazar of the Hebrew University of Jerusalem said "No Jews built the pyramids because Jews didn't exist at the period when the pyramids were built," and blamed the story on a claim by Menachem Begin on a 1977 visit to Egypt, [the AP reported](https://www.csmonitor.com/World/2010/0111/Egypt-says-Jewish-slaves-didn-t-build-pyramids). The idea is far older: in the first century AD, Josephus wrote that the Egyptians set the Hebrews "to build pyramids," in [Whiston's translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2848/pg2848.txt).

The workers left their own names, too. Gangs painted theirs on blocks above the King's Chamber, among them ["The gang, The white crown of Khnum-Khufu is powerful"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Pyramid_of_Giza). The missing workforce once fed theories of space aliens and Atlantis, until Lehner began uncovering, a few hundred yards from the pyramids, [apartment blocks that might have housed as many as 20,000 people](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/ancient-egypt-shipping-mining-farming-economy-pyramids-180956619/) in 1999.

An inspector named Merer, writing on papyrus, logged day by day the boat trips that carried white limestone from the Tura quarries to the Great Pyramid, and his logbook has outlasted most of the pyramid's white casing. Strictly FYI.
