Loaded with limestone from the quarry at Tura South, an inspector named Merer and his crew cast off for Akhet-Khufu and stopped for the night at a place called She-Khufu. That is day 26 of a month in [Merer's logbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diary_of_Merer), and the next two days run on in the same flat style: sail on toward Akhet-Khufu with the stone, sleep there, cast off in the morning and head back upriver to the quarry.

Akhet-Khufu, the "Horizon of Khufu," was [the original name of the Great Pyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diary_of_Merer), and [the oldest known papyri with text](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diary_of_Merer), written over 4,500 years ago, are Merer's. A French team under Pierre Tallet and Gregory Marouard dug them up in **2013** at Wadi al-Jarf, a harbor on the Red Sea coast. Merer wrote in [the last known year of Khufu's reign](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/ancient-egypt-shipping-mining-farming-economy-pyramids-180956619/), and Smithsonian magazine reads his entries as a snapshot of the ancients [putting finishing touches on the Great Pyramid](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/ancient-egypt-shipping-mining-farming-economy-pyramids-180956619/).

## How were the pyramids built?

The Giza pyramids were built by organized crews of Egyptian workers, not slaves. They cut most of the Great Pyramid's [estimated 2.3 million blocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Pyramid_of_Giza) from quarries beside it with copper tools, shipped casing limestone and granite in by boat, dragged stone on sledges and most likely raised it with [ramps helped by levers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_pyramid_construction_techniques). The exact ramp system is still debated.

## Most of the stone was quarried next door

[Most of the blocks were quarried at Giza just south of the pyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Pyramid_of_Giza), in an area now called the Central Field, from a limestone packed with fossil shells. Only the casing limestone and the chamber granite traveled far.

| Stone | Where it came from | What it was for |
|---|---|---|
| Local limestone | The Central Field, [just south of the pyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Pyramid_of_Giza) | The core, most of the bulk |
| White limestone | The Tura quarries, [by boat across the Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Pyramid_of_Giza) | The smooth outer casing |
| Granite | Aswan, [more than 900 km (560 mi) upriver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Pyramid_of_Giza) | The King's Chamber, in blocks of up to **80 tonnes** |

In another quarry of Khufu's, found in 2017, an almost finished block lay beside the tools that cut it: [hardened arsenic copper chisels, wooden mallets, ropes and stone tools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Pyramid_of_Giza). The stonemason Franck Burgos used copies of them on a block of about **2.5 tonnes**, the average size in the Great Pyramid, and four workers took four days to free it. Wetting the stone made the work six times faster, and from his numbers [Burgos estimated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Pyramid_of_Giza) that about **3,500 quarrymen** could have produced the 250 blocks a day needed to finish the pyramid in 27 years, close to the [about 26 years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Pyramid_of_Giza) the build is thought to have taken.

Most of the copper came by sea. The Egyptians needed massive amounts of it to cut the pyramid stones, and the principal source was [the mines in the Sinai just opposite Wadi al-Jarf](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/ancient-egypt-shipping-mining-farming-economy-pyramids-180956619/), the harbor where Merer's papyri were buried.

## The long hauls went by water

Merer's log covers the casing, [in Tallet's reading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diary_of_Merer). Its best-preserved sections [document the transportation of white limestone blocks from the Tura quarries to Giza by boat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diary_of_Merer), and they name the official at the Giza end: Ankhhaf, [Khufu's half brother](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Pyramid_of_Giza), who appears in the papyri as [overseer of Ra-shi-Khufu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diary_of_Merer), the harbor at Giza. The Egyptologist Mark Lehner said the orderly writing of the papyri was "like Excel spreadsheets of the ancient world," [in Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/ancient-egypt-shipping-mining-farming-economy-pyramids-180956619/).

The Nile runs several miles from Giza today. Lehner had argued that the Egyptians built a major harbor or port near the pyramid complex, and [Smithsonian reported](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/ancient-egypt-shipping-mining-farming-economy-pyramids-180956619/) that the papyri "offer important support for a hypothesis" he had been developing for years. A 2024 study in *Communications Earth & Environment* traced an extinct branch of the river, which its authors named the Ahramat Branch, [bordering 31 pyramids](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43247-024-01379-7), and suggested it carried workmen and building materials.

Herodotus, who wrote [more than 2000 years after the structure was built](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Pyramid_of_Giza), was told that some workers dragged stone from quarries in the "Arabian mountains," east of the river, to the Nile while others received "the stones after they had been carried over the river in boats," in [Macaulay's translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2707/pg2707.txt).

## On land the stone rode on sledges

A wall painting in the tomb of Djehutihotep, from [about 1900 B.C.](https://www.livescience.com/45285-how-egyptians-moved-pyramid-stones.html), centuries after Khufu, shows 172 men hauling a huge statue on a sledge while a man at the front pours water on the sand. "Egyptologists thought it was a purely ceremonial act," the physicist Daniel Bonn [told Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/45285-how-egyptians-moved-pyramid-stones.html). His team at the University of Amsterdam found that sand at the right dampness stops piling up in front of a sledge, and [the university said](https://iop.uva.nl/content/news/2014/00/prl-egyptian-pyramids.html) the trick let the Egyptians "halve the number of workers needed."

Slopes were managed with rope. At Hatnub, an alabaster quarry in the Eastern Desert, a French and British mission found a ramp flanked by two staircases with post holes, and a sled tied to wooden posts could be hauled up "very steep slopes of 20 percent or more," its co-director Yannis Gourdon [told Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/63978-great-pyramid-ramp-discovered.html) in 2018. Tool marks and two inscriptions of Khufu date the system [at least to Khufu's reign](https://www.livescience.com/63978-great-pyramid-ramp-discovered.html).

## The ramp that raised the top is still missing

How the blocks reached the upper courses is the open question. Most Egyptologists consider ramps the most tenable method, but the archaeological record shows [only small ramps and inclined causeways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_pyramid_construction_techniques), and a single large straight ramp is routinely discredited for its size, its labor cost and the lack of any trace of it.

Herodotus described stones raised from step to step "with machines made of short pieces of timber," in [the same translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2707/pg2707.txt). The architect Jean-Pierre Houdin argues for a ramp built inside the pyramid, a thesis that [remains unproven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_pyramid_construction_techniques), and the Egyptologist David Jeffreys called it "far-fetched and horribly complicated."

## The slave story is a myth, and Herodotus did not say slaves

Even the Associated Press, reporting on the builders' tombs in 2010, wrote that Herodotus ["once described the pyramid builders as slaves"](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2010/jan/11/great-pyramid-tombs-slaves-egypt). His account describes Egyptians drafted by a tyrant: Cheops "bade all the Egyptians work for him," and they worked "by a hundred thousand men at a time, for each three months continually," in [Macaulay's translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2707/pg2707.txt). Archaeologists now believe the Great Pyramid was built by skilled workers who [worked for a salary or as a form of tax payment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_pyramid_construction_techniques).

The builders' graves were first found near Giza in **1990**, and the shafts uncovered in **2010** held a dozen skeletons with jars of beer and bread for the afterlife, [the AP reported](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2010/jan/11/great-pyramid-tombs-slaves-egypt). Dieter Wildung, a former director of Berlin's Egyptian Museum, called the slave myth "only the stuff of tabloids and Hollywood," in [the same report](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2010/jan/11/great-pyramid-tombs-slaves-egypt). Their lives were still hard; the skeletons show arthritis, and "their bones tell us the story of how hard they worked," the excavation supervisor Adel Okasha [said](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2010/jan/11/great-pyramid-tombs-slaves-egypt).

> "No way would they have been buried so honourably if they were slaves." - Zahi Hawass, Egypt's chief archaeologist, [in the AP report](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2010/jan/11/great-pyramid-tombs-slaves-egypt), 2010

The version with Israelite slaves fares no better. Amihai Mazar of the Hebrew University of Jerusalem said "No Jews built the pyramids because Jews didn't exist at the period when the pyramids were built," and blamed the story on a claim by Menachem Begin on a 1977 visit to Egypt, [the AP reported](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2010/jan/11/great-pyramid-tombs-slaves-egypt). The idea is far older: in the first century AD, Josephus wrote that the Egyptians set the Hebrews "to build pyramids," in [Whiston's translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2848/pg2848.txt).

The workers left their own names, too. Gangs painted theirs on blocks above the King's Chamber, among them ["The gang, The white crown of Khnum-Khufu is powerful"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Pyramid_of_Giza), and worker graffiti suggest haulers were divided into [groups of 40 men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Pyramid_of_Giza). A few hundred yards from the pyramids, Lehner's excavations found [apartment blocks that might have housed as many as 20,000 people](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/ancient-egypt-shipping-mining-farming-economy-pyramids-180956619/), many of whom appear to have eaten a great deal of beef.

Theories of space aliens and Atlantis grew out of the gap these finds now fill: [Smithsonian notes](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/ancient-egypt-shipping-mining-farming-economy-pyramids-180956619/) that the paucity of evidence for the people needed gave rise to many bizarre alternative theories. The unfinished pyramid on the dollar bill gets [blamed on the Illuminati](/blog/illuminati-on-the-dollar-bill/) too.

Egypt had been [united under one king](/blog/what-is-the-oldest-country-in-the-world/) for about five centuries when Khufu's crews went to work, and its officials wrote things down. An inspector named Merer, writing on [papyrus](/blog/how-is-paper-made/), logged day by day the boat trips that carried white limestone from the Tura quarries to the Great Pyramid, and his logbook has outlasted most of the pyramid's white casing.
