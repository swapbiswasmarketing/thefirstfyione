# Carousels Hold Viewers, Reels Recruit Strangers

On Instagram in 2025-2026, carousels earn the most from each person they reach and Reels reach the most people: Buffer's 2025 medians put engagement per account reached at **6.9% for carousels, 4.4% for single images and 3.3% for Reels**, while Reels reached **29% more people than carousels** in Metricool's 24-million-post sample from early 2026. The reach finding matters more for a brand-new account, because Feed and Stories rank only accounts people already follow, so every early view comes from Explore, the Reels feed and suggested posts, whose published system cards reward posts that viewers do not abandon in the first seconds, that hold attention longer than comparable posts, that get sent on and that earn follows, plus, for suggested Feed posts, an LLM-scored measure of how informative a post is. Adam Mosseri calls sends per reach "slightly more important" than likes for reaching non-followers, and since **30 April 2026** Instagram has kept accounts that mainly repost other people's photos and carousels out of recommendations, so originality now decides eligibility before any engagement is counted. Unlike X, Instagram documents no trust gate for new accounts and offers no evidence that commenting under big accounts builds a following. Strictly FYI's 30 checked carousels already fit what Instagram calls original and informative, so the plan keeps them as a daily post at **22:30 IST (noon in New York)** with four small repairs, adds eight narrated Reels of 30 to 60 seconds, each paired with its own entry's carousel to test which format reaches more strangers for this account, schedules everything at no cost, and asks the owner for about ten minutes a day plus a 45-minute Sunday sitting. No benchmark covers accounts under 1,000 followers and first-month anecdotes run from 12 followers to 15,000, so 100 followers by day 30 is a fair target, and 200, the level at which Instagram's Help Center opens Trial Reels to professional accounts, is the stretch.

## A new account lives on surfaces built for strangers

Instagram has no single algorithm. Meta publishes a separate system card for each surface ([Meta](https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/)), and the cards divide cleanly by audience. **Feed ranks only posts from accounts the viewer follows.** Suggested posts come from a separate system ([Feed card](https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/ig-feed/)), and Stories draw only on followed accounts ([Stories card](https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/ig-stories/)). An account with no followers is invisible to both. Its first views come from Explore, the Reels feed, suggested Feed posts, Search and Suggested Accounts, plus whatever its viewers send on to friends. Instagram explains how the Reels route opens. Since 2024, every eligible reel "is shown to a small audience that we think will enjoy it, regardless of whether they follow the account that posted it or not", and the best performers move on to wider audiences in stages ([Instagram](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/recommendations-and-originality)). In September 2026 Mosseri named four ranking principles: originality, recency, larger ranking models and "breaking content". The last means testing small creators' posts on people who do not follow them ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-chief-shares-algorithm-insights-and-posting-tips/831326/)).

The cards list what each surface predicts, though not how much each prediction weighs:

| Surface | Audience | Predictions that matter for a new account |
|---|---|---|
| Feed (card updated 29 Jun 2026) | Followers only | Swiping through the whole carousel; time on the post; sending it by DM; skipping it; time on the author's profile afterwards |
| Feed Recommendations, the suggested posts (29 Jun 2026) | Non-followers | "How informative a post is", from a signal "purely generated by LLM"; clicking the author's profile; clicking to comment; tapping "Interested"; skipping |
| Explore (22 Jun 2026) | Mostly non-followers | Following the author; viewing for more than five seconds; watching more than 95% of a video; liking, commenting, resharing and saving; tapping "Not interested" |
| Reels Chaining (11 Nov 2025) | Followers and non-followers | Watching under three seconds; outlasting 95% of viewers of reels the same length; resharing; sharing off Instagram; following; commenting; tapping "Interested" |

Sources: [Feed](https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/ig-feed/), [Feed Recommendations](https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/ig-feed-recommendations/), [Explore](https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/ig-explore/), [Reels Chaining](https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/ig-reels-chaining/).

Mosseri's own shorthand narrows the list. "The top three signals that matter most for ranking are watch time, likes and sends," he said in January 2025, adding that "sends are slightly more important for unconnected content", by which he meant reach to non-followers. He told creators to watch "average watch time, likes per reach, and sends per reach" ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/)). In 2024 he went further: "sends per reach correlate more, in my experience, with overall reach than anything else", and "the rate is more important than the count" ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-chief-post-share-rates-key-driver-reach/716540/)). Two inputs in the 2026 cards suit a fact publisher unusually well. A language model scores suggested Feed posts on how informative they are. The Feed card's share signal also asks whether a post is informative and less than 90 days old. Meta publishes no weight for either and warns that its signals "change frequently" ([Explore card](https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/ig-explore/)). Recency carries weight in general. Mosseri said it "matters a lot" ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-chief-shares-algorithm-insights-and-posting-tips/831326/)), and in Metricool's sample **76% of a regular post's views and 65% of a reel's arrived within three days** ([Metricool](https://newsletter.metricool.com/p/instagram-tell-me-your-goal-and-i-ll-tell-you-what-can-help)).

Several things that decide reach on X do not apply here. Instagram documents **no trust gate for new accounts** like X's graduated access ([Jane Manchun Wong](https://x.com/wongmjane/status/1601871343099805696)). Its gates are follower counts for particular features. Trial Reels are shown to non-followers first and stay off the profile grid unless shared to everyone, which can happen automatically when they do well. Instagram's Help Center opens them to professional accounts with at least 200 followers ([Help Center](https://help.instagram.com/1013292530224018)), though Mosseri gave 1,000 in July 2025 ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-expands-access-trial-reels/752895/)). Live needs 1,000 followers ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/apps/instagram-public-accounts-with-less-than-1000-followers-can-no-longer-go-live-133049758.html)). X tells organic posters to drop hashtags ([X Business](https://business.x.com/en/basics/organic-best-practices)). Instagram began capping them at five per post in December 2025 and still recommends "fewer (up to 5) more targeted hashtags, rather than many generic ones", even though Mosseri says they "don't work" to increase reach ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-implements-new-limits-on-hashtag-use/808309/)). Metricool found that posts with any hashtag drew **31.7% fewer views** than the platform average, a comparison with no controls ([Metricool](https://metricool.com/press-release-instagram-study-2026/)). Instagram Search matches a query against an account's "username or profile name" and against hashtags ([Search card](https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/ig-search/)). Instagram's August 2026 guidance says captions "tell search engines what your content is about" ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-stresses-the-importance-of-captions/828554/)), and public posts from professional accounts run by adults can appear in Google and Bing results ([Help Center](https://help.instagram.com/147542625391305)).

Two routes a reader might expect to help do not. No Meta source says links cut reach. The question hardly arises: Meta now sells "links in organic posts and reels" as a feature of its $49.99-a-month Meta One Advanced plan ([Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-meta-one-subscription-service-more-features-ai/)), which implies that free posts lack them. The free place for links is the bio, which holds up to five ([Help Center](https://help.instagram.com/362497417173378/?cms_platform=iphone-app)). Stories cannot carry a new account either. In April 2026 Mosseri said that "Feed generally gets more reach than Stories anyway", and that resharing your own feed post to Stories is "not going to meaningfully change your reach overall" ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-chief-debunks-popular-engagement-hack/816781/)).

## Carousels win per viewer, Reels win on unique reach

Every large 2025-2026 dataset splits the same way. The apparent conflicts come from what each one counts.

| Dataset | What it measures | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Buffer, 2025 medians ([source](https://buffer.com/resources/data-best-content-format-social-media/)) | Engagement per account reached | Carousels **6.9%**, single images 4.4%, Reels 3.3% |
| Buffer, 4M+ posts, Jan 2022 to Oct 2024 ([source](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-reach-engagement-analysis/)) | Unique accounts reached | Reels **1.36x** carousels and 2.25x single images |
| Metricool, 24.4M posts, Jan-Feb 2026 ([source](https://newsletter.metricool.com/p/instagram-tell-me-your-goal-and-i-ll-tell-you-what-can-help)) | People reached | Reels **29% above** carousels and 212% above single images |
| Metricool, same sample | Views per post | Carousels **27,000+**, 20% above Reels |
| Buffer, all account sizes, Sept 2026 ([source](https://buffer.com/insights/instagram-benchmarks)) | Median reach; engagement rate | Reels 555 and 3.9%; carousels 483 and 5.8%; single images 129 and 3.8% |
| Socialinsider, brands with 1K-1M followers, Q3 2026 ([source](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/instagram)) | Engagement per follower | Carousels **0.50%**, Reels 0.48%, single images 0.35% |

Metricool's own sample resolves the puzzle over views and reach. In the same data, carousels drew 20% more views than Reels while Reels reached 29% more people. A view counts every display, and Socialinsider calls views "the equivalent of the old impressions metric" ([Socialinsider](https://socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-length/)), so carousels plausibly collect repeat displays from the same people, including the re-serves described below. That reading is an inference. Reels lead for reaching strangers, and carousels lead for holding the people already reached. Single images trail on both counts and are losing ground: Metricool measured their reach **down 21.96%** and their engagement down 45.98% year on year. Carousels drew **nine times the saves** of single images ([Metricool](https://metricool.com/press-release-instagram-study-2026/)). For accounts with 1,000 to 5,000 followers, though, every format averages about one save per post ([Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/instagram)), so saves start to count only once an audience exists. One dataset dissents on reach. Socialinsider's estimated reach rate for brands with 1,000 to 1 million followers, January 2025 to August 2026, puts carousels slightly ahead, at 4.5% of followers against 4.1% for Reels ([Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/social-media-reach/)). Those are established accounts whose reach plausibly comes mostly from followers, so the figure says less about strangers.

Carousels have mechanics of their own. Feed predicts whether a viewer will swipe through the whole carousel. In October 2024 Mosseri said: "If someone sees your carousel post but they don't swipe, we'll often give that carousel a second chance", and the second showing starts from the next slide. He added that carousels "often get more reach than photos", and that music makes a post eligible for "video experiences like the Reels tab" ([CCStartup, quoting Mosseri's Reel](https://ccstartup.com/blog/2024/10/21/why-instagram-carousels-sometimes-start-on-the-second-slide/)). No 2026 Meta page restates the music point. Meta's API documentation caps carousels at 20 items ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/content-publishing/)). Since January 2025, profile grids have shown tall rectangles instead of squares ([The Verge via Pipedot](https://pipedot.org/6TN8M)); a vendor gives the ratio as 3:4 ([Planoly](https://www.planoly.com/blog/the-ups-and-downs-of-instagrams-new-rectangle-grid.md)). Only one study tests slide counts. It covered 22 million posts in 2020, when the limit was 10 slides:

| Comparison | Result |
|---|---|
| Slide count | 10-slide carousels did best; engagement dipped after three slides and recovered from eight |
| Media mix | Mixed photo-and-video carousels scored highest, at 2.33% |
| "Swipe" prompt | 2.0% with a prompt, 1.83% without |

Source: [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/instagram-carousels/379311/). No later study exists, and nothing public measures swipe-through or compares text-heavy carousels with photo carousels.

Reels have the cleanest 2026 length data. Socialinsider studied 9.8 million organic Reels from 295,475 profiles, January to June 2026 ([Socialinsider](https://socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-length/)):

| Reel length | Engagement per follower | Median views |
|---|---|---|
| Under 30 s | 0.28% | 4,700 |
| 30-45 s | 0.30% | 8,564 |
| 45-60 s | **0.35%** | **10,374** |
| 60-90 s | 0.30% | 9,790 |
| 90-180 s | 0.30-0.33% | 8,000-9,000 |
| Over 3 min | 0.15% | 4,428 |

The plateau from 30 seconds to three minutes is broad. Only very short and very long Reels clearly lose, and Instagram says "Reels over 3 minutes won't be recommended to new audiences" ([Instagram](https://creators.instagram.com/how-to-grow)). Attention is short whatever the length: the average Reel watch time is **8.5 seconds**, more than double the year before ([Metricool](https://metricool.com/press-release-instagram-study-2026/)). Instagram's September 2026 Reels guide asks for Reels that are "engaging from the first second". It recommends text overlays to "create an opening hook", subtitles for accessibility, and 15 to 60 seconds for "tutorials, day-in-the-life clips, and most creative formats". It also notes that still photos can play as a slideshow in the Reels feed ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-reels-creation-tips-in-new-guide/831551/)). That slideshow route is a trap for a text-led brand. Instagram makes "reels that are majority text" less visible ([Instagram](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained)), and its creator guidance asks for vertical video with little on-screen text ([Instagram](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/instagram-recommendations-eligibility-tips-creators)). Narration, and overlaid text that adds information, count as original ([Instagram](https://creators.instagram.com/original-content-guidelines)). No study measures faceless, text-only or AI-voiced Reels.

Captions matter mostly for search and for the first two lines. Instagram says the strongest captions "tell a personal story or offer useful information that viewers will want to save or share". Only the first two lines show before "more", so Instagram suggests leading with a surprising fact. It also suggests "Invite them to save for later" and "Direct them to a link in your bio" ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-stresses-the-importance-of-captions/828554/)). Evidence on caption length conflicts. Socialinsider's 2023 data on 9.1 million posts favoured captions under 30 words ([Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-caption-length/)), while a small 2026 vendor sample of 4,408 posts favoured longer ones ([PostPlanify](https://postplanify.com/blog/instagram-caption-length)). The two timing studies agree that midweek beats the weekend, and they overlap around weekday middays. Buffer studied 9.6 million posts from 2024 and 2025. Its best slots were Thursday at 9 a.m. and Wednesday at noon and 6 p.m., and its weakest were Friday, Saturday and 1-5 a.m. ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/when-is-the-best-time-to-post-on-instagram/)). Sprout measured nearly 2 billion engagements from November 2025 to February 2026. Its best windows were Tuesday 1-7 p.m., Wednesday noon to 9 p.m. and Thursday noon to 2 p.m., and weekends were weakest ([Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-times-to-post-on-instagram/)). Neither study says how much the best slot beats an average one. Frequency has better evidence than timing. Buffer compared each of 102,000 accounts with its own baseline, using a fixed-effects model. Weeks with 3-5 posts lifted weekly follower growth to +0.26%, against +0.12% for weeks with 1-2. Weeks with 6-9 posts reached +0.44%, and weeks with no post fell below the account's baseline ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-instagram/)). Instagram adds that the creators with the greatest net follower growth post "10 or more reels per month" on average ([Instagram](https://creators.instagram.com/reach-your-target-audience)).

Read against the five actions the owner cares about, the evidence gives each a different driver:

| Action | What the evidence ties it to | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| Reach to strangers | Reels and their staged non-follower tests; not being abandoned in the first three seconds | [Metricool](https://newsletter.metricool.com/p/instagram-tell-me-your-goal-and-i-ll-tell-you-what-can-help); [Reels card](https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/ig-reels-chaining/) |
| Likes | "Slightly more important" among followers than among strangers; predicted in Explore | [Mosseri](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/) |
| Comments | Posts with a question drew 36.7% more comments; replying to comments came with about 21% more engagement in the same account; carousels were "the most effective content type for generating conversations" in 2023 | [Metricool](https://metricool.com/press-release-instagram-study-2026/); [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/state-of-social-media-engagement-2026/); [Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-caption-length/) |
| Sends and shares | Mosseri's main signal for reaching strangers; Reels shares grew 67% in a year, the fastest of any format | [Mosseri](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-chief-post-share-rates-key-driver-reach/716540/); [Metricool](https://metricool.com/press-release-instagram-study-2026/) |
| Saves | Carousels, with nine times the saves of single images; predicted in Explore; meaningful only once there is an audience | [Metricool](https://metricool.com/press-release-instagram-study-2026/); [Explore card](https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/ig-explore/) |
| Follows | Predicted in Explore and Reels; in the one detailed new-account case, one Reel brought 11,634 follows from 614,966 views | [Explore card](https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/ig-explore/); [Stan](https://stan.store/blog/creator-strategies-case-study/) |

## Originality decides eligibility before engagement counts

For faceless accounts, Instagram's most consequential change of 2024-2026 was a filter, not a new signal. In April 2024 it began recommending the original whenever it found identical reels, and stopped recommending accounts that repeatedly post other people's reels without material change. Its stated reason was that "creators with large followings and aggregators that share copies of original content have gotten more reach in recommendations than smaller, original content creators" ([Instagram](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/recommendations-and-originality)). On **30 April 2026** the same rules reached photos and carousels. Accounts that mainly re-upload others' work in those formats are no longer eligible for suggestions ([Instagram](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/rewarding-original-creators-on-instagram/); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-updates-algorithm-to-benefit-original-creators/819016/)). Instagram says that **in the US, 75% of recommendations now come from original posts**. Original work includes "Content you designed — whether it's a photo series, a how-to guide, or a visual story". It excludes "borders, watermarks, speed changes, just crediting the original creator". An account becomes eligible again "when a significant percentage of your recently posted reels, photos, and carousels are considered original in a 30-day period" ([Instagram](https://creators.instagram.com/original-content-guidelines)). Other people's material qualifies only when it is heavily edited, for example with original text that adds context or graphics that add information. On X, crediting the creator of a reposted clip is the norm. On Instagram, credit alone no longer protects reach.

The Recommendations Guidelines also hold back content that "depicts or trivialises themes around death or depression" and content "that may depict violence". They likewise hold back long captions unrelated to the post and "coordinated comment networks intended to artificially drive engagement and distribution" ([Help Center](https://help.instagram.com/313829416281232)). The 2024 announcement adds "clickbait or engagement bait" ([Instagram](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/recommendations-and-originality)). Whole accounts lose eligibility for recent violations, for repeatedly buying likes, for advertising bans, or for repeatedly posting realistic AI-generated people without the AI-generated profile label. Followers still see an ineligible account's posts, but "none of your content will be recommended", and Account Status shows professional accounts where they stand ([Help Center](https://help.instagram.com/653964212890722)). For a new account, losing recommendations means losing everyone.

The cost of an error depends on the country. In the US, Meta ended third-party fact-checking on 7 April 2025 and moved to Community Notes, which "won't impact who can see the content or how widely it can be shared" ([Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/03/testing-begins-community-notes-facebook-instagram-threads/)). Notes are also rare on Instagram. Of the posts AFP's US bureau debunked from April 2025, **3.0% on Instagram carried a note, against 20.5% on X** ([Indicator](https://indicator.media/p/meta-says-community-notes-is-bigger-than-fact-checking-that-s-not-the-whole-story)). On X, an attached note cuts a post's reposts by 46% ([Slaughter et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12478135/)). Outside the US, Meta says "There are no changes to other countries at this time" ([Meta](https://transparency.meta.com/features/community-notes/)). There, content that fact-checkers rate false is not recommended, and accounts that repeatedly post it lose eligibility ([Help Center](https://help.instagram.com/313829416281232)). No page read for this report says whether India is a fact-checked country, or which regime governs an Indian account's post when Americans see it.

AI adds a further layer of labels. Meta marks AI-generated media "AI info", and since September 2024 it has put the label for merely AI-edited content in the post's menu ([Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/04/metas-approach-to-labeling-ai-generated-content-and-manipulated-media/)). In October 2026 Mosseri said "We do not throttle the reach of AI content just for being AI content", though Instagram wants such content labelled ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-chief-says-stronger-ai-slop-prevention-is-needed/833416/)). The niche's documented failures are AI fakes presented as history. In June 2025, for example, an image watermarked "Meta AI" circulated on Instagram, Facebook and X as a "rare historical photograph" of the 1908 Hyderabad flood ([DFRAC](https://dfrac.org/?p=63447)).

For Strictly FYI the implication is direct. Its carousels are its own typography carrying its own checked text, which meets Instagram's definition of original almost word for word. The risks lie elsewhere: in any archival photo or video clip it adds later, and in entries about death or violence.

## The niche rewards original explainers over reposters

Growth sits with brands that post their own material, and with face-led creators. Every account on FeedSpot's list of top Instagram history accounts, save possibly one, is a person, led by Alice Loxton at 1.9 million ([FeedSpot](https://influencers.feedspot.com/history_instagram_influencers/)). The faceless brands built on other people's footage are flat or shrinking. That fits Instagram's originality push, though follower counts cannot prove the link and the sample is small. X shows the opposite pattern: @historyinmemes grew 61-fold between 2022 and 2026 on uncredited clips ([2022](https://web.archive.org/web/20220730173114/https://socialblade.com/twitter/user/historyinmemes); [2026](https://web.archive.org/web/20260627221841/https://x.com/historyinmemes)). Education-type accounts also outperform media accounts on Instagram, which makes education the better yardstick for an explainer publisher. Rival IQ found Higher Ed engagement "more than quadrupled the median" even though those accounts posted less often than the median ([Rival IQ](https://www.rivaliq.com/blog/social-media-industry-benchmark-report/)). Hootsuite, which does not state its formula, put Education carousels at 5.4% against 3.2% for Entertainment and media ([Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/social-media-benchmarks/)).

The niche's best posts share a shape. NASA's "Swipe for countless stars", a three-slide carousel of James Webb Space Telescope images, had **836,055 likes three days after posting**, about 2.5 times NASA's average of 340,593 ([post capture](https://web.archive.org/web/20261005105805/https://www.instagram.com/p/DeAYvsnn4la/); [Social Blade](https://web.archive.org/web/20260928034003/https://socialblade.com/instagram/user/nasa)). It joined imagery nobody could see unaided to a short, playful first line, one to three sentences of explanation, a named source and a news peg. History Cool Kids' 12 sampled posts were all single images: text cards, maps or archival photos with text. Their captions had a median of 148 words and carried no hashtags. Its best early post was a LIFE magazine portrait with a full photo credit, which drew 1,345 likes within hours ([post capture](https://web.archive.org/web/20261008010932/https://www.instagram.com/p/DeNe5xMvwVX/)). Among the niche's explainer accounts, caption medians run from 44 words (NASA) to 297 (NASA Webb), with 0 to 4 hashtags; only Science Channel's seven-word clip titles are shorter. Engagement rate falls with size: National Geographic is at 0.05% and NASA at 0.33%, against a platform average of 0.48% ([Social Blade](https://web.archive.org/web/20261011142437/https://socialblade.com/instagram/user/natgeo); [Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/instagram)).

No reputable source documents a faceless educational account growing from zero in 2024-2026. The most detailed claim comes from Stan, a company that sells creator tools, about its own account @creatorstrategies. Stan says the account passed 15,000 followers in 30 days on 20 posts, mostly AI-voiced Reels that broke down other creators' viral videos using downloaded clips. One Reel brought 614,966 views and 11,634 follows. Across its life the account converted 20 follows per 1,000 views, and 65-70% of viewers stayed past three seconds ([Stan](https://stan.store/blog/creator-strategies-case-study/)). The figures are self-reported, and the borrowed-clip method sits on the edge of the 2026 originality rules. The counter-example is a personal-finance creator who posted daily for 30 days, five Reels and three carousels a week, and ended with 12 followers ([Niki Tisza](https://www.nikitisza.com/writing/i-spent-30-days-posting-on-instagram-daily-heres-what-actually-happened)). The benchmarks start at 1,000 followers. Brand accounts with 1,000 to 5,000 followers averaged 22% growth in 2025, down from 38% in 2024 ([Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/instagram)), and only 21% of accounts under 10,000 followers grew at all in early 2026 ([Metricool](https://metricool.com/press-release-instagram-study-2026/)). The honest expectation for a new faceless account is tens to a few hundred followers in its first month, and thousands only if a Reel breaks out.

The research on sharing explains why NASA's post travelled. The closest measured analogue to an Instagram send is emailing an article to someone, because both are private sharing; that comparison is an inference. In 6,956 New York Times articles, a one-standard-deviation rise in **awe or practical value raised the odds of making the most-emailed list by 30%**. The same rise in interest added 25% and in surprise 14%, while sadness cut the odds by 16% ([Berger & Milkman](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2011/11/Virality.pdf)). Information alone is not enough: in shared video ads, "Information-focused content has a significantly negative effect on sharing, except in risky contexts" ([Tellis et al.](https://doi.org/10.1177/0022242919841034)). When true and false stories reach the same size they spread the same way; the false ones are simply more often infectious ([Juul & Ugander](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8609637/)). People share what is interesting if true and what they judge accurate ([Altay et al.](https://doi.org/10.1080/21670811.2021.1941163)). In a 2026 experiment, the credibility cost of clickbait styling "was stronger for factually accurate posts" ([Craig et al.](https://researchportal.northumbria.ac.uk/en/publications/the-clickbait-paradox-why-sensationalist-styling-damages-trustwor/)). One finding separates the platforms. In brand posts, a human face and a close fit between image and text raised engagement "on Twitter but not on Instagram", while professional-quality pictures helped on both ([Li & Xie](https://doi.org/10.1177/0022243719881113)). A faceless, typographic brand therefore gives up less on Instagram than the face-led influencer lists suggest.

## A 30-day plan for @strictly.fyi

The evidence leaves the prepared kit mostly intact and changes what surrounds it. The carousels are the format Instagram's data favours for engagement and saves, and they meet its 2026 definition of original. What the kit lacks is a format built for reaching strangers, and strangers are a zero-follower account's whole audience. The owner's hand time, which the X plan spends on replies because hand replies are how X accounts grow, buys little on Instagram, where reach comes from the posts themselves. Every recommendation below carries one of four labels:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Strong** | Instagram's or Meta's own rules, system cards or developer documents, matched by independent data or repeated Meta statements |
| **Moderate** | One large dataset; or one Meta statement, often relayed by the press; or Meta's system cards alone, whose weights are unpublished |
| **Weak** | Vendor figures without a stated method, data from before 2024 or before a rule change, self-reported cases, or studies from other platforms |
| **Judgment** | No direct evidence; a reasoned call, set up as a test where possible |

### Add eight narrated Reels as a same-entry test

The case for putting Reels first is real. In Buffer's and Metricool's counts of unique people reached, Reels lead. Instagram describes its staged stranger test explicitly for reels, Reels account for "over 50% of all time spent on Instagram" ([Instagram](https://creators.instagram.com/how-to-grow)), and the fastest-growing creators average ten or more a month. The case against is also real. Reels earn the lowest engagement per person reached (3.3% against 6.9%). Their reach lead in 2026 data is 15% to 29%, not double, and Socialinsider's brand data shows none. No study covers faceless or text-led Reels, Instagram holds back reels that are mostly text, and Strictly FYI has no video pipeline but 30 checked carousels. Neither case settles the question, so the plan runs a test the account can read for itself. Eight Reels go out, each built from the same entry as that day's carousel and posted an hour after it. The topic is held constant, and the only difference is the format.

| Decision | What to do | Why | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Length and shape | 9:16 video, 30-60 seconds, aiming at about 45 | In 9.8 million Reels, 45-60 seconds led on views and engagement, and under 30 seconds drew about half the views. Over three minutes, Reels are not recommended to new audiences | Moderate |
| Script | The cover question, then slides 2 to 5, read word for word. Leave out the answer slide, which repeats | Only checked text may be posted. At roughly 110-160 words, the script runs about 45-60 seconds | Judgment for the cut; house rule for the text |
| Opening | Show the question as on-screen text over a moving picture from the first frame | The average Reel is watched for 8.5 seconds, and Instagram says to engage "from the first second" with a text-overlay hook | Moderate |
| Pictures | Under every line, a diagram, map, chart or public-domain image, either drawn in the card engine's style or taken from the entry's own cited sources. Keep subtitles small and use no full-screen text cards | Instagram holds back "majority text" reels, while narration and informative graphics count as original | Moderate |
| Voice | Local text-to-speech with Instagram's AI label, or the owner's own voice, unnamed. With a synthetic voice, post from the Instagram app or the account's own API app, which accepts `is_ai_generated`; Buffer's support for the label was not checked | Mosseri says AI content is not throttled "just for being AI content", but Instagram wants it labelled | Moderate |
| Sound | Narration only: no third-party clips and no other app's watermark | Instagram makes watermarked and reposted reels less visible | Strong |
| Never | AI-generated or colourised "historical" images; TV footage or viral clips, which rules out video for the exploding whale (22 Nov) and the cucumbers (28 Nov) | The originality rule, and the niche's documented AI fakes | Strong |
| Topics | 5 Nov QWERTY (archival drawings); 9 Nov noon and midnight (a clock); 10 Nov veins (a light diagram); 17 Nov the Moon (rotation); 18 Nov gold (a neutron-star image); 24 Nov the Mayflower (a map); 26 Nov tryptophan (a bar chart); 30 Nov web cookies (a browser-server diagram) | Each has a picture that carries the fact. The set spans awe (the Moon, gold) and practical value (noon, cookies), the two strongest brand-safe drivers in the sharing research | Judgment |
| Trial Reels | Once the account has 200 followers (or 1,000, if the app says so), post each experimental Reel as a Trial | Trials go to non-followers first and "don't impact the performance or ranking of your standard reels content" ([Help Center](https://help.instagram.com/1013292530224018)) | Moderate |

It costs $0 run locally; a hosted voice like the one Stan used costs $22 a month | Judgment |

### Ten minutes a day on comments, not outreach

The X plan's evening hour of hand replies under other accounts' posts has no Instagram equivalent. Meta ranks comments by "the relevance of the comment to the post and how popular the comment is among all viewers" ([Comments card](https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/ig-comments/)). It holds back content tied to coordinated comment networks, and no Meta statement or study links commenting on big accounts to follower growth. The owner's Instagram time therefore goes to the account's own comment section, where Instagram's advice is to "like or reply to comments on your reels within the first couple days" ([Instagram](https://creators.instagram.com/how-to-grow)) (evidence: moderate).

| When (IST) | New York | Task | Minutes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day 0, one evening | — | Create and set up the account, connect Buffer, post the first two carousels by hand, and load 3-8 November | 60 |
| Daily, 21:40-21:50, after the X block | 11:10-11:20 a.m. | Answer every new comment, using the entry or no new facts at all; read DMs; follow back real accounts that engage | 10 |

Instagram's tests distribute posts on their own merits, while X's growth runs through hand replies (evidence: moderate).

### Six numbers every Sunday decide December

Each Sunday, log only posts at least three days old, since most views arrive by then. Instagram Insights on a professional account shows reach, views, likes, comments, saves, shares and follows for each post, and watch time for Reels. The thresholds below are judgment calls, each anchored to a published figure.

| Number | Anchor | Day 14 | Day 30 | If it misses |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Median accounts reached per carousel | Buffer's September 2026 median for carousels across all account sizes: 483 | 100 | 250 | Under 40 at day 14: open Account Status, then compare the week's slide 2s with the best post's |
| Engagement per account reached, carousels (likes, comments, saves and shares ÷ reach) | Buffer's 2025 median for carousels: 6.9% | 4% | 6% | Under 3% for two weeks: rewrite the next week's slide 2s around each entry's most surprising checked fact |
| Sends per account reached (shares ÷ reach), every post | None published; Mosseri's main metric for reaching strangers | Rank all posts | Rank all posts | Take December's Reels from the five best topics |
| Followers, and follows per 1,000 accounts reached | Stan's outlier account: 20 per 1,000 views | 25 followers | 100; 200 opens Trial Reels | Under 50 at day 30: spend December's spare time on Collab invitations |
| Reels: share of viewers still watching at three seconds (the inverse of the skip rate), and average watch time | Stan's 75% target (65-70% achieved); 8.5 seconds on average | 65% | 70% | Under 60% on two Reels: rebuild the first second |
| Health: Account Status | "Eligible" for recommendations | Clean | Clean | Any flag: fix, delete or appeal that day, and pause that kind of post |

Three decision rules sit on top of the table (evidence: judgment). The **format test** is read on 22 November, after five pairs, and again on 3 December, when the eighth pair is three days old. If the Reel out-reaches its own carousel in at least six of the eight pairs, without fewer follows per 1,000 accounts reached, December moves to three or four Reels a week, posted as Trial Reels once the account has 200 followers. If the Reel wins three pairs or fewer, video stops and the effort goes back into carousels. Any result in between keeps two Reels a week for another month. The **question test** comes free: 18 of the 30 closing questions ask what people were told or taught, and 12 ask what they did, saw or thought, so comment rates per reach can be compared on 30 November. Finally, a credible report of an error is checked against the entry within 24 hours. If the entry is wrong, the caption gets a correction line and the bio's corrections link does the rest, as the house policy requires.

### The likeliest failures are rule breaks, not weak posts

| Risk | What the evidence says | Mitigation | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| The account loses recommendation eligibility | An ineligible account reaches only its followers, which for a new account is nobody. Unoriginal posts, bought likes and comment networks all cause it | Post only the brand's own designs and narration; credit any archival image and add context to it; open Account Status every Sunday and after each post on death or war | Strong |
| A post about death or violence is held back | The guidelines hold back content that "depicts or trivialises themes around death" or "may depict violence" | Keep 2, 11 and 22 November text-only, as built, and use no graphic images in any Reel | Moderate |
| A wrong fact | Outside the US, a false rating demotes the post, and repeated ratings cost the account its eligibility. In the US a note carries no penalty, but the post's engagers are notified. One false story costs a source more trust than a true one earns ([Altay et al.](https://doi.org/10.1177/1461444820969893)) | Take every word from the checked entry, confirm what every Reel image shows, and correct in public within 24 hours | Strong |
| An AI image presented as history | The niche's documented failures are AI "vintage" photos | Never post AI-generated or colourised archival images; label any synthetic voice | Strong |
| Engagement bait | Instagram lists engagement bait among the content it does not recommend | No requests for likes, follows, shares, saves or tags; one genuine question per caption | Moderate |
| An automation flag | The Terms forbid unauthorized automated access. Meta's spam rules cover actions done "either manually or automatically, at very high frequencies" ([Meta](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/spam/)), even though an official API now lets approved apps like posts | Post only through the app, Buffer or the account's own API app; do every like, follow and comment by hand | Strong |
| The owner's identity reaching the brand | Meta Verified for creators verifies a person by ID, and Facebook sharing goes to whatever profile or Page is linked | No Meta Verified and no personal Facebook link; check the profile name after sign-up | Strong |
| Reading noise as signal | No benchmark covers accounts under 1,000 followers, and at 1,000-5,000 followers every format averages about one save a post | Compare medians and same-entry pairs; act only on gaps of about 1.5 to 2 times | Moderate |
| Rules shifting mid-month | Meta says its signals "change frequently". The Trial Reels threshold and the API's daily cap are each stated two ways | Recheck the system cards and Help Center pages monthly; trust the account's own numbers over this report | Strong |
| The owner's time | The X plan already takes about eight hours a week | Thin the ten-minute daily check first; the scheduled carousels keep running without it | Judgment |

## Conclusion

Instagram inverts the X lesson. On X, the owner's hands were the growth engine and the posts gave them something to talk about. On Instagram, the posts are the engine: every eligible reel is shown to strangers whatever the follower count, and suggested posts are now scored by a language model on how informative they are. That makes Instagram the platform where Strictly FYI's prepared, checked material is worth most. There, production is the constraint, not presence, and production is the part an agent can do. By Mosseri's account, the strongest signal for reaching strangers is a private send, which the house rules rightly forbid asking for. The only lever left is choosing topics people want to pass on, and the research on private sharing points to awe and practical value.

The originality rule turns the kit's slowest feature, its hand-checked original writing, into an asset. Faceless pages that grew by re-uploading other people's material, the model that still wins on X, are now shut out of Instagram's recommendations. A publication that writes and draws its own slides from cited sources is exactly what the 2026 rule calls original. What no study can yet say is whether narrated explainers from a faceless publisher out-reach its carousels, because nobody has measured faceless Reels. That makes the eight same-entry pairs the month's most valuable output: by 3 December the account will hold the only evidence that applies to it. One opening is worth watching beyond that. AI-made "historical" photos circulate on Meta's platforms, yet only 3% of the Instagram posts that AFP debunked in the US carried a note. A sourced "real or fake?" carousel would fill a gap on Instagram that X's note writers already crowd.
